印度阶梯井为什么挖得那么深？

Why are Indian stepwells dug so deep?

干旱地区要靠深井取地下水，阶梯还能降温并兼作宗教与公共空间。
In arid lands they reach deep groundwater; the steps also cool the air and serve as sacred public space.

古代没有水泥，砖石是靠什么粘在一起的？

How did ancients bind stone without cement?

古代分三路。一是密砌干摆：石块磨配成缝，靠自重与摩擦承载，灰浆只填缝均摊应力，如金字塔严接石面。二是石灰砂浆：生石灰熟化成浆，上墙后吸收二氧化碳、变回碳酸钙而硬化。三是复合配方：熟石灰掺糯米汤汁成三合土，更韧、黏结更好，罗马混凝土以火山灰拌石灰，在海水中仍能硬化。要害是石灰浆怕水，碳化只能由表及里，厚墙中心久久不结实。
Ancient builders had three routes. The first was tight dry masonry: blocks were ground to fit one another, load was carried by self-weight and friction, and mortar only filled joints and spread stress, as in the closely joined facing stones of the pyramids. The second was lime mortar: quicklime was slaked into a paste, and on the wall it absorbed carbon dioxide and hardened by turning back into calcium carbonate. The third was composite mixes: sticky-rice broth stirred into slaked lime made a tougher mortar with a better grip on the interface, and Roman concrete, lime mixed with volcanic ash, could keep hardening under seawater. The catch is that lime mortar is water-sensitive and carbonation creeps only inward from exposed surfaces, so the core of a thick wall stays soft for a long time.

赵州桥为什么是单孔敞肩拱？

Why is the Zhaozhou Bridge a single open-spandrel arch?

大拱上开小拱（敞肩）减轻自重、利于泄洪又省料，是隋代李春的巧思。
Small arches on the big one (open spandrel) cut weight, aid flood release and save stone—clever 7th-century design.

罗马混凝土为什么泡在海里两千年还不坏？

Why does Roman concrete last 2,000 years in the sea?

火山灰加石灰反应生成特殊矿物，遇海水反而自我强化、裂了也能自愈。
Volcanic ash plus lime forms minerals that actually strengthen and self-heal when hit by seawater.

埃及金字塔的巨石是怎么垒到那么高的？

How were the pyramid's huge blocks lifted so high?

可能用土坡、杠杆和湿沙减阻等办法逐步抬升，具体方法至今仍有争议。
Ramps, levers and wet-sand friction tricks likely raised them step by step; details are still debated.

日本古木构（如法隆寺）为什么能抗震？

Why do Japanese wooden temples (like Horyu-ji) resist quakes?

柔性木架、榫卯和轻自重允许摆动耗能，体现了隔震的思想。
Flexible timber frames, joints and light weight let them sway and dissipate energy—an early seismic-isolation idea.

By day they burned smoke, by night lit fires; the number signaled the threat, relayed tower to tower.
