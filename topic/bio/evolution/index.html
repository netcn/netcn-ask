早期人类大约在何时开始制造石器和使用火？

When did early humans begin making stone tools and using fire?

目前已知最早的石器出自肯尼亚Lomekwi 3遗址（约330万年前，早于能人，属独立的Lomekwi工业）；经典的奥杜威（Oldowan）文化约在260万-170万年前出现于东非，通常与能人相关。更精致的阿舍利手斧约176万年前出现，常归因于直立人。用火的最早证据约在100万年前（南非Wonderwerk洞穴的烧焦骨和灰烬），而系统、普遍用火约在40万年前（欧洲多个遗址）。火提供了保暖、防护和烹饪，烹饪提高食物能量密度并缩短消化时间，被认为是驱动脑增大和社会化的关键因素。
The oldest known stone tools come from Lomekwi 3 in Kenya (about 3.3 million years old, predating Homo habilis, belonging to a distinct pre-Oldowan industry); the classic Oldowan industry appeared in East Africa about 2.6-1.7 million years ago and is usually linked to habilines. More refined Acheulean handaxes appeared around 1.76 million years ago, generally attributed to Homo erectus. The earliest evidence of fire use is about 1 million years old (burnt bones and ash at Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa), with systematic, habitual fire use around 400 thousand years ago (multiple European sites). Fire provided warmth, protection, and cooking; cooking raises the energy density of food and shortens digestion, and is considered a key driver of brain expansion and sociality.

古DNA研究揭示了现代人与尼安德特人、丹尼索瓦人之间的什么关系？

What do ancient DNA studies reveal about the relationship between modern humans, Neanderthals, and Denisovans?

2010年，Svante Pääbo团队发表尼安德特人基因组草图（获2022年诺贝尔生理学或医学奖），显示非洲以外的现代人基因组中含有约1-2%的尼安德特人DNA，证明走出非洲的现代人与尼安德特人发生过混血。丹尼索瓦人基因组（2010年从西伯利亚丹尼索瓦洞穴指骨中测出）显示其基因主要残留在东南亚和大洋洲人群（巴布亚新几内亚人群约含5%）。这些入侵的基因片段影响现代人的免疫系统（如HLA、TLR基因）和高原适应等表型。值得注意的是，尼安德特人的线粒体DNA和Y染色体几乎未在现代人中留下，提示混血主要发生在尼安德特男性与智人女性的方向（或此类谱系受选择淘汰）。
In 2010, Svante Pääbo's team published the Neanderthal genome draft (awarded the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine), showing that non-African modern humans carry about 1-2% Neanderthal DNA, proof that modern humans leaving Africa interbred with Neanderthals. The Denisovan genome, sequenced in 2010 from a finger bone at Denisova Cave in Siberia, survives mainly in Southeast Asian and Oceanian populations (up to about 5% in Papua New Guineans). These introgressed segments affect modern immunity (e.g., HLA and TLR genes) and phenotypes such as high-altitude adaptation. Notably, Neanderthal mtDNA and Y chromosomes are essentially absent in modern humans, suggesting interbreeding was mainly between Neanderthal males and sapiens females, or that such lineages were selected against.

生物学家怎么定义“物种”？加拉帕戈斯群岛的地雀为什么会演化成许多不同的种？

How do biologists define a "species," and why did Darwin's finches on the Galapagos Islands evolve into so many different species?

最常用的生物学物种概念认为，物种是能在自然条件下相互交配并产生可育后代的最大种群。地雀的分化典型地体现了异域物种形成：各岛屿之间被海水隔离，不同岛上的种群基因交流被切断，各自积累不同的突变，又因食物不同（种子、仙人掌、昆虫、吸血等）受到不同的选择压力。经过漫长隔离，它们的喙形、取食方式等差异越来越大，最终即使相遇也不能顺利杂交，形成生殖隔离，新的物种就此诞生。
The most common definition, the biological species concept, treats a species as the largest group whose members can interbreed and produce fertile offspring under natural conditions. The finches illustrate allopatric speciation: islands separated by ocean cut off gene flow between populations, so each island's birds accumulated different mutations and faced different selective pressures because food differed, from seeds and cactus to insects and even blood. Over long isolation, differences in beak shape and feeding grew until, even when they met, they could no longer interbreed smoothly, and reproductive isolation made new species.

什么是红皇后假说？

What is the Red Queen hypothesis?

红皇后假说认为，物种必须不断进化以应对竞争者、猎物和病原体的同步变化，才能维持相对地位。就像红皇后所说，必须不停奔跑才能留在原地。它常用于解释有性繁殖和军备竞赛式进化。
The Red Queen hypothesis says species must keep evolving just to maintain their relative standing against competitors, prey, and pathogens that are also changing. As the Red Queen put it, one must run constantly just to stay in place. It is often used to explain sexual reproduction and evolutionary arms races.

分子钟是怎样估算物种分化时间的？

How does a molecular clock estimate when species diverged?

分子钟利用 DNA 或蛋白质序列以近似恒定的速率积累中性突变，通过比对两个类群的遗传差异来推算它们分开的年代。再用已知年代的化石校准速率，就能为缺乏化石记录的类群构建时间树。
A molecular clock uses the roughly constant rate at which neutral mutations accumulate in DNA or proteins; by comparing genetic differences between two groups, it estimates when they split. Calibrating the rate with fossils of known age lets researchers build timed trees even for groups lacking fossils.

How did multicellular life arise?

Metasequoia, Cathaya and dove tree are “living fossil” relic plants—why did they survive in China?

这些古老类群在地球气候剧变中于多数地区灭绝，却因中国地形复杂、避难所多（如横断山、华中山地）而留存零星种群。水杉曾被认为已灭绝，1940年代才在湖北利川重新发现活植株。

Hox genes are master regulators expressed in order along the anterior-posterior axis, specifying segment identity; their highly conserved arrangement indicates animals share an ancient body-plan toolkit, where small changes yield major morphological evolution.

人类到底是从哪里来的？“人是猴子变的”这种说法科学吗？

Where did humans come from, and is it scientifically accurate to say “people evolved from monkeys”?

严格地说，人类不是由今天的猴子或猿演化来的，而是与现代黑猩猩在约600万至800万年前分道扬镳，共同拥有一个早已灭绝的祖先。人科的化石链条包括约400万年前能直立行走的南方古猿（著名的“露西”）、约200万年前的能人和直立人，以及晚近的智人；脑容量逐渐增大，工具和用火出现得越来越早。约30万年前出现的智人最终成为现代人类的直接祖先，这一过程由化石、分子钟和古 DNA 共同证实。
Strictly speaking, humans did not evolve from today's monkeys or apes; our lineage split from that of chimpanzees about six to eight million years ago, sharing a long-extinct common ancestor. The hominin fossil chain includes the bipedal Australopithecus of about four million years ago (the famous “Lucy”), Homo habilis and Homo erectus around two million years ago, and later Homo sapiens. Brain size grew and tools and fire appeared ever earlier; Homo sapiens, emerging about 300,000 years ago, became the direct ancestor of modern humans, a story confirmed by fossils, molecular clocks and ancient DNA.

"间断平衡"学说怎样解释化石记录中物种的"突然出现"？

How does punctuated equilibrium explain the "sudden appearances" of species in the fossil record?

间断平衡认为，物种在漫长的地质时间里形态相对稳定（停滞），随后在较短时期内发生快速成种与形态改变，而非以恒定的缓慢速度渐变。它强调成种事件本身而非个体变异的累积，是化石中常见"突然出现"现象的一种解释。
Punctuated equilibrium holds that species stay morphologically stable (stasis) for long spans, then undergo rapid speciation and change in relatively short bursts, rather than changing at a constant slow pace. It emphasizes speciation events over gradual individual change and helps explain the "sudden appearances" common in fossils.

基因漂变是什么，它与自然选择有何不同？

What is genetic drift, and how does it differ from natural selection?

基因漂变是指小种群中因随机偶然事件导致某些基因频率波动，与性状是否“有利”无关。它常见于瓶颈效应和奠基者效应，与自然选择“择优”的机制完全不同。
Genetic drift is the random fluctuation of gene frequencies in small populations due to chance events, unrelated to whether a trait is “beneficial.” Seen in bottlenecks and founder effects, it differs entirely from selection's “survival of the fittest” mechanism.

什么是间断平衡理论？

What is the theory of punctuated equilibrium?

间断平衡认为生物进化大多数时间处于稳定状态，物种长期少有变化。在相对短暂的地质时期内，环境剧变会触发快速的物种形成。它与渐进式进化的传统图景形成补充。
Punctuated equilibrium holds that species stay largely stable for most of their history with little change. Over geologically brief intervals, sudden environmental shifts can trigger rapid speciation. It complements rather than replaces the traditional picture of gradual change.

进化是物种随时间逐渐改变、并可能产生新物种的过程。它主要通过自然选择等机制，让更适应环境的特征在后代中变得更常见。地球上的所有生物都可以追溯到共同的祖先。
Evolution is the gradual change of species over time, which can give rise to new species. Mainly through mechanisms like natural selection, traits that help survival become more common in later generations. All living things on Earth can trace back to common ancestors.
