How are light and dark reactions of chloroplasts connected in space and energy?

一个受精卵为什么能发育成有肌肉、神经和皮肤的复杂身体？干细胞在其中扮演什么角色？

How can a single fertilized egg develop into a complex body with muscle, nerves and skin? What role do stem cells play?

受精卵是最全能的“干细胞”，它反复分裂并逐渐“决定”命运：在分裂和发育中，不同的细胞开启不同的基因、关闭另一些基因，通过转录因子和表观遗传修饰固定各自的表达模式，于是分化成神经、肌肉、皮肤等两百多种细胞。成体干细胞则负责日常更新，比如造血干细胞每天产生数以千亿计的血细胞。分化方向的“锁定”使成体细胞难以互换，这是组织再生和移植中的核心难题。
The fertilized egg is the ultimate totipotent stem cell. As it divides and develops, different cells switch on some genes and silence others; transcription factors and epigenetic modifications lock in each cell's expression pattern, driving differentiation into over two hundred cell types such as neurons, muscle and skin. Adult stem cells handle routine renewal, with bone-marrow stem cells, for instance, producing on the order of a hundred billion blood cells every day. Because differentiation locks cells into their fates, adult cells can hardly swap identities, which is the central challenge in tissue regeneration and transplantation.

细胞怎样用"泛素-蛋白酶体"系统清除坏掉或多余的蛋白质？

How does the ubiquitin–proteasome system tag and destroy damaged or surplus proteins?

细胞把泛素分子像标签一样共价连接到需要清除的蛋白质上，多聚泛素链被26S蛋白酶体识别并降解成小肽。这套系统清除错误折叠的蛋白、调控细胞周期蛋白，其失灵与帕金森病等多种神经退行性疾病有关。
Cells covalently attach ubiquitin molecules as tags to proteins marked for destruction; a poly-ubiquitin chain is recognized and degraded into peptides by the 26S proteasome. This system clears misfolded proteins and controls cell-cycle regulators, and its failure is linked to neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's.

How is the cell cycle regulated?

细胞周期由周期蛋白（cyclin）与周期蛋白依赖性激酶（CDK）组成的复合物推动，并受到检验点（如 G1/S、G2/M）监控。p53 等蛋白会在 DNA 损伤时叫停周期，直到修复或诱导凋亡，调控失常常导致癌症。
The cell cycle is driven by complexes of cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) and monitored by checkpoints such as G1/S and G2/M. Proteins like p53 halt the cycle upon DNA damage until repair or apoptosis is triggered, and loss of this control commonly leads to cancer.

The cytoskeleton is made of three protein-fiber systems, microtubules, actin microfilaments, and intermediate filaments, that give the cell support and drive movement, transport, and division. Microtubules also build cilia, flagella, and the spindle that segregates chromosomes.

哺乳动物受精时卵子是如何阻止多个精子进入的？

How does a mammalian egg prevent more than one sperm from entering (polyspermy)?

精子先在女性生殖道中获能（capacitation），然后与卵子透明带的ZP3蛋白结合，触发顶体反应释放蛋白水解酶，消化透明带，使精子穿入并接触卵细胞膜。精子融合引发皮质反应：卵子皮质颗粒释放酶类（如ZP2被水解、透明带硬化），使其余精子无法再结合或穿过透明带，这是主要的多精阻断机制；同时卵细胞膜电位变化提供快阻断。多精受精会导致胚胎染色体异常和发育失败。受精还使卵子恢复减数分裂II、释放第二极体，合子的原核融合后开始卵裂。
Sperm undergo capacitation in the female reproductive tract, then bind ZP3 of the zona pellucida, triggering the acrosome reaction: hydrolytic enzymes digest the zona, allowing the sperm to penetrate and contact the oocyte membrane. Sperm fusion triggers the cortical reaction: enzymes released from cortical granules harden the zona (e.g., ZP2 cleavage) so other sperm can no longer bind or pass through; this is the main slow block to polyspermy, with a rapid block from a change in membrane potential. Polyspermy causes chromosomal abnormalities and failed development. Fertilization also resumes meiosis II and expels the second polar body; after pronuclear fusion, cleavage begins.

减数分裂和有丝分裂有什么关键区别？为什么产生精子和卵子必须靠减数分裂？

What are the key differences between meiosis and mitosis, and why must sperm and eggs be made by meiosis?

有丝分裂只分裂一次，把复制好的染色体平均分给两个与母细胞相同的二倍体子细胞，用于生长和修复。减数分裂则连续分裂两次：第一次分裂时同源染色体配对并发生交叉互换，把等位基因重新洗牌，然后成对分开；第二次分裂时姐妹染色单体分开，最终产生四个染色体数目减半的单倍体细胞。只有单倍体的精子和卵子结合才能恢复二倍体，既使染色体数目代代恒定，交叉互换和染色体的随机分配又同时创造了遗传多样性。
Mitosis is one division that gives two diploid daughter cells identical to the parent, serving growth and repair. Meiosis divides twice: in the first division, homologous chromosomes pair, swap segments by crossing over (reshuffling alleles), and then separate; in the second, sister chromatids separate, yielding four haploid cells with half the chromosome number. Only when a haploid sperm fuses with a haploid egg is the diploid number restored, keeping the chromosome count constant across generations, while crossing over and the random assortment of chromosomes generate genetic variety.

同源异形基因（Hox基因）是如何控制动物身体前后轴形态建成的？

How do Hox genes control patterning along the anterior-posterior axis of animals?

Hox基因编码一类含同源框（homeobox）的转录因子，在几乎所有后生动物中成簇排列，染色体上的基因顺序对应其在身体前后轴的表达区域（共线性）：靠近5'端的基因在身体后部表达，3'端的基因在前部表达。它们像区域身份卡，决定每个体节发育出何种结构。突变会使结构出现在错误位置，如果蝇Antennapedia突变让头部该长触角的位置长出腿、Bithorax突变使第三胸节长出第二对翅。Hox基因从果蝇到哺乳动物高度保守，其复制和变异推动了动物体型的演化。
Hox genes encode homeobox-containing transcription factors that are clustered in nearly all metazoans, with their order on the chromosome mirroring their expression domains along the body axis (collinearity): genes at the 5' end are expressed posteriorly, those at the 3' end anteriorly. They act as regional identity cards, specifying what each segment develops into. Mutations place structures in the wrong positions, e.g., the Drosophila Antennapedia mutant grows legs on the head where antennae should be, and Bithorax produces a second pair of wings on the third thoracic segment. Hox genes are highly conserved from flies to mammals, and their duplications and modifications have driven the evolution of animal body plans.

完全变态昆虫的幼虫-蛹-成虫转变是如何由激素调控的？

How is the larva-to-pupa-to-adult transition of holometabolous insects controlled by hormones?

昆虫脑中的神经分泌细胞分泌促前胸腺激素（PTTH），刺激前胸腺释放蜕皮激素（ecdysone），启动每一次蜕皮。同时，咽侧体（corpora allata）分泌保幼激素（JH）。蜕皮的结果取决于JH浓度：幼虫期JH高，蜕皮后仍是幼虫；末龄幼虫JH下降，蜕皮进入蛹期；JH消失时，蛹蜕皮羽化为成虫。保幼激素类似物（如甲氧普烯methoprene）或蜕皮激素类杀虫剂（如甲氧虫酰肼，为蜕皮激素受体激动剂）可破坏这一调控，被用作昆虫生长调节型杀虫剂，对变态过程敏感的害虫高效而对哺乳动物毒性低。
Neurosecretory cells in the insect brain release prothoracicotropic hormone (PTTH), which stimulates the prothoracic gland to secrete ecdysone and trigger each molt. Meanwhile, the corpora allata produce juvenile hormone (JH). The outcome of each molt depends on JH concentration: with high JH the larva molts into another larval instar; when JH drops in the final instar, molting produces a pupa; when JH is essentially absent, the pupa molts into the adult. Juvenile hormone analogs (e.g., methoprene) or ecdysone-agonist insecticides (e.g., methoxyfenozide, an ecdysone receptor agonist) disrupt this regulation and are used as insect growth regulators, effective against metamorphosing pests with low mammalian toxicity.

什么是细胞凋亡，它和细胞坏死有何不同？

What is apoptosis, and how does it differ from necrosis?

细胞凋亡是由基因调控的、有序的「程序性死亡」，细胞会收缩、碎裂成被邻近细胞清理的小体，不引发炎症。坏死则是外伤、中毒等导致的被动、无序死亡，常伴随内容物外泄和炎症反应。凋亡对发育和组织更新不可或缺。
Apoptosis is a genetically programmed, orderly form of cell death in which the cell shrinks and breaks into fragments cleared by neighbors without causing inflammation. Necrosis is passive, disordered death from injury or toxins, often leaking contents and triggering inflammation. Apoptosis is essential for development and tissue renewal.

When a cell divides, how is the long DNA molecule split “evenly” between the two daughter cells?

秘密在于“先复制、再平分”。分裂前 DNA 先完整复制一遍，每份染色体的两条姐妹染色单体由着丝粒连在一起。分裂时纺锤丝把姐妹染色单体拉开，分别移向细胞两极，细胞才一分为二。这样每个子细胞都拿到一套与母细胞一模一样的完整基因组。
The trick is “copy first, then split.” Before division, the DNA is fully replicated so each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids joined at the centromere. During division, spindle fibers pull the sister chromatids apart toward opposite poles, and only then does the cell pinch in two. Each daughter cell ends up with a complete genome identical to the parent's.

DNA 是怎么“复制”出一份一模一样的副本的？

How does DNA make an exact copy of itself?

DNA 是双螺旋，复制是“半保留”的：解旋酶先把两条链像拉链一样解开，DNA 聚合酶再沿着每条旧链逐个配上互补的碱基，合成两条新链。结果每个新 DNA 分子都含一条旧链和一条新链，碱基配对规则（A对T、C对G）保证了序列准确无误。
DNA is a double helix, and replication is “semiconservative”: helicase unzips the two strands, then DNA polymerase walks along each old strand adding complementary bases to build two new strands. Each new DNA molecule therefore holds one old and one new strand, and the base-pairing rules (A with T, C with G) keep the sequence accurate.

为什么癌细胞会“停不下来”地分裂？

Why do cancer cells keep dividing without stopping?

正常细胞受三道闸门约束：细胞周期检查点、接触抑制和程序性死亡（凋亡）。癌细胞的这些控制机制因基因突变而失灵——促进分裂的原癌基因过度活跃，抑制分裂的抑癌基因（如 p53）失活，于是细胞无视损伤信号持续分裂，还能骗过免疫系统并长出新血管供养自己，甚至扩散到其他器官形成转移。
Normal cells are kept in check by three gateways: cell-cycle checkpoints, contact inhibition, and programmed cell death (apoptosis). In cancer cells, mutations disable these controls: proto-oncogenes that promote division become overactive, while tumor-suppressor genes such as p53 are knocked out. The cell then divides relentlessly, ignores damage signals, dodges the immune system, grows its own blood supply, and can spread to other organs as metastasis.

植物细胞有细胞壁、叶绿体和中央大液泡，而动物细胞没有细胞壁和叶绿体，但有中心体。
Plant cells have a cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large central vacuole; animal cells lack these but have centrioles.

What does the Golgi apparatus do in the protein secretion pathway?

高尔基体接收内质网送来的蛋白质，进行糖基化修饰、分类和包装，再以囊泡将成品送往细胞膜分泌或送往溶酶体等目的地。
The Golgi receives proteins from the ER, modifies them (e.g. glycosylation), sorts and packages them into vesicles that deliver finished products to the membrane for secretion or to destinations like lysosomes.

有丝分裂的作用是什么？

What is the purpose of mitosis?

有丝分裂把一套染色体精确复制并平均分给两个子细胞，使子细胞与母细胞拥有相同的遗传信息。它是多细胞生物生长、组织修复和单细胞生物无性繁殖的基础，保证了遗传的稳定性。
Mitosis duplicates one set of chromosomes and divides them equally between two daughter cells, giving each the same genetic information as the parent. It underlies growth, tissue repair in multicellular organisms, and asexual reproduction in single-celled ones, preserving genetic stability.

受精卵是如何通过卵裂发育成囊胚的？

How does a fertilized egg develop into a blastocyst through cleavage?

受精后，合子经多次有丝分裂（卵裂），细胞数量增加而总体积几乎不增，细胞越分越小（卵裂球）。约16细胞期称为桑葚胚，继续分裂后细胞围成中空的球形，即囊胚（哺乳动物称胚泡），中间为充满液体的囊胚腔。哺乳动物囊胚的外层滋养层将参与形成胎盘，内细胞团则发育为胚胎本身，这些细胞具有多能性。
After fertilization, the zygote undergoes repeated mitotic divisions (cleavage) in which cell number increases while total volume barely grows, so the cells, the blastomeres, become smaller. At about the 16-cell stage the embryo is a morula; further division produces a hollow ball, the blastula (called blastocyst in mammals), with a fluid-filled blastocoel. In the mammalian blastocyst the outer trophoblast will contribute to the placenta, while the inner cell mass develops into the embryo proper, and these cells are pluripotent.

细胞里的核糖体是干什么的？

What do ribosomes do inside a cell?

核糖体是细胞里的小工厂，按照mRNA上的指令把氨基酸一个一个连起来，组装成蛋白质。它们有的漂在细胞质里，有的附着在内质网上，是所有细胞（包括细菌）都有的必备结构。
Ribosomes are tiny factories inside cells that read the instructions on mRNA and link amino acids one by one to build proteins. Some float freely in the cytoplasm while others sit on the endoplasmic reticulum, and they are found in every cell, including bacteria.

为什么说细胞是生命活动的基本单位？

Why is the cell called the basic unit of life?

细胞是能独立进行新陈代谢、生长和繁殖的最小结构单位，所有已知生物都由细胞组成（病毒除外，它必须借助细胞才能复制）。无论是单细胞生物还是多细胞生物，生命活动都建立在细胞的基础之上。
The cell is the smallest structural unit that can independently carry out metabolism, growth, and reproduction, and all known living things are made of cells (viruses being the exception, since they need a cell to replicate). Whether an organism is single-celled or multicellular, life is built on cells.

植物的叶子为什么通常是绿色的？

Why are plant leaves usually green?

叶子里的叶绿体含有叶绿素，它能吸收阳光用来制造养分，而叶绿素偏偏把绿光反射出来，所以我们看到的叶子是绿色的。叶绿体就像植物的小太阳能工厂，把阳光、水和二氧化碳变成糖和氧气。
Leaf cells contain chloroplasts with chlorophyll, which absorbs sunlight to make food but reflects green light, so leaves look green to us. Chloroplasts act like tiny solar factories, turning sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into sugar and oxygen.
