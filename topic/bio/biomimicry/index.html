苍蝇长着复眼，科学家模仿它做出了什么样的相机，为什么能拍超广角？

Flies have compound eyes—what camera did scientists build from them, and why such ultra-wide angle?

复眼由许多小眼拼成半球，每个看一小块，合起来视野近360°。仿生“复眼相机”用大量微型透镜阵列，可一次拍极广角、几乎不变形，适合监控、全景和机器人视觉。
A compound eye packs many ommatidia into a hemisphere, each seeing a slice for near-360° view. Biomimetic “compound-eye cameras” use dense micro-lens arrays for ultra-wide, near-distortion-free shots—great for surveillance, panoramas and robot vision.

游泳运动员的“快皮”泳衣模仿了鲨鱼皮，鲨鱼皮到底靠什么减少阻力？

“Fastskin” swimsuits mimic shark skin—what exactly lets shark skin cut drag?

鲨鱼皮覆盖着细小的齿状鳞片（肤齿），能引导水流、抑制乱流（涡流）形成，从而减少摩擦阻力。泳衣用类似的沟槽纹理降低水阻，但国际泳联后来限制了这类装备。
Shark skin is covered with tiny tooth-like denticles that channel flow and suppress turbulent eddies, cutting friction. Swimsuits used similar grooves to reduce drag, later restricted by swimming's governing body.

中国高铁车头做成“子弹头”，听说灵感来自翠鸟，鸟嘴和火车有什么关系？

China's bullet trains have a pointed nose—said to be inspired by a kingfisher. What do a bird's beak and a train share?

翠鸟入水捕鱼时，细长尖喙能减少水花和阻力。工程师把车头设计成类似的长尖流线型，让高速列车钻进隧道时气压波更顺、噪声更小、更省电。
A kingfisher's long, slender beak cuts into water with minimal splash. Engineers shaped the train nose similarly, so at high speed the pressure wave entering tunnels is smoother—less noise, less drag, less power.

壁虎能贴在玻璃上走来走去不掉，它的脚有什么秘密，人类想用它发明什么？

Geckos walk on glass without falling—what's their foot secret, and what do humans want to invent from it?

壁虎脚趾有数百万根极细的刚毛，靠范德华力（分子间微弱吸力）牢牢吸附，却不留胶、可反复黏脱。科学家据此研制可重复使用的干黏胶、爬壁机器人。
Gecko toes bear millions of tiny hairs that stick via van der Waals forces—no glue, reversible. Scientists imitate this for reusable dry adhesives and climbing robots.

蜘蛛丝比同粗的钢丝还结实又轻，人类想用它来造什么？

Spider silk is stronger and lighter than steel wire of the same thickness—what do humans want to make with it?

蜘蛛丝强度高、韧性好、可降解。科学家尝试用基因工程让细菌或蚕生产类蜘蛛丝，用于轻型防弹衣、手术缝线、人工肌腱等，既强又对人体友好。
Spider silk is strong, tough and biodegradable. Researchers use genetic engineering to make spider-silk-like proteins in bacteria or silkworms for light bulletproof vests, surgical sutures and artificial tendons—strong yet body-friendly.

蝙蝠靠“回声定位”在黑夜里抓虫，这套本领怎样帮盲人“看见”世界？

Bats catch insects in the dark by echolocation—how does this help blind people “see”?

蝙蝠发出声波、听回声判断物体的位置和形状。工程师做出声波手杖或眼镜，向四周发射超声波并转成声音或震动提示障碍，让视障者像蝙蝠一样“听”出前方道路。
Bats emit sound and read echoes to map objects. Engineers built sonic canes or glasses that send ultrasound and turn echoes into sound or vibration, letting visually impaired users “hear” the path ahead like a bat.

莲叶“出淤泥而不染”，这种自清洁本领能不能用来做永远不脏的布料？

Lotus leaves stay clean in mud—can this self-cleaning trick make forever-clean fabric?

能。莲叶表面有纳米级的微小凸起和蜡质，水珠滚落时会带走灰尘（荷叶效应）。人们据此做出拒水拒油的纳米布料、自洁涂料和建筑外墙。
Yes. Lotus leaves have nanoscale bumps and wax so water beads roll off carrying dirt (lotus effect). This inspired water- and oil-repellent nano-fabrics, self-cleaning coatings and building facades.

蜜蜂的蜂巢为什么是六角形，这种形状对蜜蜂和人类工程师有什么好处？

Why are honeycombs hexagonal, and what's good about that shape for bees and engineers?

六角形能把平面铺得最满、不留空隙，又最省蜂蜡、最结实。蜜蜂用最少材料造出最大空间；人类也用蜂窝结构做飞机、自行车里又轻又硬的部分。
Hexagons tile a flat space with no gaps, using the least wax and staying strong. Bees get max storage from min material; humans use honeycomb structures in planes and bikes—light yet rigid.
