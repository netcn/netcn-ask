辛普森悖论为什么能让结论完全反转？

Why can Simpson's paradox flip a conclusion completely?

当数据分组时A优于B，但合并后B反而优于A，往往因为各组样本量悬殊。它提醒我们：只看汇总数字或只看分组，都可能被骗。
Data can show A better than B within groups yet B better overall once combined, often because group sizes differ sharply. It warns that both pooled and grouped views can mislead.

What is the unexpected hanging paradox?

法官说周末前某天绞刑且当天早上你不知情，囚犯推理出不可能发生，结果却在出乎意料的那天执行。它揭示了自指预言与知识逻辑的微妙矛盾。
A judge says the hanging will occur on a weekday and be a surprise; the prisoner deduces it cannot happen, yet it does. It exposes subtle conflicts in self-referential prediction and knowledge.

双信封悖论为什么让人左右为难？

Why does the two-envelope paradox leave you stuck?

一个信封的钱是另一个的两倍，你算期望后总想换，但换完用同样逻辑又想换回去，陷入循环。它暴露了期望值在信息不足、对称设定下的自相矛盾。
One envelope holds twice the other; computing expected value makes you want to switch, but the same logic then makes you want to switch back, an infinite loop. It exposes how expected value can contradict itself under symmetric incomplete information.

#巴拿赫塔斯基 #悖论

“秘书问题”（最优停止）为什么说先看放弃前约 37% 的候选再选，成功率最高？

In the 'secretary problem' (optimal stopping), why does rejecting the first ~37% of candidates then picking the next best maximize success?

在只能顺序面试、当场决定且不能回头的设定下，最优策略是跳过前 r−1 个（用于建立标准），之后一旦出现比前面都好的就录用。数学上最优分界约为 n/e ≈ 37%，此时选中“全局最佳”的概率约 1/e ≈ 37%。它把“何时停止”转化为漂亮的 1/e 常数，是效用与信息权衡的经典范例。
With sequential interviews, irrevocable choices, and no recall, the optimal rule skips the first r−1 to set a standard, then takes the first candidate better than all before. The optimal cutoff is about n/e ≈ 37%, giving a ~1/e ≈ 37% chance of the overall best—turning 'when to stop' into the elegant constant 1/e, a classic example of the utility-information trade-off.

为什么样本越大越准，却也可能更暴露极端个案？

Why do larger samples get more accurate yet expose more extremes?

样本越大，罕见事件越可能出现（如百万分之一的副作用在千万人中会出现几十例），所以大数据既更可靠又更容易显出极端个案，需要正确解读。
Larger samples make rare events more likely to appear (a one-in-a-million effect shows up dozens of times in tens of millions), so big data is both more reliable and more likely to surface extremes, needing careful interpretation.

Why is the chance two people share a birthday over 50% with just 23 people?

轮盘赌里“每次输了就加倍下注”的马丁格尔策略，为什么长期一定亏？

In roulette, why does the martingale strategy ('double after every loss') inevitably lose in the long run?

马丁格尔法靠“输后加倍”保证一回本就净赚一手本金，但每次都需要比上一次多一倍的资金；一旦遇到长连输，所需本金会超过桌限或你的全部家当，于是在有限财富下迟早被迫停手，此前累积的小赢一次清零。数学期望上它仍为零和偏负（抽水），只是把亏损推迟成大崩盘。
The martingale recovers one unit by doubling after each loss, but each step needs double the capital; a long losing streak exhausts the table limit or your wealth, forcing you to stop and wipe out prior small gains. Its expected value stays zero or negative (house edge), merely delaying loss into a rare crash.

拍卖里的“赢家诅咒”为什么让出价最高的人反而最可能吃亏？

Why does the 'winner's curse' in auctions mean the highest bidder is most likely to lose out?

在价值不确定的共同价值拍卖中，每个竞拍者只凭自己的估计出价；出价最高的人，往往正是把价值估得最高（最可能高估）的那位，中标后才发现真实价值低于自己付的价。因此“赢”了拍卖却常“亏”了钱，理性买家应故意压低出价。
In a common-value auction with uncertain worth, each bids on their own estimate; the winner is typically the one who overestimated most, discovering post-win that the true value is below the price paid. So winning the auction often means losing money, and rational bidders shade their bids down.

为什么0.999…看起来小于1，其实等于1？

Why does 0.999… equal 1 even though it looks smaller?

设x=0.999…，则10x=9.999…，两式相减得9x=9，所以x=1。或者说1/3=0.333…，乘3得到1=0.999…。无限小数没有最后的9，二者是同一实数的两种写法。
Let x=0.999…; then 10x=9.999…, subtract to get 9x=9, so x=1. Or 1/3=0.333… and times 3 gives 1=0.999…. There is no final 9, so they are two names for one real number.

Monty Hall problem: is switching doors really better?

是的。你初选正确的概率是1/3；主持人揭开一只空门后，剩下那扇门正确的概率变成2/3，所以换门把胜率从1/3提高到2/3。
Yes. Your first pick is right only 1/3 of the time; after the host reveals an empty door, the remaining door is right 2/3 of the time, so switching doubles your win rate from 1/3 to 2/3.

Why is true randomness actually hard?

人编出的数字序列常有规律（避免过长连号），算法随机数多为伪随机（决定性公式），真正随机需要物理噪声。这也是密码学关心的话题。
Human-made sequences show patterns (avoiding long runs) and algorithmic randoms are usually pseudorandom (deterministic formulas); true randomness needs physical noise. That is why cryptography cares.

Why can Hilbert's infinite hotel never be full?

抛两枚硬币，正正和正反哪个更可能？

Tossing two coins, which is more likely: two heads or one of each?

抛两枚有正正、正反、反正、反反四种等可能结果。一正一反包含正反和反正两种，所以概率1/2，比正正的1/4更可能；顺序会误导直觉。
Two coins give HH, HT, TH, TT, all equally likely. One head one tail covers HT and TH, so its chance is 1/2, more than HH's 1/4; order tricks our intuition.

为什么平均数会骗人？平均数和中位数差很多说明什么？

Why can the average lie, and what does a big gap with the median mean?

少数极大的值会拉高平均数（如贫富差距），使它远高于多数人的真实情况。看收入、房价时，中位数往往更能代表普通人。
A few huge values pull the average up (e.g. wealth gaps), making it far above most people's reality. For incomes or house prices the median usually represents the typical person better.

Why is almost winning the lottery not actually rare?

中奖组合只有一种，但差一个数字的组合有成千上万种，所以几乎每次开奖都有大量差一点的票；这并不说明你运气好，只是零事件附近的近邻很多。
There is only one winning combination, but millions of combinations are off by a single number, so nearly every draw produces many near-misses. That does not mean you were lucky; near-misses are just numerous.
