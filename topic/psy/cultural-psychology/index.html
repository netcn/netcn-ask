为什么很多人觉得“有信仰”能让人更平静、更坚韧？

Why do many people find faith brings calm and resilience?

心理学研究显示，宗教和灵性通常通过三条路径支持心理健康：提供意义感和解释框架、提供稳定的社会支持、以及提供仪式化的应对方式（祈祷、冥想、节庆）来舒缓焦虑。当然，这些都是群体统计趋势，个人差异很大；无信仰者同样可以靠意义感、亲密关系和应对技巧获得韧性与平静。关键在于“联结”本身。
Psychological research suggests religion and spirituality support mental health through three routes: a sense of meaning and explanatory framework, stable social support, and ritualized coping (prayer, meditation, festivals) that eases anxiety. These are group-level trends with large individual variation; nonreligious people gain the same resilience and calm through meaning, close relationships, and coping skills. Connection itself is what matters.

文化背景如何塑造人们的归因风格？

How does cultural background shape people's attributional style?

个体主义文化成员更倾向于将行为归因于个人特质，而集体主义文化成员更常考虑情境与关系因素。这种差异部分源于自我构念的不同，并在跨文化实验中被稳定重复。
Members of individualistic cultures more often attribute behavior to personal dispositions, whereas collectivist cultures weigh situational and relational factors. This difference partly stems from self-construal and replicates across cross-cultural experiments.

为什么同样是移民，有人融入顺利，有人一直冲突不断？

Why do some immigrants adjust smoothly while others stay in conflict?

文化适应研究区分四种策略：整合（既保留原文化认同，又参与新文化）、同化（放弃原文化）、分离（只守原文化）、边缘化（两边都不投入）。大量研究认为“整合”与最好的心理适应相关，而“边缘化”风险最高。不过最优策略也取决于社会是否包容——文化适应是双向的，不是单方面“变成别人”。
Acculturation research distinguishes four strategies: integration (keeping heritage identity while participating in the new culture), assimilation (giving it up), separation (clinging to it), and marginalization (engaging neither). A large body of research links integration with the best adjustment and marginalization with the worst — though the ideal also depends on how welcoming society is. Acculturation is two-way, not just “becoming someone else.”

亨里奇等人抨击心理学长期依赖所谓“WEIRD”被试,这对各类“人类普遍性”论断构成了怎样的方法论威胁?

Henrich and colleagues attacked psychology's long reliance on so-called WEIRD participants—what methodological threat does this pose to claims of human universality?

他们论证,心理学被试大多来自西方、受过教育、工业化、富裕且民主的社会,而这些人群在公平博弈、视错觉、空间推理等任务上恰恰是全球分布中的离群点,于是便利样本得出的规律可能只是局部规范被误认作物种特征。又因为期刊极少报告样本的文化背景,理论家难以察觉自己的“普遍”命题踩在极其狭窄的证据底座上。作者主张用人口学透明的报告方式和跨人群比较设计来取代单一样本的外推。
They argued that psychology draws most participants from Western, educated, industrialized, rich, and democratic societies—populations that are outliers in global distributions on fairness games, visual illusion, and spatial reasoning—so patterns from convenience samples may mistake local norms for species-wide traits. Because journals rarely report the cultural background of samples, theorists can overlook that their universal propositions stand on an extremely narrow evidence base. The authors urged demographically transparent reporting and cross-population comparison designs in place of extrapolation from a single group.

为什么有些事你“说不清理由，但就是觉得不对”？

Why do some things feel wrong even when we can't explain why?

道德心理学家海特提出，很多道德判断首先来自快速的情绪直觉，理性的理由常常是“事后的解释”。比如面对某些场景，人的反应先于论证。这并不意味着直觉不可靠，而是提醒我们：既然直觉先于理由，反思“我为什么会这么想”就格外重要，也能帮助我们理解为什么他人的道德反应与自己不同。
Moral psychologist Haidt proposes that many moral judgments begin as fast emotional intuitions, with rational reasons often arriving as after-the-fact explanations. Faced with certain situations, people react before they can argue. This doesn't make intuition unreliable — it just means reflecting on “why do I feel this way” matters, and it helps explain why others' moral reactions differ from ours.

个人主义与集体主义文化如何影响人们的动机取向？

How do individualistic and collectivistic cultures influence people's motivational orientation?

在个人主义文化中，成就动机更多指向个人卓越与自我提升；在集体主义文化中，动机常与维护群体和谐、履行角色责任相连。这解释了不同文化下奖励设计与团队管理的有效性差异。
In individualistic cultures achievement motivation targets personal excellence and self-improvement; in collectivist cultures it ties to group harmony and role duty. This explains why reward designs and team management differ in effectiveness across cultures.

为什么有人把“和大家处好”看得很重，有人更在乎“做自己”？

Why do some people prioritize harmony while others prioritize being themselves?

文化心理学区分“独立型自我”和“互依型自我”：前者强调独特性、内在品质和自我表达，在欧美文化中更常见；后者强调关系角色、归属与群体和谐，在东亚文化中更常见。其实大多数人都同时具备两种自我构念，只是侧重不同，并会随环境和场合切换。
Cultural psychology distinguishes an independent self-construal — uniqueness, inner traits, self-expression, more common in Western cultures — from an interdependent one — roles, belonging, group harmony, more common in East Asian cultures. Most people actually hold both, just weighted differently and switched by context.

为什么到了新环境，人会不知不觉按别人的方式做事？

Why do we unconsciously follow other people's behavior in a new environment?

这叫“描述性规范”：人们会参考“别人怎么做”来决定自己怎么做。新班级、新公司里，怎么说话、怎么排队、用什么词，会很快“传染”给新来者。规范让群体高效运转，但也可能放大不良行为——看到有人乱扔垃圾，别人更可能跟着扔。选择你希望被其影响的环境，是聪明的做法。
That's descriptive norms: people use “what others do” as a guide. In a new class or company, how people talk, queue, and phrase things quickly rubs off on newcomers. Norms make groups efficient, but they can also amplify bad behavior — seeing litter makes others litter more. Choosing environments you want to be influenced by is a smart move.

文化如何影响情绪的「表达规则」？

How does culture shape the display rules of emotion?

情绪表达规则规定在何种情境下应展现或压抑何种情绪。东亚文化更鼓励抑制负面外露以维持和谐，欧美文化更容忍甚至鼓励情绪的外显表达，这塑造了跨文化的情绪行为差异。
Display rules dictate which emotions may be shown or suppressed in given contexts. East Asian cultures encourage dampening negative expression for harmony, while many Western cultures tolerate or encourage open expression, shaping cross-cultural emotional behavior.

心理学研究中的「WEIRD」样本问题指什么？

What is the WEIRD sample problem in psychological research?

WEIRD 指西方、受教育、工业化、富裕、民主社会的大学生群体，他们仅占人类人口极少数却长期主导心理学样本。亨里奇等人指出，基于该群体的结论未必适用于其他文化，呼吁更具代表性的抽样。
WEIRD stands for Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic samples—often undergraduates—who are a tiny fraction of humanity yet dominate psychology. Henrich and colleagues argue conclusions from them may not generalize, urging representative sampling.

为什么同一句话，在一种文化里是礼貌，在另一种文化里可能是冒犯？

Why is the same sentence polite in one culture but offensive in another?

不同文化对直接性、眼神接触、沉默和拒绝方式的规则不同。东亚文化常用含蓄表达，直接拒绝可能显得粗鲁；许多西方文化则把直接沟通视为诚实。没有哪一套“更正确”，它们都是长期形成的沟通规范。跨文化交往中，保持好奇、多确认少猜测，比单纯套用自己熟悉的规则更有效。
Cultures differ in rules about directness, eye contact, silence, and refusal. East Asian cultures often favor indirect expression, where blunt refusal can seem rude; many Western cultures treat directness as honesty. Neither set is “more correct” — both are long-evolved communication norms. In cross-cultural encounters, curiosity and checking rather than assuming beats applying your own rules.

为什么初到国外的人，常会经历一段特别想家、情绪低落的时期？

Why do people new to a foreign country often feel homesick and low for a while?

这是“文化适应”的正常过程：面对陌生的语言、规则和习惯，人会经历从兴奋到困惑、失落再到逐渐适应的曲线，有时被称为“文化休克”。大多数人在几个月到一年内会找到新的平衡。给自己适应时间、保持与家乡的联系、同时开放地接触新环境，都能帮助平稳度过。
It's a normal part of acculturation: facing unfamiliar language, rules, and customs, people move through excitement, confusion, homesickness, and gradual adjustment — sometimes called culture shock. Most find a new balance within months to a year. Giving yourself time, staying connected to home, and openly engaging the new environment all help you ride it out.

Markus 与 Kitayama 的独立型—互依型自我建构理论怎样解释东西方在自我评价上的系统性差异?

How does Markus and Kitayama’s independent–interdependent self-construal account explain systematic East–West differences in self-evaluation?

该理论指出,北美文化把自我塑造为边界清晰、由内在特质界定的独立实体,自尊维护与自我拔高因此更常见;东亚文化将自我安放于关系网络之中,推崇谦逊与情境调适,自我批评式表达反而有助于维系和谐。后续研究提示,这些分歧集中体现在外显自评层面,而内隐积极偏见在各文化间相对普遍。
The theory argues that North American culture casts the self as a bounded entity defined by inner attributes, making self-esteem maintenance and self-enhancement routine, whereas East Asian cultures seat the self within relational networks that prize modesty and contextual adjustment, so self-critical expression can even serve harmony. Follow-up work indicates the divergence concentrates in explicit self-reports, while an implicit positivity bias appears fairly widespread across cultures.
