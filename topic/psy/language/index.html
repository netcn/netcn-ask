阅读时大脑里的“视觉词形区”是什么？

What is the 'visual word form area' in reading?

左枕颞叶的“视觉词形区”（VWFA）会对字母串产生特异性反应，支持快速词形识别。它由识字经验塑造，把原本用于物体的视觉回路“再利用”，显示文化如何重塑脑结构。
The visual word form area (VWFA) in left occipitotemporal cortex responds selectively to letter strings and supports fast visual recognition of words. It is shaped by literacy and recycles visual circuitry, showing how culture reshapes brain organization.

婴儿是怎样“统计式”学会分辨语音的？

How do infants 'statistically' learn to segment speech sounds?

婴儿能追踪音节同现的频率：经常出现在一起的音节更可能被听成一个词，从而在无分隔的自然语音中切分出词边界。这种统计学习在语言、视觉甚至音乐中普遍存在，是早期认知的通用机制。
Infants track transitional probabilities between syllables: pairs that co-occur often are heard as one word, letting them segment speech without boundaries. This statistical learning appears in language, vision and music—a general early-cognition mechanism.

什么是“心理词典”（mental lexicon）？

What is the mental lexicon?

心理词典是我们脑中储存词形、语音、语义与语法信息的网络，词与词通过意义与语音联结成网。提取一个词会部分激活其邻居（语义/语音启动），所以有时会说错音近或义近的词。
The mental lexicon is the networked store of word forms, sounds, meanings and grammar; words are linked by sense and sound. Retrieving a word partially activates neighbors (semantic/phonological priming), which is why we sometimes produce similar-sounding or similar-meaning words.

脑海里那个“内心的声音”是什么？

What is that 'inner voice' in your head?

内部语言（内心独白）是默念而不出声的语言活动，借助布罗卡等语言区运行。它用于计划、自我指导和调节情绪，并非所有人每时每刻都有，强度因人而异。
Inner speech is silent, unvoiced language activity engaging Broca's and related areas. It is used for planning, self-guidance and emotion regulation, and not everyone experiences it constantly—its vividness varies between people.

语言相对论有什么现代实验证据？比如空间隐喻。

What modern evidence supports linguistic relativity, e.g., spatial metaphors?

实验发现语言习惯会影响非语言任务：如把时间想成“前/后”还是“上/下”会影响记忆排列；说强时态语言的人更倾向储蓄。这表明语言范畴会渗透进抽象的推理与决策。
Experiments show linguistic habits leak into non-linguistic tasks: whether time is framed as front/back or up/down shifts how memories are arranged; speakers of strong-future-tensed languages save more. Language categories can seep into abstract reasoning and decisions.

说话时比划手势，只是装饰还是真的帮助思考？

When we gesture while speaking, is it decoration or does it help thinking?

手势并非单纯的附带动作：它能外化尚未成形的想法、减轻工作记忆负担，并帮助听者理解。研究表明让儿童边说边比划能促进数学与语言任务的推理。
Gesture is not mere decoration: it externalizes half-formed ideas, offloads working memory and helps listeners understand. Studies show encouraging children to gesture while explaining improves reasoning in math and language tasks.

孩子先说对了 went，后来却改口说 goed——什么是“过度规则化”？它揭示了怎样的习得过程？

A child who first said “went” correctly later switches to “goed”—what is overregularization, and what does it reveal about language acquisition?

过度规则化指儿童把不规则词形套进一般规则，例如英语里把 went 说成 goed、把 feet 说成 foots。耐人寻味的是，不少孩子先记住正确形式，之后才出现这类“退步”，呈U形轨迹：先逐个记忆，继而归纳出加 -ed 的规则并推广过头，最后才掌握例外。这些错误恰恰证明儿童在主动构建语法规则，而不是机械模仿大人的话。
Overregularization means fitting irregular forms into a general rule—saying goed for went and foots for feet in English. Intriguingly, many children first memorize the correct forms and only later commit such “errors,” a U-shaped course: item-by-item storage, then induction of the add -ed rule pushed too far, and finally mastery of exceptions. These very errors prove that children actively construct grammar instead of parroting adult speech.

婴儿只听没有任何停顿的连续语流，是如何学会切分出一个个单词的？

How can infants learn to segment individual words out of continuous speech that carries no pauses?

Saffran 等人让8个月大的婴儿聆听约两分钟无停顿的人造语言音节流，再用听觉偏好程序测试：婴儿听词内音节组合的时间明显短于跨越词边界的组合。原因在于音节间的过渡概率在词内较高、在词边界处骤降，婴儿对这一统计规律敏感，据此把连续语音切成可能的单词。这说明统计学习是语言习得早期即可利用的机制。
Saffran and colleagues had eight-month-old infants listen for about two minutes to an artificial language delivered without pauses, then tested them with a listening-preference procedure: infants attended less to syllable sequences that formed words than to sequences spanning word boundaries. Transitional probabilities between syllables are high within words and drop sharply at boundaries, and infants track this statistic to carve continuous speech into candidate words. The finding established statistical learning as a mechanism available early in language acquisition.

没人给孩子上语法课,他们怎么就学会了造句?语言习得是天生的吗?

Nobody teaches toddlers grammar — how do they start building sentences, and is language innate?

证据指向强大的先天装备。其一,全世界婴儿都在相近月龄经历咿呀学语、单词句、电报句、语法爆发,顺序惊人一致;其二,儿童听到的输入充满省略、口误与未纠正的错误,却总能输出一套规范得多的语法,乔姆斯基据此提出人脑内置普遍语法的语言习得装置假设;其三,关键期证据来自个案与第二语言研究:青春期前沉浸学习可达母语水平,之后学习几乎总带口音与语法残留。当代共识折中为先天的学习偏向加社会互动微调,而非现成的语法硬盘。
Evidence points to formidable innate equipment. Babies worldwide babble, produce one-word utterances, telegraphic speech then grammar bursts on similar timetables; children hear messy, ungrammatical input with sparse correction yet output far more regular grammar — Chomsky proposed a language acquisition device with universal grammar; and critical-period evidence shows immersion before adolescence reaches native levels while later learning almost always retains accent and residue. Current consensus blends innate learning biases with social-interaction fine-tuning rather than a ready-made grammar module.

双语家庭的孩子会语言混淆吗?大脑装两套系统是不是更费劲?

Do bilingual kids confuse languages — does running two systems burden the brain?

早期研究曾担忧双语儿童词汇量单项偏低、混语码是混乱征兆;方法学修正后结论反转:总概念词汇量不低于单语者,幼年混用(如一句中英夹杂)恰是双系统在线的标志,多数儿童在学龄前完成语码分离。神经机制上,双语者前额叶与前扣回负责语言切换的执行控制常年加班,带来抑制控制的轻微优势(即著名的双语优势),尽管近年大样本研究对其稳健性提出质疑。确定收益包括老年失语症恢复更佳与痴呆发病平均推迟约四五年——两套系统不是负担,更像终身锻炼。
Early work feared smaller vocabularies and code-mixing as confusion; methodological corrections reversed the verdict: total conceptual vocabulary matches monolinguals, childhood mixing within sentences actually signals both systems online, and most children separate codes before school age. Neural accounts put prefrontal and anterior cingulate control networks on lifelong language-switching duty, once credited as the bilingual advantage in inhibition — though large recent samples question its robustness. Solid findings include better post-stroke aphasia recovery and dementia onset delayed by roughly four to five years. Two systems are not a burden but a lifetime workout.

说两种语言的人，脑子里有两个人格吗？

Do bilingual people have two personalities in their heads?

不会有两个人格，但双语者在切换语言时常伴随轻微认知与情绪风格变化，因为不同语言关联不同记忆与社交语境。研究还显示双语可能延缓痴呆并提升某些执行功能，但效果并非绝对。
No split personalities, but bilinguals often show subtle shifts in cognition and emotional style when switching languages, since each language evokes different memories and social contexts. Bilingualism may delay dementia and boost some executive functions, though effects are not guaranteed.

语言会影响我们怎么思考吗？（萨丕尔-沃尔夫假说）

Does language shape how we think? (Sapir-Whorf hypothesis)

强版本（语言决定思维）已被否定，但弱版本有证据：语言提供的词汇和语法范畴会便利某些认知，比如有更细颜色词的人辨色更快。语言更像思维的“助推器”而非“牢笼”。
The strong version (language determines thought) is rejected, but a weak version has support: the words and grammar a language offers make some distinctions easier—e.g., speakers with finer color terms discriminate colors faster. Language nudges thought rather than imprisoning it.
