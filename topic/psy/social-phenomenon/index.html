为什么“私下作答”能让人不再随大流？

Why does answering privately stop people from following the crowd?

Asch 后续发现，当被试匿名写下答案，从众率大幅下降——公开场合顺从多为保面子、不被排斥，私下则按事实。这说明很多从众是表演性的社会顾虑，而非真看不清。
Asch's later work found conformity dropped sharply when answers were written anonymously; public compliance is mostly face-saving and avoidance of exclusion, while private answers follow facts. Much conformity is performative social caution, not failed perception.

为什么东亚文化下的“从众”率和西方不同？

Why does conformity rate differ between East Asian and Western cultures?

在强调和谐的互依型文化，规范性从众（为不丢脸、不合群而跟随）更高；Asch 范式的复现研究发现东亚被试更易随大流，但也更愿为集体协调让步。这反映文化价值，而非更没主见。
In harmony-valuing interdependent cultures, normative conformity (to avoid losing face or standing out) runs higher; Asch-paradigm replications find East Asians more likely to follow, yet also more willing to yield for group coordination. It reflects values, not less backbone.

内隐偏见能“训练掉”吗？哪些方法被研究支持？

Can implicit bias be trained away, and which methods are supported?

内隐态度较难短期消除，但接触积极反例、换位思考想象、重复配对新联结可小幅减弱。更稳的是改变环境线索与制度（如盲审），而非指望个人想通就好。
Implicit attitudes are hard to erase quickly, but contact with positive counterexamples, perspective-taking imagination and repeated new pairings can weaken them modestly. More robust is changing environmental cues and institutions like blind review, not just hoping individuals see reason.

代际创伤会“传”给下一代吗？机制是什么？

Can intergenerational trauma be passed to the next generation, and by what mechanism?

研究指童年逆境可通过教养方式、家庭情绪氛围与表观遗传（基因表达被环境修饰）跨代影响；并非记忆遗传，而是风险与环境的传递。觉察这一链条有助于截断循环。
Research shows childhood adversity transmits across generations via parenting, family emotional climate and epigenetics (environment modifying gene expression); not memory inheritance but risk and environment transfer. Seeing this chain helps break the cycle.

网络时代有“数字旁观者效应”吗？

Is there a digital bystander effect in the online age?

有。求助帖下人人以为别人已报警或已安慰，结果无人行动；匿名还降低责任感。研究提示，公开事件中指定具体人行动、或自己率先回应，能打破沉默。
Yes. Under a help post everyone assumes someone else already called or consoled, so none act; anonymity lowers responsibility. Research suggests naming a specific actor or responding first yourself breaks the silence.

什么是“隐性歧视”？它和公然歧视有何不同？

What is subtle discrimination, and how does it differ from overt bias?

隐性歧视来自无意识的内隐态度，当事人可能真诚否认偏见却仍在简历、距离、语气上差别对待；它比公开歧视更难察觉也更普遍，需用结构化测量（如内隐联想测验）才显形。
Subtle discrimination springs from unconscious implicit attitudes; people may sincerely deny bias yet treat others differently in resumes, distance and tone. It is harder to spot and more common than overt bias, surfacing only via structured measures like the IAT.

为什么谣言和阴谋论传得比真相快？

Why do rumors and conspiracy theories spread faster than the truth?

研究表明，虚假消息往往比真相传播得更快更广。谣言常带有新奇、惊悚或愤怒的情绪，更容易激起分享欲；同时它迎合人们的既有偏见（确认偏误），并让人产生“我知道内情”的优越感。真相通常平淡、复杂，反而没那么“好传”。
Research shows false news typically spreads faster and wider than the truth. Rumors often carry novelty and strong emotions like fear or outrage that trigger sharing, and they flatter our existing biases (confirmation bias) while giving a feeling of being 'in the know.' The truth is usually calmer and more complex, so it travels less easily.

从众一定是坏事吗？它有什么进化好处？

Is conformity always bad, and what evolutionary benefit does it have?

从众并不全是坏事，它在进化上很有用。跟随多数能降低风险、快速学到他人的经验，也让群体更容易协调与合作。适度的从众带来归属感和安全感；只有当它压制独立思考、让人盲从错误时，才变成问题。
Conformity is not always bad—it carries real evolutionary benefits. Following the majority helps us avoid risk, learn from others' experience, and coordinate within a group. Moderate conformity brings belonging and safety; it only becomes harmful when it suppresses independent thinking or leads people to follow mistakes blindly.

什么是“刻板印象威胁”？它怎么让人考得更差？

What is stereotype threat, and how does it lower test performance?

当人被提醒所属群体某方面不行（如女性数学、少数族裔智力），焦虑会占用工作记忆，反而拉低表现，形成自我实现的预言。仅是“被评估”的暗示就足以触发，与真实能力无关。
When reminded their group is supposedly weak at something (women at math, minorities at IQ), anxiety eats working memory and lowers performance, a self-fulfilling prophecy. Mere hints of being evaluated trigger it, unrelated to real ability.

为什么父母和子女常在同一件事上“代沟”巨大？

Why do parents and children often have a huge generation gap on the same issue?

代际差异来自成长时代的不同心理脚本：物质稀缺对丰裕、权威对平等、线下对线上。双方常用自己的经验地图解读对方，误把差异当不懂事。互相讲清背景比争对错更有效。
Generational gaps come from different mental scripts of the era they grew up in: scarcity vs abundance, authority vs equality, offline vs online. Each reads the other through their own map and mistakes difference for defiance. Explaining context beats arguing right or wrong.

为什么人会对“自己人”更宽容、对“外人”更严？

Why are people more lenient with insiders and stricter with outsiders?

这来自“内群体偏好”：人天生会把人分成“自己人”和“外人”。对内部成员宽容能增强群体凝聚力、维持合作关系；对外人更严则是一种防范风险和资源竞争的心理。这种倾向在进化中有助于保护自己的族群和联盟。
This comes from in-group favoritism: people naturally sort others into 'us' and 'them.' Being lenient with insiders strengthens group cohesion and cooperation, while judging outsiders more harshly is a defensive response to risk and competition over resources. Evolutionarily, this tendency helped protect one's own kin and alliance.

什么是“基本归因错误”？为什么我们总怪人、不怪环境？

What is the fundamental attribution error, and why do we blame the person not the situation?

我们解释他人行为时常夸大性格、忽视情境（如迟到等于懒，而不是堵车）。东方文化因更重情境，此偏差比西方弱。意识到我也可能在同样情境下这样做能减少苛责。
We explain others' acts by overstating traits and ignoring context (late equals lazy, not traffic). Because East cultures weigh context more, this bias is weaker there. Realizing you might do the same in that situation reduces harshness.

旁观者效应里，“责任分散”到底怎么发生？

In the bystander effect, how exactly does diffusion of responsibility happen?

在场人越多，每个人感到的别人会管的负责越稀释，且大家互看彼此反应来定义紧急程度（多元无知）。破解之道：明确指向某人求助，穿蓝衣的您快打120。
The more bystanders, the more each person's sense that someone else will help is diluted, and people read each other's calm to define the situation as non-urgent (pluralistic ignorance). The fix: single out one person, you in blue, call 120 now.
