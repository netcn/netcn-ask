为什么往往是少数坚持己见的人，最终改变了整个群体的想法？

Why is it often a stubborn minority that ends up changing the whole group's mind?

莫斯科维的“少数派影响”研究（Moscovici, 1976）发现，只要少数派保持一致、自信且不惜被孤立，就能动摇多数派的私下态度，促发真正的思考而非表面服从。与从众相反，少数派靠的是“差异性”撬动群体的认知重构。
Moscovici's minority influence work (1976) shows that a consistent, confident, isolation-accepting minority can shift the majority's private attitudes and spark real thought rather than mere public compliance. Opposite to conformity, the minority leverages distinctiveness to reconstruct the group's cognition.

从众分“信息性”和“规范性”两种——动机不同后果也不同？

Conformity splits into informational and normative—different motives, different outcomes?

从众分两类：信息性（别人可能更懂，跟着准）与规范性（想被接纳，跟着安全）。前者改善决策，后者导致盲目跟随；辨别动机才能既借群体智慧又保独立。
Conformity splits into informational (others may know better) and normative (want acceptance). The former improves decisions, the latter blinds; telling them apart lets you borrow crowd wisdom yet stay independent.

心智是普遍还是文化塑造？——什么是“文化模块”之争

Is the mind universal or culture-shaped—what is the cultural-modules debate?

一些学者主张存在文化特异的“心智模块”（如东亚更关系本位、西方更分析式认知），另一些强调人类共通底层加表面差异。折中看法：普遍机制之上，文化像“旋钮”调出不同默认。
Some argue for culture-specific modules (East Asian relational, Western analytic cognition); others stress universal bases with surface variation. The compromise: universal mechanisms on which culture acts like a dial setting different defaults.

从众会“升级”吗——什么是“渐次服从”与“升级承诺”？

Can conformity escalate—what are graduated compliance and escalation of commitment?

人在小步让步后更难拒绝后续更大要求（脚在门内/渐次服从），因公开承诺与自我一致感被锁定。组织里的“升级承诺”则是对失败项目继续加码，受沉没成本与面子驱动。
After small concessions people resist bigger later demands less (foot-in-the-door / graduated compliance), locked by public commitment and self-consistency. Escalation of commitment is pouring more into a failing project, driven by sunk cost and face-saving.

文化维度里“权力距离”指什么？它怎样影响管理与课堂？

In cultural dimensions, what is power distance—how does it shape management and class?

权力距离衡量社会接受不平等权力的程度。高权力距离文化更服从权威、少质疑老师/老板；低权力距离更平权、鼓励参与。理解它可避免跨文化误解，而非评判优劣。
Power distance measures how unequally power is accepted. High-PD cultures obey authority and rarely challenge teachers/bosses; low-PD are egalitarian and participatory. Understanding it prevents cross-cultural misunderstanding, not judging which is better.

给人贴“病人/失败者”标签会产生什么心理后果？——标签效应

What are the psychological consequences of labeling—the labeling effect?

标签效应指被贴上某类标签后，本人与他人按标签期待行事（自证预言），并损害身份与自尊。去标签化、先看能力再看诊断，是更尊重也更准确的方式。
Labeling effect: once tagged (patient, failure), the person and others act to match the label (self-fulfilling prophecy), harming identity and self-worth. De-labeling—seeing ability before diagnosis—is more respectful and accurate.

什么是“内群体偏爱”——我们为何天然偏向“自己人”？

What is in-group favoritism—why do we naturally favor our own?

仅随机分成两组就出现对内群体的偏好与对外群体的贬低（最小群体范式）。它源于身份归属需求与资源倾斜，提醒我们“我们 vs 他们”常是无中生有。
Mere random grouping yields in-group favoritism and out-group derogation (minimal group paradigm). It stems from belonging needs and resource tilt, reminding us that 'us vs them' is often conjured from nothing.

什么是“社会懈怠”——为什么大群体里有人偷懒？

What is social loafing—why do some slack in big groups?

社会懈怠指个人在群体中出力少于单独时，因贡献难被识别、责任分散。可逆：让个人产出可衡量、目标有意义、成员相互依存时懈怠下降。
Social loafing is exerting less in a group because effort is hard to spot and responsibility diffuses. It reverses when individual output is measurable, goals matter, and members depend on each other.

什么是“去个体化”——为何人群中会做出平时不做的事？

What is deindividuation—why do people do in crowds what they wouldn't alone?

去个体化指匿名与群体淹没感削弱自我觉察与规范约束，使人更易随群体冲动（如网络喷子、骚乱）。责任分散与自我意识降低是主因，恢复可识别身份可抑制。
Deindividuation: anonymity and being submerged in a crowd weaken self-awareness and norms, making people follow group impulses (trolls, riots). Diffusion of responsibility and lowered self-awareness drive it; restoring identifiability curbs it.

什么是“群体极化”——群体讨论为何让观点更极端？

What is group polarization—why does discussion make views more extreme?

群体极化指成员原已偏向某立场，讨论后倾向更极端。机制包括信息重复强化、想显得比同伴更忠诚、以及责任分散。它解释网络回音室与陪审团极端化。
Group polarization: members already leaning one way end up more extreme after discussion, via repeated reinforcing info, wanting to out-devote peers, and diffusion of responsibility. It explains online echo chambers and jury extremism.

刻板印象是怎么“形成”的——不止是偏见那么简单？

How do stereotypes form—more than just prejudice?

刻板印象常来自对群体特征的过度概括与确认偏误：少数为例被当成全体，例外被忽略。它只是认知捷径，不等于恶意，却会借锚定与可用性影响判断。
Stereotypes form from over-generalizing group traits and confirmation bias: a few examples stand for all, exceptions ignored. It is a cognitive shortcut, not necessarily malice, yet it skews judgment via anchoring and availability.

什么是文化的“紧—松”（tightness-looseness）维度？

What is the cultural tight-loose dimension?

紧文化有严格规范与低容忍偏差（如强调守序），松文化规范灵活、容许多样。它受历史威胁（瘟疫、灾害）塑造，影响从排队到创新的多方面行为。
Tight cultures hold strict norms with low tolerance for deviance; loose cultures are flexible and permissive. Shaped by historical threats (plague, disaster), it affects everything from queuing to innovation.

为什么一被提醒“你们这拨人数学差”，考试反而真的考砸了？

Why does being reminded your group is bad at math make people actually perform worse?

斯蒂尔与阿伦森的“刻板印象威胁”（Steele & Aronson, 1995）指，被激活的负面群体标签会占用工作记忆并引发焦虑，拖垮表现，形成自我实现的预言。它不是能力问题，而是情境中被额外加载的心理负担，换掉标签语境成绩就能回升。
Steele and Aronson's stereotype threat (1995) means an activated negative group label drains working memory and spikes anxiety, sinking performance in a self-fulfilling loop. It is not ability but an extra situational load; change the label context and scores rebound.

什么是“基本归因错误”——为何总把别人行为归为“人品”？

What is the fundamental attribution error—why blame character for others' acts?

我们解释他人行为时常过度归因为性格、低估情境（如迟到=不靠谱，而非堵车）。对自己则相反（自我服务偏差）。它源于注意焦点在“人”而非“环境”。
We over-attribute others' acts to character and under-weight context (late = unreliable, not traffic). For ourselves the reverse holds (self-serving bias). Attention fixes on the person, not the situation.

服从权威在什么条件下会减弱？——距离与责任的角色

When does obedience to authority weaken—the roles of distance and responsibility?

后续研究显示服从随“受害者距离近”“权威远程/不具名”而变化：当能看见受害者或自己亲手执行，服从下降；当责任被分摊给上级，服从上升。说明恶行常是情境产物。
Later work shows obedience drops when the victim is near or one acts directly, and rises when authority is remote or responsibility is diffused upward. It suggests harmful obedience is often a product of situation, not just character.

为什么让对立两群孩子一起完成目标，偏见反而慢慢消了？

Why does putting rival groups toward a shared goal quietly shrink their prejudice?

奥尔波特的“接触假说”（Allport, 1954）认为，当不同群体在平等地位、共同目标、合作且权威支持的条件下接触，刻板印象会被真实经验瓦解。皮尤与特罗普的元分析（Pettigrew & Tropp, 2006）汇总数百项研究证实，单纯增加接触就能小幅降低偏见，满足最佳条件时效果更强。
Allport's contact hypothesis (1954) says prejudice erodes when groups meet as equals, toward shared goals, cooperatively and with institutional support. Pettigrew and Tropp's meta-analysis (2006) of hundreds of studies confirms mere contact lightly reduces bias, and optimal conditions amplify it.

为什么“有人看”反而做得更好或更糟？——社会助长与抑制

Why does an audience make you better or worse—social facilitation vs inhibition?

围观会唤醒驱力：对熟练任务表现提升（社会助长），对生疏任务则因焦虑而变差（社会抑制）。所以观众未必帮倒忙，关键在你是否已自动化该动作。
An audience arouses drive: skilled tasks improve (social facilitation), unfamiliar ones worsen via anxiety (social inhibition). So spectators aren't automatically bad—it depends if the action is automated.

什么是“首因效应”与“近因效应”——顺序如何影响印象？

What are primacy and recency effects—how does order shape impressions?

首因效应指最先信息影响最大（形成第一印象），近因效应指最后信息最易被记住。在连续呈现时两者并存；理解它有助于意识到“顺序”本身就在塑造判断。
Primacy effect: first info weighs most (first impressions); recency effect: last info is easiest to recall. Both coexist in serial presentation; knowing this shows order itself shapes judgment.

为什么“随大流”在进化上曾是好事？

Why was following the crowd adaptive in evolution?

早期人类靠群体生存，跟随多数常等于跟随安全与正确（如哪条路有食物）。从众因此被保留，今天多数情境仍便捷，只是网络时代它也可能放大错误。
Early humans survived via groups; following the majority often meant safety and correctness (which path has food). Conformity persisted, still handy today, though online it can also amplify error.
