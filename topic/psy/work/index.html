为什么“番茄工作法”对一部分人有效，对另一部分人没用？

Why does the Pomodoro technique work for some people but not others?

它的核心是把工作切成有边界的时段，降低启动阻力和拖延；但对深陷“心流”或需长段思考的人，频繁打断反而伤效率。关键不在钟，而在找到自己的专注节奏。
Its core is slicing work into bounded sessions to lower start-up friction and procrastination, but for people in flow or needing long thinking, frequent breaks hurt. The point is not the timer but finding your own focus rhythm.

为什么在邮件和文档之间反复跳来跳去，一天会格外累？

Why does jumping between email and documents leave us unusually tired?

心理学称“注意力残留”：切换任务时，上一件事还占着一部分注意力没退场。频繁切换让大脑始终处于半加载状态，效率与精力双双下降。
Called attention residue, when you switch tasks part of your attention stays stuck on the previous one. Frequent switching keeps the brain half-loaded, draining both efficiency and energy.

为什么用“时间块”规划一天，能减少决策疲劳？

Why does time-blocking a day reduce decision fatigue?

每个“现在做什么”的临时决定都消耗意志力，累积成决策疲劳；时间块提前把选择定好，省下脑力留给真正重要的事。它把“随机应变”换成“预先承诺”。
Every spur-of-the-moment what now choice spends willpower and builds decision fatigue; time blocks pre-decide, saving mental energy for what matters. It swaps improvising for pre-commitment.

为什么我们做计划时，预计的时间总比实际短？

Why are our time estimates in planning almost always too short?

这叫“规划谬误”：人倾向于只想理想路径，忽略意外和切换成本。一个好用的补丁是“参考过去类似任务实际花了多久”，而非凭感觉估。
Called the planning fallacy, we picture only the ideal path and ignore surprises and switching costs. A useful fix is anchoring on how long similar tasks actually took before, not a feeling.

为什么长时间专注后，我们需要的是“真休息”而不是更刺激的内容？

Why after long focus do we need real rest, not more stimulating content?

专注消耗的是同一套注意资源，再喂刺激只是在不同频道继续耗。真正的恢复是低刺激、让默认网络松弛的活动，比如发呆、散步、闭眼。
Focus spends the same attention resource, and more stimulation just keeps spending on another channel. Real recovery is low-stimulus calm that lets the default network loosen, like idling, walking or closing your eyes.

为什么午休去散步，比刷手机更能恢复精力？

Why does a walk at lunch restore energy better than phone scrolling?

散步结合轻微活动、自然光和脱离屏幕，能重置注意力并提升下午的警觉；刷手机则继续喂给大脑碎片刺激，休息得不彻底。
A walk mixes light movement, daylight and screen break, resetting attention and lifting afternoon alertness, while scrolling keeps feeding fragmented stimuli and rests incompletely.

为什么手机“通知红点”总能把专注打断得干干净净？

Why do notification badges break focus so completely?

红点触发好奇心与“万一重要”的担忧，是一种强大的注意捕获；每次点开都留下注意力残留。关掉非必要通知，是保护专注最便宜的办法。
A badge triggers curiosity and worry it might be important, a strong attention capture, and each open leaves residue. Turning off non-essential notifications is the cheapest way to protect focus.

下班后，为什么“心理脱离”对工作恢复这么重要？

After work, why is psychological detachment so important for recovery?

如果脑子里还一直转着工作，注意力和情绪就始终没“下班”，疲劳会累积。真正放下——哪怕一小段不看消息——才能让身心重置。
If work keeps spinning in your head, attention and mood never clock out and fatigue piles up. Genuinely letting go, even a short no-message stretch, resets body and mind.

为什么会议太多会悄悄损耗一天的专注？

Why do too many meetings quietly drain a day's focus?

每次会议都打断深度工作的节奏，并留下注意力残留，恢复需要时间。把会议集中、留出整块“无会时段”，能保护真正产出所需的专注。
Each meeting breaks the rhythm of deep work and leaves attention residue that takes time to recover. Batching meetings and protecting blocked no-meeting time guards the focus real output needs.

为什么“做完正事再玩”比“先玩再做”更可持续？

Why is work then reward more sustainable than play then work?

先玩容易让娱乐无限拉长、正事被推到精力最低点；先完成再放松，奖励变得确定且毫无负担。把难的事放在精力高点，是更省力的安排。
Playing first tends to stretch leisure and push real work to the lowest-energy point; finishing first makes the reward certain and guilt-free. Put hard tasks at your energy peak, a simpler arrangement.
