音乐心理学中的“期望—情绪”理论如何解释听音乐时的紧张与释放体验?

How does the expectation-tension model account for felt tension and release in music?

迈耶以及后来的休伦认为,音乐情绪很大程度上源于听者对后续音响的预测:旋律偏离已习得的音阶统计规律时制造张力,而向主音解决则带来释放感。休伦的ITPRA框架进一步把反应拆解为想象、张力反应与预测评估等阶段,说明恰到好处的意外为何比完全可预测或彻底混乱的序列更打动人。脑成像研究亦发现,音乐的惊喜时刻伴随伏隔核的多巴胺能活动。
Leonard Meyer, and later David Huron, argue that musical emotion largely arises from listeners' predictions: melodic deviation from learned scale statistics builds tension, while resolution toward the tonic delivers release. Huron's ITPRA framework decomposes the response into imagination, tension reaction, and predictive appraisal stages, explaining why a well-placed surprise moves us more than wholly predictable or chaotic sequences. Neuroimaging likewise finds dopaminergic activity in the nucleus accumbens at moments of musical surprise.

为什么有些音乐段落会让人起鸡皮疙瘩、甚至浑身一颤?

Why do some passages of music give listeners goosebumps or a shiver?

音乐战栗多出现在期待被延迟或突然兑现的时刻,例如和声的意外转折或漫长渐强后的爆发;休伦的期待理论认为,大脑持续对声音事件做概率预测,预测落空触发唤醒反应,延迟兑现则放大随后的奖赏体验。萨林普尔等人的成像研究发现,多巴胺在“期待阶段”与“高潮体验阶段”分别于背侧与腹侧纹状体释放,把这种快感锚定到了具体神经回路。
Musical chills tend to strike when expectation is delayed or suddenly fulfilled — an unexpected harmonic turn, or the burst after a long crescendo. Huron's expectation theory holds that the brain continuously issues probabilistic predictions about sound events; violated predictions trigger arousal, while delayed fulfillment amplifies the reward that follows. Imaging work by Salimpoor and colleagues found dopamine released in dorsal striatum during anticipation and ventral striatum during peak experience, anchoring the pleasure to specific circuits.

Why do we feel shy?

在陌生人或很多人面前，身体会变得紧张，想躲起来一点，这就是害羞。害羞没关系，慢慢熟悉了就会放松。
Around strangers or crowds your body gets nervous and wants to hide a bit—that's shyness. It's okay; you relax as you get used to people.

我们为什么会感到尴尬？

Why do we feel embarrassed?

当你觉得别人在评价自己、或出了小差错时，大脑会触发脸红和想躲的反应，这就是尴尬。它提醒你在意别人的看法，是正常的社交信号。
When you think others are judging you or you made a small slip, the brain triggers blushing and hiding—that's embarrassment. It shows you care about others' views, a normal social signal.
