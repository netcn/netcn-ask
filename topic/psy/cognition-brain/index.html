双过程理论里，直觉（系统1）在什么情况下反而可靠？

In dual-process theory, when is intuition (System 1) actually reliable?

在专家积累了大量结构化经验的领域（如棋手、护士识症），直觉是基于模式匹配的快速判断，往往准确。但在低经验、高风险或统计稀疏的领域，直觉易偏，应让系统2复核。
In domains where experts hold vast structured experience (chess, nurses spotting deterioration), intuition is fast pattern-matching and often accurate. In low-experience, high-stakes or statistically sparse contexts, intuition errs and System 2 should double-check.

为什么我们总看到“支持自己观点”的证据？

Why do we keep noticing evidence that backs our own view?

这叫确认偏误：大脑在搜索和解读信息时会系统性地偏向自己已有的信念，忽略反例，甚至把模糊信息往自己方向解释。它节省认知资源，但也让我们难以更新观点。主动“寻求反对自己的证据”是少数能对冲它的方法，也是科学思维的核心习惯。
Confirmation bias: when seeking and interpreting information, the brain systematically favors existing beliefs, ignores counterexamples, and reads ambiguous evidence its own way. It saves cognitive effort but blocks updating. Actively hunting evidence against your view is one of the few countermeasures — and the core habit of scientific thinking.

Status-quo bias is over-preferring the default or current state, from loss aversion, inertia and switching costs. It explains why opt-out organ donation far exceeds opt-in, and reminds designers to use defaults wisely.

工作记忆真的只有一个“储物间”吗？

Is working memory really just one storage room?

巴德利提出，工作记忆由中央执行系统统一调度，下面分设语音回路（处理声音和语言信息）、视觉空间画板（处理图像和空间信息）和情景缓冲器（把各来源信息整合成连贯片段）。所以边听讲边做笔记会互相挤占语音回路，而边看图边听语音则相对不冲突。这也解释了为什么“分通道”学习（图文声并用）常更有效。
Baddeley's model splits working memory into a central executive plus three subsystems: the phonological loop (speech and sound), the visuospatial sketchpad (images and space), and the episodic buffer (integrating information into coherent episodes). Listening while taking notes competes for the same loop, while looking at a diagram while listening clashes less. That's why multimodal learning often works better.

为什么有的学生能准确知道“自己会不会”，有的总高估自己？

Why can some students accurately judge what they know while others overestimate?

这是“元认知”——对自己认知过程的监控。成绩好的学生常在自测后准确判断薄弱点，再针对性下功夫；而新手常把“眼熟”误当成“掌握”，因为再认比回忆容易得多，这就是“熟悉感陷阱”。训练元认知很简单：合上书本默写、向自己或他人讲解、先预估考试分数再对照实际——准确性会逐步提高。
That's metacognition — monitoring one's own cognitive processes. Strong students judge their weak spots accurately after self-testing and work on them; novices mistake familiarity for mastery, because recognition is far easier than recall — the “familiarity trap.” Training metacognition is straightforward: recall without the book, explain aloud, predict your exam score and compare. Accuracy improves with practice.

为什么有些人在重要考试前“故意不复习”或先玩个痛快？

Why do some people deliberately skip studying before a big exam?

这叫“自我妨碍”：为了保护自尊，预先制造“失败的理由”——考前不复习、熬夜、声称“我根本没准备”。如果考砸，可以归咎于没复习而不是没能力；如果考好，则显得“天赋高”。它短期内保护自我形象，长期却损害成绩和自信。更健康的路径是把“怕失败”换成“努力本身就是成长”，允许自己不完美地尝试。
That's self-handicapping: creating excuses in advance to protect self-esteem — skipping study, staying up late, insisting “I didn't prepare.” Fail then gets blamed on lack of preparation, not ability; success looks like raw talent. It shields the self-image short-term but erodes grades and confidence long-term. A healthier path is trading “fear of failure” for “effort is growth” and allowing imperfect attempts.

Why do early teens suddenly become able to reason about pure abstractions?

皮亚杰认为从12岁左右进入形式运算阶段：能对抽象命题进行逻辑推理，能“假设—演绎”地想“如果……那么……”，而不必依赖眼前的具体事物。这让青少年能够系统检验假设、思考未来与理想。当然，是否充分发展也受教育和环境支持影响，不是年龄一到就自动全部出现。
Piaget placed the formal operational stage around age 12: people can reason logically about abstract propositions and run hypothetical “if...then” thought without concrete objects in front of them. That enables systematic hypothesis testing, thinking about the future, and ideals. Whether it fully develops also depends on education and environmental support — it's not automatic at a set age.

为什么在嘈杂场合分心时，我们常把红帽子和蓝衣服“错配”成红衣服的人？

Why, when distracted in a crowd, do we often misbind a red hat with a blue shirt into a red-shirted person?

特雷斯曼的特征整合理论（Treisman & Gelade, 1980）认为，颜色、形状等“特征”在前期注意阶段被平行自动登记，但要把它们绑定成完整物体需要集中注意。当注意资源被抢走时，特征会错误组合成“错觉结合”，于是出现了红帽子配蓝衣服的“幽灵对象”。
Treisman and Gelade's feature-integration theory (1980) says basic features like color and shape are registered automatically and in parallel, but binding them into one object needs focused attention. When attention is stolen, features miscombine into illusory conjunctions - hence the ghost of a red-hat-with-blue-shirt person.

为什么自己的旧东西，总觉得比别人的更值钱？

Why do we value our own old belongings more than identical ones we don't own?

这叫“禀赋效应”：一旦拥有某物，就会不自觉地给它更高估值，因为“放弃”会触发损失厌恶——失去的痛苦被大脑放大。脑成像研究显示，面对“可能失去自己拥有的物品”时，情绪相关脑区的活动比“可能得到”更强。这就是二手交易总谈不拢的原因：同一件东西在买卖双方眼里价格不同。
That's the endowment effect: owning something inflates its value because giving it up triggers loss aversion — the pain of losing is amplified. Neuroimaging shows stronger emotion-related brain responses to losing what we own than to gaining. That's why second-hand deals often collapse: the same item is priced differently by buyer and seller.

The anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) acts like a conflict detector: when competing responses co-activate (e.g., Stroop), it signals and the prefrontal cortex allocates attention to resolve it. This monitor-control loop underpins self-control and distraction resistance.

为什么有朋友在旁边时，青少年更容易冒险？

Why do adolescents take more risks when friends are around?

脑成像研究发现，同伴在场会放大青少年奖赏脑区的反应，让“被同伴看到”的刺激格外诱人，而负责自我控制的前额叶此时还不够成熟，两者不同步就推高了冒险。这不是“被带坏”，而是社会奖赏系统与控制系统的发育时间差。给青少年提供安全的冒险出口（运动、竞赛、创作）和可以讨论风险的空间，能把冲动引向成长。
Neuroimaging shows peers' presence amplifies adolescent reward-brain responses, making “being seen by friends” especially enticing, while the still-immature prefrontal control system lags behind — the mismatch drives risk-taking. It's not “bad influence” but a developmental gap between social-reward and control systems. Offering safe outlets for risk (sports, competition, creation) and space to talk it through channels the impulse toward growth.

前瞻性记忆是“记得在将来执行某意图”（如服药、赴约），分基于时间和基于事件两类。它依赖中央执行与提醒线索，比回想过去更容易被干扰，所以“走到厨房却忘了干嘛”很常见。
Prospective memory is remembering to perform an intention in the future (taking medicine, keeping an appointment), split into time- and event-based. It relies on the central executive and cues and is more disruption-prone than recalling the past, hence 'walked to the kitchen and forgot why.'

什么是“必要难度”（desirable difficulties）？为什么顺手的学法未必好？

What are desirable difficulties—why does the easy method not mean the better one?

必要难度指那些增加当下提取努力、却提升长期学习的干扰（如间隔、交错、自测）。它们降低流畅感却强化记忆，提醒我们“学得轻松”不等于“学得牢”。
Desirable difficulties are manipulations that increase retrieval effort now but boost learning later (spacing, interleaving, self-testing). They reduce fluency yet strengthen memory, reminding us that easy now is not learned well.

工作记忆的“中央执行”还负责什么——为什么它能“刷新”和“更新”信息？

Beyond storage, what does the central executive of working memory do—why can it refresh and update information?

工作记忆的中央执行像调度员：它能把不再相关的信息从注意中清掉，并主动把新信息“刷新”进意识焦点（如把“2+3”更新成“5”）。这种更新能力往往比单纯容量更能预测阅读与推理成绩。
The central executive acts like a dispatcher: it clears no-longer-relevant items from attention and actively refreshes new information into the focus of consciousness (e.g., updating 2+3 to 5). This updating ability often predicts reading and reasoning better than raw capacity.

什么是“神经发生”？成年大脑还会长新神经元吗？

What is neurogenesis—does the adult brain still grow new neurons?

过去认为成年不再生神经元，现已知海马齿状回等区终生有新神经元生成（神经发生），受运动、学习、丰富环境促进，压力与衰老则抑制。它提示大脑始终保有更新潜力。
Once thought impossible in adults, new neurons are now known to form for life in areas like the hippocampal dentate gyrus (neurogenesis), boosted by exercise, learning and enrichment, suppressed by stress and aging. It shows the brain keeps a capacity to renew.

注意会被“自动捕获”吗——为什么手机通知总能偷走目光？

Can attention be captured automatically—why do phone notifications steal our gaze?

显著刺激（突然亮起、移动、声音）会由自下而上的显著网络自动触发注意捕获，即使你努力不去看。它是进化留下的警报机制，但也让通知和广告轻易劫持注意。
Salient stimuli (a sudden flash, motion, sound) automatically trigger attentional capture via the bottom-up salience network, even when you try to ignore them. It is an evolutionary alarm, but also why notifications and ads hijack attention.

成人的“执行功能”也包含抑制、更新、灵活切换三件事吗？

Do adults' executive functions also include inhibition, updating and flexible shifting?

是的。执行功能三大核心：抑制（挡住冲动与分心）、工作记忆更新（在脑中保持并操作信息）、认知灵活性（在任务间切换）。它们由前额叶主导，是计划、自控与适应新情况的底层能力。
Yes. The three core executive functions are inhibition (blocking impulses/distraction), working-memory updating (holding and manipulating info), and cognitive flexibility (shifting between tasks). Prefrontal-driven, they underpin planning, self-control and adapting to novelty.

睡眠中的“记忆巩固”到底做了什么？

What does memory consolidation during sleep actually do?

睡眠（尤其慢波与REM）把白天的脆弱记忆回放并转移、整合进长时存储，同时修剪冗余。缺睡会削弱巩固，所以考前熬夜常得不偿失。
During sleep (especially slow-wave and REM), the day's fragile memories are replayed, transferred and integrated into long-term storage while redundancy is pruned. Skimping sleep weakens consolidation—cramming all night often backfires.
