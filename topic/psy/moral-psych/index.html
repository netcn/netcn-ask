道德基础理论如何解释不同政治文化群体的道德判断分歧?

How does moral foundations theory explain divergence in moral judgment across political-cultural groups?

海特等人主张人类道德直觉建立在若干进化而来的心理基础之上,涵盖关爱/伤害、公平/欺骗、忠诚/背叛、权威/颠覆与圣洁/堕落五个维度。跨文化问卷显示,自由派主要依赖关爱与公平两种基础,而保守派对五种基础的权重更为均衡。这种“基础敏感性差异”解释了双方为何互指对方失德,也为跨意识形态的沟通提供了可操作的诊断框架。
Jonathan Haidt and colleagues argue that moral intuition rests on evolved psychological foundations spanning care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, and sanctity/degradation. Cross-cultural surveys show liberals rely chiefly on care and fairness, whereas conservatives weight all five foundations more evenly. This difference in foundation sensitivity explains why each side deems the other immoral, and offers an operational diagnostic for cross-ideological dialogue.

道德判断的双加工理论如何解释人们在电车难题里“扳道岔”与“推人”两种反应的分歧?

How does the dual-process theory of moral judgment explain why people diverge between throwing a switch and pushing a man in trolley dilemmas?

格林的双加工模型认为,个人化困境(亲手推人)强烈激活以杏仁核为核心的情境情绪系统,产生对直接身体伤害的排斥;非个人化困境(扳动道岔)则更多调用前额叶支持的功利权衡,两个系统的竞争强度决定了反应延迟与立场摇摆。库什曼进一步区分行动—后果与意图—后果的不同编码方式,说明人们对造成同等伤害的不同手段会给出系统性不同的道德评价。
Greene's dual-process model holds that personal dilemmas (pushing with your own hands) strongly engage a situational emotion system centered on the amygdala, producing aversion to direct bodily harm, whereas impersonal dilemmas (throwing the switch) lean more on prefrontal utilitarian weighing; the competition between the two systems shapes response latencies and vacillation. Cushman further distinguishes how action-outcome and intention-outcome are encoded, explaining why people systematically judge different means to the same harm.

为什么人们做出道德判断时往往是先有直觉、再补上理由?

Why do people often arrive at a moral judgment first and only afterwards come up with reasons for it?

海特的社会直觉主义模型认为,快速的情绪性直觉直接产生道德判断,而推理主要在事后为已定的结论辩护。'道德失声'实验提供了关键证据:面对并无实际伤害的禁忌情境,被试坚称行为是错的却给不出站得住脚的理由。可见理性论证更像律师而非法官,主要用于说服他人,而非形成判断本身。
Haidt's social intuitionist model holds that fast affective intuitions produce moral judgments directly, while reasoning mainly serves as post hoc advocacy for conclusions already reached. Studies of 'moral dumbfounding' supply key evidence: faced with harmless taboo scenarios, participants insist the act is wrong yet cannot give any defensible justification. Reasoning thus behaves more like a lawyer than a judge, built to persuade others rather than to form the judgment itself.
