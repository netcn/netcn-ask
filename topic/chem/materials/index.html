混凝土凝固时为什么会收缩开裂，这和水分迁移有什么关系？

Why does setting concrete shrink and crack, and what role does water movement play?

混凝土凝固（水化）时，水泥与水反应生成凝胶并消耗水分；表面蒸发和内部水分向表面迁移（干燥收缩）使体积缩小，而内部约束限制了自由收缩，于是在内部产生拉应力。当拉应力超过混凝土早期抗拉强度时便开裂。控制水灰比、养护保湿、添加减缩剂或配筋可减轻收缩开裂。
As concrete sets, cement reacts with water (hydration) to form a gel and consumes water; evaporation from the surface and water migrating outward (drying shrinkage) make the volume shrink, but internal constraints prevent free contraction, building tensile stress. When that stress exceeds the young concrete's tensile strength, cracks form. Keeping the mix wet during curing, lowering the water–cement ratio, adding shrinkage reducers, or using reinforcement all help limit cracking.

ABX₃ perovskites absorb strongly and have long carrier diffusion, giving rapid efficiency gains; yet they degrade with water, heat and light, so stability is the key barrier.

为什么高温超导材料（如钇钡铜氧）只需液氮冷却就能超导？零电阻意味着什么？

Why does a high-temperature superconductor like YBCO superconduct with just liquid-nitrogen cooling, and what does zero resistance mean?

常规超导体（如铅、铌）的超导转变温度很低，只有几开尔文到20开尔文左右，必须用昂贵的液氦冷却。1986年科学家发现铜氧化物超导体后，很快研制出钇钡铜氧（YBa2Cu3O7，转变温度约92开尔文），超过液氮沸点77开尔文，只需廉价的液氮即可。零电阻意味着电流通过时不产生焦耳热、没有能量损耗，可以承载巨大的电流密度；超导体还完全排斥磁场（迈斯纳效应），从而实现磁悬浮。至今铜氧化物高温超导的机理仍在争论中，但零损耗输电和超导磁体已经开始改变技术面貌。
Conventional superconductors like lead and niobium have very low transition temperatures, only a few kelvin to about 20 K, and need expensive liquid helium. After copper-oxide superconductors were discovered in 1986, YBCO (YBa2Cu3O7, transition temperature about 92 K) quickly followed, exceeding the 77 K boiling point of liquid nitrogen, so it needs only cheap liquid nitrogen. Zero resistance means no Joule heating and no energy loss as current flows, so wires can carry enormous current densities; a superconductor also expels magnetic fields completely (the Meissner effect), enabling levitation. The mechanism of high-temperature superconductivity is still debated, yet lossless power lines and superconducting magnets are already reshaping technology.

为什么合金（如钢）通常比组成它的纯金属更硬、更强？

Why are alloys such as steel usually harder and stronger than the pure metals they are made from?

金属受力变形主要靠晶体中的位错滑移，位错移动越容易，金属越软。纯铁的晶格非常规整，位错几乎畅通无阻；加入碳、铬等后，异种原子尺寸与铁不同，造成局部晶格畸变，像钉子一样钉住位错，使它难以运动，金属就变硬变强，这叫做固溶强化。析出的碳化物和晶粒细化还能进一步阻碍位错。但强度提升往往伴随塑性下降，所以钢需要淬火、回火等热处理，在强度与韧性之间取得平衡。
Metals deform mainly by the sliding of dislocations through the crystal, and the easier they move the softer the metal. Pure iron has a very regular lattice where dislocations glide almost freely; adding carbon or chromium introduces atoms of different size that distort the lattice locally, acting like nails that pin dislocations and stop them moving - solid-solution strengthening. Precipitated carbides and grain refinement pile on further obstacles. But strength usually comes at the cost of ductility, so steel is heat-treated through quenching and tempering to balance strength against toughness.

为什么石墨烯又轻又强？

Why is graphene strong yet light?

石墨烯只有一个原子厚，碳以 sp² 杂化铺成蜂窝平面，受力时由面内共价键直接承担，不靠层间弱接合；薄到这份上裂纹与位错无处容身，强度接近本征上限而非缺陷下限。它几乎全是表面，面密度可忽略，比强度约比多数工程材料高一个数量级。但这说的是单片：宏观膜与复合材料有褶皱、滑移和界面失效，远达不到，石墨能当润滑剂也正出于此。
Graphene is one atomic layer, carbons sp²-bonded into a honeycomb plane, so loading strains those in-plane bonds directly rather than the weak join between layers, and a layer this thin leaves no room for cracks or dislocations, putting strength near the intrinsic ceiling instead of a defect-set floor. Nearly all surface, its mass per area is negligible, giving specific strength about an order of magnitude above most engineering materials. That is one clean flake: membranes and composites fold, slip between layers and fail at interfaces, and graphite is soft enough to lubricate for the same reason.

为什么钢化玻璃比普通玻璃结实好几倍，摔碎后还会碎成小颗粒？

Why is tempered glass several times stronger than ordinary glass, and why does it shatter into small granules?

钢化玻璃是把普通玻璃加热到接近软化点后快速均匀冷却制成的。冷却时表面先凝固、内部后收缩，结果表面形成压应力、内部形成张应力；玻璃的抗压强度远高于抗拉强度，表面的压应力像铠甲一样抵消外来的拉应力，所以更结实。一旦受冲击破裂，内部储存的张应力使整块玻璃瞬间碎成圆钝的小颗粒，而不是尖锐的大碎片，安全性因此大大提高。
Tempered glass is ordinary glass heated near its softening point and then cooled rapidly and uniformly. The surface solidifies first while the interior contracts later, leaving compressive stress in the surface and tensile stress inside; because glass resists compression far better than tension, the surface's compressive armour cancels incoming tensile stress and makes it stronger. When it finally breaks, the stored internal tension shatters the whole pane instantly into small, blunt granules rather than sharp shards - which is what makes it far safer.

用形状记忆合金做的眼镜架被弯折后，为什么一加热又能弹回原来的形状？

Why does a bent eyeglass frame made of shape-memory alloy spring back to its original shape when heated?

形状记忆合金（最常见的是镍钛合金）存在两种晶体结构：低温的马氏体和高温的奥氏体。常温下弯折只改变马氏体晶体的孪晶排列方式，原子没有真正滑移，变形是可逆的；加热到相变温度以上，合金转变回奥氏体，晶体恢复原本排列，宏观形状也跟着复原。这一记住高温相形状的本领来自可逆的固相相变，而不是普通金属的弹性，因此被用于牙箍、血管支架和航天机构中。
Shape-memory alloys, most commonly nitinol (nickel-titanium), have two crystal structures: martensite at low temperature and austenite at high temperature. Bending at room temperature only rearranges the twin domains of the martensite without real atomic slip, so the deformation is reversible; heating above the transformation temperature converts the alloy back to austenite, whose original atomic arrangement restores, and the frame recovers its shape. This memory of the high-temperature phase comes from a reversible solid-state phase change, not ordinary elasticity - which is why it is used in braces, vascular stents and space mechanisms.

纳米金溶液是红色的，而大块金是黄色的，为什么缩小到纳米尺度颜色就变了？

Gold nanoparticles in solution are red while bulk gold is yellow - why does shrinking to the nanoscale change the colour?

纳米颗粒的尺寸已经小到可以和可见光波长相比，光不再被金属表面直接反射，而是激发颗粒中的自由电子集体振荡，形成局域表面等离激元共振，强烈吸收特定波长的光。金的等离激元共振落在绿光附近，吸收掉绿光后透过的光呈红色；颗粒大小、形状改变时共振波长移动，颜色也随之从红到紫、蓝变化。纳米材料比表面积巨大、表面原子占比高，所以催化活性、熔点等性质也和大块材料明显不同。
When particles shrink to sizes comparable to visible wavelengths, light no longer reflects off a flat metal surface; instead it excites collective oscillation of the free electrons inside each particle - localized surface plasmon resonance - which strongly absorbs light of a specific wavelength. Gold's resonance sits near the green, so green light is absorbed and the transmitted light looks red; changing particle size or shape shifts the resonance, marching the colour from red through purple to blue. Nanomaterials also have enormous surface area and a high fraction of surface atoms, so catalytic activity, melting point and other properties differ markedly from bulk material.

Why do modern furnaces blow pure oxygen instead of air in steelmaking?

Air is ~78% nitrogen, which cools the bath and forms NOx. Pure oxygen gives faster decarburization, higher temperature and cleaner steel.

碳纤维材料为什么能同时做到很轻和很强？

How can carbon-fiber materials be simultaneously so light and so strong?

碳纤维由含碳原料在高温下石墨化得到，内部是沿纤维轴向高度取向的细小石墨微晶，碳-碳共价键极其牢固且方向性好，因此沿纤维方向的抗拉强度可达钢的数倍。碳的原子量又小，密度只有钢的约四分之一，所以比强度（强度除以密度）遥遥领先。用树脂复合后，受力通过基体传递到纤维，铺层方向设计得当，就能得到又轻又强的结构件，广泛用于飞机、赛车、自行车和风电叶片。
Carbon fiber is made by graphitizing carbon-rich feedstocks at high temperature; inside, tiny graphite crystallites align tightly along the fiber axis, and carbon-carbon covalent bonds are extremely strong and highly directional, so tensile strength along the fiber can be several times that of steel. Carbon is also light, about a quarter of steel's density, so its specific strength (strength divided by density) is far ahead. Embedded in resin, loads transfer through the matrix into the fibers, and with proper ply orientation you get structural parts that are both light and strong, used in aircraft, race cars, bicycles and wind-turbine blades.
