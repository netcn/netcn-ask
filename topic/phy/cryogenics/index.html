为什么越接近绝对零度，给系统再降一度就变得越来越“贵”？

Why does cooling a system by one more degree get exponentially 'costlier' near absolute zero?

热力学第三定律说，系统的熵在绝对零度趋于零，而把熵降到零需要无限多步操作。实际中每靠近一点零度，可用来“吸热”的余地都更小，制冷效率和代价急剧恶化。
The third law says entropy approaches zero at absolute zero, and reaching zero entropy needs infinitely many steps. In practice each step closer leaves less 'room' to dump heat, so efficiency collapses and cost explodes.

#第三定律 #熵 #制冷

为什么液氮（约 -196℃）能瞬间把活的花朵冻脆，却不会让装它的金属杯结冰爆炸？

Why can liquid nitrogen (~-196°C) instantly freeze a flower brittle without making its metal cup explode?

液氮极冷，花朵里的水分瞬间结冰膨胀、细胞被撑碎，所以一碰就碎。金属杯只是被降温，本身不会“结冰”，而且在开放容器里氮气蒸发跑掉，不会憋炸。
The extreme cold freezes a flower's water in an instant, swelling and shattering cells so it crumbles. The metal cup only cools; it doesn't 'freeze', and in an open vessel the nitrogen boils away instead of building pressure.

#液氮 #低温 #脆化

为什么液氦比液氮还冷得多，却更稀有、更难造？

Why is liquid helium far colder than liquid nitrogen, yet rarer and harder to make?

液氦要冷到比 -269℃ 才变成液体，比液氮低了快 100 度，制冷极其费电。而且氦在地球上很少，主要混在天然气里，所以又贵又难造。
Helium only liquefies below about -269°C, nearly 100°C colder than nitrogen, so cooling it wastes huge energy. Helium is also scarce on Earth, found mostly in natural gas, making it costly and hard to produce.
