A white dwarf, supported by electron degeneracy pressure after its fusion ends, crams about a solar mass into an Earth-sized ball, reaching roughly a tonne per cubic centimeter. It stops collapsing because electrons obey the Pauli exclusion principle and cannot share a quantum state, generating a quantum pressure that balances gravity. A single spoonful brought to Earth would weigh about a tonne — no ordinary container could hold it.

DAMA和LUX这两个实验是怎么搜寻暗物质粒子的？

How do experiments like DAMA and LUX search for dark matter particles?

如果暗物质由弱相互作用大质量粒子（WIMP）组成，它们应当像影子一样不断穿过地球，偶尔与原子核碰撞并留下微小的能量信号。意大利的DAMA实验声称在碘化钠晶体中观测到了随地球公转方向变化的年度调制信号；美国的LUX实验则把液氙探测器埋藏在极深的地下，用光电倍增管捕捉稀有事件。LUX并未发现可信的信号，与DAMA的结果存在矛盾，这正是当前暗物质研究最核心的谜团之一。
If dark matter is made of weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs), they should stream through Earth like ghosts and occasionally bounce off an atomic nucleus, leaving a tiny energy signal. Italy's DAMA experiment claimed an annual modulation in sodium-iodide crystals, linked to Earth's direction of travel around the Sun; the US-led LUX experiment buried a liquid-xenon detector deep underground and used photomultipliers to catch rare events. LUX found no credible signal, disagreeing with DAMA — one of the central puzzles in dark matter research today.

科学家凭什么确信存在看不见的暗物质？

What evidence convinces scientists that invisible dark matter exists?

主要有三方面。一是星系旋转曲线：星系外围的恒星转速没有像预期那样随距离下降，说明星系里藏着大量看不见的质量；二是星系团里星系运动太快、引力透镜效应也太强，可见物质解释不了；三是宇宙微波背景辐射的温度涨落图与暗物质分布的理论预言吻合。暗物质约占宇宙物质总量的85%，但它不发光，也几乎不参与电磁相互作用，至今没有被直接探测到。
Three lines of evidence stand out. First, galaxy rotation curves: stars at a galaxy's edge orbit faster than expected, implying hidden mass. Second, galaxies in clusters move too fast and lens background light too strongly for visible matter alone. Third, the ripples in the cosmic microwave background match predictions that include dark matter. It accounts for about 85% of all matter, yet it emits no light and barely touches electromagnetism — it has never been directly detected.

宇宙微波背景辐射是怎么被发现的？它为什么那么重要？

How was the cosmic microwave background discovered, and why does it matter so much?

1965年，彭齐亚斯和威尔逊在调试一台射电天线时，发现无论天线对准哪个方向，都残留一种消除不掉的微弱噪声。它后来被确认为宇宙微波背景辐射，即大爆炸约38万年后宇宙冷却到足以让光自由穿行时留下的余晖。这种辐射均匀地充满整个天空，温度约为2.7开尔文，是大爆炸理论最有力的证据，两人因此获得1978年诺贝尔物理学奖。
In 1965, Penzias and Wilson were debugging a radio antenna when they found an irreducible faint hiss no matter which way the antenna pointed. It turned out to be the cosmic microwave background — the afterglow left when the universe cooled enough, about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, for light to travel freely. This radiation fills the sky uniformly at about 2.7 kelvin, the strongest evidence for the Big Bang, and earned the pair the 1978 Nobel Prize in Physics.

为什么恒星会眨眼，而行星看起来却不怎么闪？

Why do stars twinkle while planets appear to shine steadily?

星光穿过地球大气层时，会被温度、密度不断变化的气流折射，光线方向轻微抖动，看起来就忽明忽暗。恒星远在天边，在地球上只是一个点，一点折射就让整个光点抖动；行星离得近，呈现一个小圆面，圆面上各部分的光一起被扰动，相互平均抵消，所以闪烁弱得多。把望远镜发射到太空，就是为了避开这层大气。
As starlight crosses the atmosphere, pockets of air with shifting density refract it, jiggling the image and making brightness flicker. Stars are so distant that they appear as mere points, so any tiny refraction wobbles the whole point; planets are close enough to show a small disk, and the perturbations of different parts average out, so they twinkle far less. That's exactly why astronomers send telescopes into space — to rise above the atmosphere.

引力波是怎么被探测到的？

How were gravitational waves detected?

2015年，美国的LIGO首次探测到两个黑洞并合发出的引力波信号。引力波是时空本身的涟漪，经过时会让物体间的距离发生极其微小的伸缩。LIGO用两臂各长4公里的激光干涉仪把这种伸缩放大到可测的程度，它记录到的长度变化比质子直径还小约一千倍。这验证了爱因斯坦一百年前的预言，并为人类观测宇宙打开了全新的窗口。
In 2015, the US-based LIGO detected gravitational waves for the first time, from the merger of two black holes. Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime itself, stretching and squeezing distances as they pass. LIGO's laser interferometer with two 4-kilometer arms amplified that stretch until it was measurable — the change was about a thousand times smaller than a proton's diameter. The discovery confirmed Einstein's century-old prediction and opened a new window on the universe.

脉冲星是高速自转的中子星，直径仅约20公里却比太阳还重，表面有极强的磁场。它磁极射出的辐射束随自转扫过地球，我们便收到极规律的射电脉冲，周期从毫秒到数秒不等，稳定得堪比原子钟。贝尔1967年发现首颗脉冲星，休伊什因此获1974年诺贝尔奖。
A pulsar is a rapidly spinning neutron star—about 20 km across yet heavier than the Sun—with an intense magnetic field. Its beams from the magnetic poles sweep past Earth as it rotates, giving extremely regular radio pulses from milliseconds to seconds, as steady as atomic clocks. Bell discovered the first pulsar in 1967; Hewish won the 1974 Nobel Prize.

月亮为什么会有规律的圆缺变化——有时弯弯如钩，有时又圆满如盘？

Why does the Moon regularly change shape—sometimes a thin crescent, sometimes a full disk?

圆缺变化不是因为月球自己会亮会暗：它只反射太阳光，而太阳始终照亮它的一半。月球绕地球一周约 27.3 天，这期间被照亮的半面朝向地球的比例不断变化，从地球看去就有了新月、上弦、满月到下弦的循环。规律性来自轨道运动的周期性；但相位走完一轮是约 29.5 天而不是 27.3 天，因为月球绕地的同时地球也在绕日前进，月球要额外转过一段弧才能重新对上同样的日–地–月夹角。
The changing shape is not the Moon lighting up and dimming: it only reflects sunlight, and the Sun always lights one half of it. The Moon takes about 27.3 days to circle Earth, so during that time the share of the lit half facing Earth keeps changing — giving the cycle from new moon to first quarter, full moon and last quarter. The regularity comes from that orbital period; a full run of phases takes about 29.5 days rather than 27.3, because Earth advances along its own orbit while the Moon circles it, so the Moon must travel an extra arc to meet the same Sun–Earth–Moon angle again.

月球为什么有阴晴圆缺？

Why does the Moon show phases?

我们看到的月光全是反射来的日光，月亮自己并不发光。太阳总是照亮月球的一半，随着月球绕地球转动，被照亮的那一半朝向我们的比例从看不见、到一条弯钩、再到整个圆面，依次出现新月、上弦、满月和下弦，走完一轮约 29.5 天，这就是农历一个月的长度。月相不是地球的影子落在月球上——那是月食，只在满月且太阳、地球、月球几乎排成一条线时才发生。
All the moonlight we see is reflected sunlight; the Moon gives off none of its own. The Sun always lights half of the Moon, and as the Moon orbits Earth the share of that lit half facing us goes from out of sight, to a thin crescent, to the whole disk — new moon, first quarter, full moon and last quarter — taking about 29.5 days, which is why a lunar month is that long. The phases are not Earth's shadow falling on the Moon: that is a lunar eclipse, and it happens only at full moon, when Sun, Earth and Moon sit almost on one line.

为什么行星绕太阳走的轨道是椭圆，而不是正圆？

Why do planets orbit the Sun in ellipses rather than perfect circles?

开普勒在1609年分析第谷的观测数据后发现，行星轨道是椭圆，太阳位于其中一个焦点上——这是开普勒第一定律。牛顿后来用万有引力定律证明：在平方反比的引力下，一般轨道天然就是圆锥曲线（椭圆、抛物线或双曲线），正圆只是速度恰好匹配时的特例。
Analyzing Tycho's observations, Kepler found in 1609 that planetary orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus—his first law. Newton later showed from gravity's inverse-square law that orbits are naturally conic sections (ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola), with a perfect circle only a special case when the speed exactly matches.
