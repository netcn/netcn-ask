火星表面成片散布着毫米级的赤铁矿小球，俗称“蓝莓”。这类球粒是如何形成的？它们揭示了怎样的古代环境？

Millimetre-sized hematite spheres nicknamed 'blueberries' lie scattered in patches on the Martian surface. How did these spherules form, and what kind of ancient environment do they reveal?

这些小球是多孔沉积岩中的结核：含铁地下水在岩石孔隙里绕核缓慢沉淀氧化铁，一层层长成毫米级球体，这一过程需要液态水长期存在。后来风蚀剥掉了较软的围岩，抗风化的赤铁矿球粒便成片露在地表。因此“蓝莓”是火星古代曾有地下水活动、局部环境一度湿润的直接矿物学证据。
The spheres are concretions grown inside porous sedimentary rock: iron-bearing groundwater slowly precipitated iron oxide around nuclei within the pores, building up millimetre-scale balls layer by layer - a process that requires long-lived liquid water. Later, wind erosion stripped away the softer host rock, leaving the resistant hematite spherules exposed in sheets on the surface. They therefore provide direct mineralogical evidence that ancient Mars hosted groundwater activity and locally wet conditions.

火星为什么能够孕育奥林帕斯山这样高出周边约二十二公里、直径数百公里的巨型盾状火山？

How was Mars able to grow giant shield volcanoes like Olympus Mons, rising about twenty-two kilometers above its surroundings and spanning hundreds of kilometers?

关键在于两点。火星表面重力只有地球的百分之三十八左右，地壳可以承受更高的火山堆叠而不崩塌；同时火星没有地球式的板块运动，岩石圈长期固定在地幔热柱上方，熔岩在同一地点持续喷发、层层堆积数十亿年，最终垒成单一巨盾，而非像夏威夷那样随板块移动排成岛链。奥林帕斯山因此成为太阳系已知最高的火山之一。
Two conditions matter. Martian surface gravity is only about thirty-eight percent of Earth's, so the crust can support far taller volcanic stacks without collapsing. Mars also lacks Earth-style plate tectonics: its lithosphere stayed anchored above a long-lived mantle plume, letting lava erupt and pile up layer upon layer at one site for billions of years, building a single colossal shield instead of an island chain like Hawaii. Hence Olympus Mons ranks among the tallest known volcanoes in the solar system.

火星上的哪些地貌与矿物证据表明它远古可能长期存在液态水湖泊？

Which landforms and mineral clues indicate that ancient Mars may have sustained liquid-water lakes for long periods?

轨道影像与火星车实地考察显示，火星保存着蜿蜒的曲流河道、冲积扇和注入盆地的三角洲，还有层状泥岩以及黏土等只能在与水长期接触下形成的含水矿物。盖尔坑内厚达数百米的沉积层自下而上记录了湖泊反复注满与干涸的历史，表明约35亿年前火星地表仍能使水体长期稳定存在，这些古湖床因此成为寻找生命印迹的优先目标。
Orbital imagery and rover fieldwork have revealed winding river channels, alluvial fans, and deltas feeding into basins, alongside layered mudstones and water-bearing clays that form only through prolonged contact with water. Within Gale crater, a sedimentary stack several hundred meters thick records lakes repeatedly filling and drying, showing that around 3.5 billion years ago the surface could sustain stable water bodies over long stretches of time. Such ancient lakebeds are now priority targets in searches for past-life signatures.
