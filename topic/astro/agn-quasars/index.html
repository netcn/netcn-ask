高红移类星体为什么让天文学家兴奋？

Why do high-redshift quasars excite astronomers?

高红移类星体是早期宇宙（z>6）中心超大质量黑洞（数亿太阳质量）剧烈吸积发光的活动星系核，其光亮到能在百亿光年外被今天的望远镜看到。它们令天文学家兴奋，是因为如此年轻（宇宙不到10亿年）的宇宙竟已存在质量惊人的黑洞，对黑洞形成与星系协同演化理论提出挑战。它们还为研究再电离时期的中性氢提供了强大的背景光源。
High-redshift quasars are active galactic nuclei at z>6 powered by supermassive black holes (hundreds of millions of solar masses) accreting matter so vigorously that they shine across more than ten billion light-years. Astronomers are excited because such massive black holes already existed when the universe was under a billion years old—a challenge for theories of black-hole formation and galaxy co-evolution. They also provide powerful background beacons for probing the neutral hydrogen of the reionization era.

为什么有些活动星系核显示宽发射线，另一些却只有窄线？活动星系核统一模型如何用尘埃环解释这种差异，有哪些观测证据？

Why do some active galactic nuclei show broad emission lines while others show only narrow lines, and what observational evidence supports the unified model's obscuring torus?

统一模型认为宽线区半径仅光日量级、紧贴黑洞，被外圈几秒差距尺度的尘埃环遮挡；视线掠过环侧的天体（如赛弗特2型）看不到宽线，只露出范围大得多的窄线区。关键证据是偏振：NGC 1068的散射光经偏振测量显出隐藏的宽线，证明宽线区存在只是被遮住；此类天体强烈的X射线重吸收也与这一几何一致。
In the unified model, the broad-line region is compact (light-day scales) and hugs the black hole, hidden from side-on views by a dusty torus a few parsecs across; only the much larger narrow-line region stays visible, giving type 2 spectra. The decisive evidence is polarimetry: in NGC 1068, polarized light revealed broad lines scattered into our line of sight, proving the hidden region exists, and the strong X-ray absorption in such sources matches the same geometry.

什么是爱丁顿光度？为什么类星体的极端亮度本身就证明其核心藏着超大质量黑洞？

What is the Eddington luminosity, and why does a quasar's extreme brightness by itself betray a supermassive black hole in its core?

吸积供能的天体存在辐射压上限：光度升高时，辐射对电离气体的外推力最终抵消引力，把气体驱散、掐断燃料，这一临界光度即爱丁顿光度，正比于中心质量，约合每太阳质量三万倍太阳光度。要在极小区域内稳定输出千亿倍太阳光度，只能依靠数亿倍太阳质量的致密黑洞在限下吸积，普通恒星集团无法胜任。
Accretion-powered sources face a radiation-pressure ceiling: as luminosity rises, the outward push on ionized gas eventually balances gravity and would disperse the fuel altogether. This critical level, the Eddington luminosity, scales with central mass—about thirty thousand solar luminosities per solar mass. Steadily emitting hundreds of billions of solar luminosities from a tiny volume therefore demands a compact black hole of several hundred million solar masses accreting below that limit; a cluster of ordinary stars cannot manage it.
