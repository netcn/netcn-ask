什么是“引力弹弓”？

What is a gravity assist (slingshot)?

引力弹弓是利用行星引力为探测器加速或改变方向的技术：探测器借行星公转的动量“借力”，从而节省燃料抵达更远目标，如旅行者号借助木星、土星加速。
A gravity assist uses a planet's gravity to speed up or redirect a probe, borrowing the planet's orbital momentum to save fuel for farther targets—Voyager used Jupiter and Saturn this way.

宇航员在太空里怎么生活？

How do astronauts live in space?

在太空站里，因为没有重力，宇航员会飘起来，睡觉要把自己固定在睡袋里。他们吃特制的脱水或压缩食品，喝水也要小心不让水珠乱飘。每天还要做运动，防止骨头和肌肉在失重下变弱。
In a space station there is no gravity, so astronauts float, and they must strap themselves into sleeping bags to rest. They eat special dried or compressed food and drink carefully so water droplets do not float around. Every day they exercise to keep their bones and muscles from weakening in weightlessness.

什么是地球同步卫星和地球静止卫星？

What are geosynchronous and geostationary satellites?

地球同步卫星的轨道周期恰好等于地球自转周期（约24小时），在赤道上空且轨道圆形时，它相对地面某点几乎不动，这就是地球静止卫星。通信、气象和电视广播卫星常放在这种轨道上，便于地面天线固定对准。它们距地面约3.6万公里。
A geosynchronous satellite has an orbital period equal to Earth's rotation (about 24 hours); if it is circular and over the equator, it stays nearly fixed above one ground point, called geostationary. Communications, weather, and broadcast satellites use this orbit so dishes can aim steadily. They sit about 36,000 km above the ground.

火星探测车有什么用？

What are Mars rovers for?

火星探测车是落在火星表面、可移动的科学实验室，能在不同地点钻取岩石、分析土壤成分并拍照传回地球。它们帮助人类寻找火星曾经有水的证据，以及是否可能存在过生命。好奇号和毅力号是目前最先进的火星车代表。
Mars rovers are mobile science labs that land on Mars and drive around to drill rocks, analyze soil, and send back photos. They help us find evidence that Mars once had water and assess whether life could have existed. Curiosity and Perseverance are the most advanced rovers to date.

为什么人类想要登陆火星？

Why do humans want to go to Mars?

因为火星是离地球较近、环境相对温和的星球，可能曾存在液态水与生命线索，被视为未来人类多行星生存的候选地，也能推动科技与科学探索。
Mars is nearby with relatively mild conditions, may have once had liquid water and life, and is a candidate for future multi-planet living—driving science and technology.

旅行者号探测器现在去哪里了？

Where are the Voyager probes now?

旅行者1号和2号于1977年发射，借助行星引力先后飞掠木星、土星等外行星。它们现已飞出太阳风层、进入星际空间，成为人类送出最远的人造物体。探测器仍缓慢向地球发回关于星际介质的珍贵数据。
Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 and 2 used planetary gravity to fly by outer planets like Jupiter and Saturn. They have now crossed the heliopause into interstellar space, becoming the most distant human-made objects. They still send back precious data about the interstellar medium, though faintly.

什么是引力弹弓效应？

What is the gravity assist effect?

引力弹弓（也称引力助推）是利用行星的引力让探测器靠近时被加速或改变方向，从而节省燃料、飞向更远的目标。探测器从行星运动方向掠过时，会像被甩出的小球一样获得额外速度。旅行者号和许多行星任务都靠它完成长途旅行。
A gravity assist uses a planet's gravity to speed up or redirect a passing spacecraft, saving fuel and reaching farther targets. When a probe swings by in the planet's direction of motion, it gains extra speed like a stone flung from a sling. Voyager and many planetary missions rely on it for long journeys.

什么是小行星采样返回任务？

What is an asteroid sample-return mission?

小行星采样返回任务让航天器飞到小行星上采集岩石和尘埃，再带回地球实验室做精细分析。日本的隼鸟号系列和美国的奥西里斯-REx任务都成功取回样本，帮助研究太阳系最原始的物质。这些样本比陨石更完整地保留了早期太阳系的成分。
An asteroid sample-return mission sends a spacecraft to collect rock and dust from an asteroid and bring it back to Earth labs for detailed analysis. Japan's Hayabusa series and NASA's OSIRIS-REx both succeeded in returning samples, helping study the Solar System's most primitive material. These samples preserve early-Solar-System composition more completely than meteorites.

What is a space telescope?

太空望远镜是发射到太空中、避开地球大气干扰的望远镜，如哈勃和詹姆斯·韦布望远镜，能拍到更清晰、更遥远的宇宙图像。
A space telescope is launched above the atmosphere to avoid distortion—like Hubble and JWST—capturing clearer, deeper views of the universe.

为什么把人送去火星特别困难？

Why is sending humans to Mars so difficult?

火星距离远，单程就要数月，往返加停留可能长达数年，需要大量食物、水和氧气并可靠循环。太空中和火星表面的强辐射会危害健康，着陆时稀薄大气又不足以充分减速，使登陆极其危险。这些难题使载人火星任务至今尚未实现。
Mars is so far that a one-way trip takes months, and a round trip with stay could last years, demanding large, reliably recycled supplies of food, water, and oxygen. Intense radiation in space and on the surface threatens health, and the thin atmosphere gives too little braking for easy landing. These challenges keep crewed Mars missions unrealized so far.

The ISS is a large, multinational crewed spacecraft orbiting about 400 km up; since 2000 astronauts have lived there conducting microgravity research.

国际空间站是什么？

What is the International Space Station?

国际空间站是一个在地球低轨道运行的大型载人空间站，由多国合作建造和维护，自1998年开始组建。它绕地球一圈约90分钟，是宇航员长期生活和做科学实验的地方。它也是人类目前在太空最大的合作工程。
The International Space Station is a large crewed station in low Earth orbit, built and maintained through international cooperation, with assembly begun in 1998. It circles Earth about every 90 minutes and serves as a home where astronauts live and run science experiments. It is the largest human cooperative engineering project in space to date.

詹姆斯·韦伯太空望远镜主要做什么？

What is the James Webb Space Telescope mainly for?

韦伯是哈勃的继任者，主镜直径6.5米，专门在红外波段观测，能看穿尘埃并探测极遥远、极早期宇宙的星系。它还能分析系外行星大气中的水分子、二氧化碳等成分。2021年底发射后，它大幅刷新了我们对早期宇宙的认识。
Webb is the successor to Hubble, with a 6.5-meter mirror observing mainly in infrared, able to see through dust and detect extremely distant, early-universe galaxies. It can also analyze molecules like water and carbon dioxide in exoplanet atmospheres. After its late-2021 launch it greatly reshaped our view of the early universe.
