How do fiat and commodity money differ?

Commodity money (like gold) has intrinsic value; fiat money's value comes from government trust and legal-tender laws rather than the material itself.

#量化宽松 #央行

为什么中央银行能“创造”货币，而普通人和商业银行不能随便印钱？

Why can the central bank "create" money while ordinary people and commercial banks can't just print it?

货币是现代信用的产物。中央银行垄断现金发行并调控基础货币，商业银行则通过贷款“派生”出存款货币——但这一切都建立在法律和监管之上：央行维系人们对货币的信任，银行受准备金率等约束，法律保证货币被普遍接受。如果人人能印钱，货币会因供给失控而快速贬值甚至崩溃。央行创造货币也并非没有边界，它必须维护价格稳定和金融稳定，过度印钞会引发恶性通胀。
Money is a product of modern credit. The central bank monopolizes cash issuance and steers the monetary base, while commercial banks create deposit money through lending; but all of this rests on law and regulation: the central bank sustains trust in the currency, banks face reserve requirements, and law ensures the money is accepted. If everyone could print, the currency would depreciate fast and collapse. Central banks don't create money without limits either; they must defend price and financial stability, since over-issuance brings hyperinflation.

#中央银行 #货币创造 #货币供给

为什么健康的银行也可能被挤兑击垮？存款保险如何改变这一逻辑？

Why can even a sound bank be destroyed by a run, and how does deposit insurance change the logic?

银行用活期存款（可随时提取）支持长期贷款，天然存在期限错配；只要存户同时取款，即使资产优质，银行也得低价抛售资产偿债，造成“自我实现的挤兑”。存款保险通过政府承诺覆盖一定额度的存款，消除存户的挤兑动机，从而稳定体系——这正是美国大萧条后设立FDIC的原因。但保险削弱了存户监督，银行可能更激进，产生道德风险，因此需要配套监管（资本要求、监管审查）和限额赔付。制度设计始终在稳定与激励之间权衡。
Banks fund long-term loans with demand deposits that can be withdrawn anytime—an inherent maturity mismatch; if depositors all withdraw at once, even a solvent bank must fire-sell assets, creating a self-fulfilling run. Deposit insurance removes the incentive to run by government guarantee of deposits up to a limit—which is why the US created the FDIC after the Great Depression. But insurance weakens depositor monitoring, inviting riskier behavior—moral hazard—so it must be paired with capital requirements, supervision, and payout limits. Institutional design is a perpetual trade-off between stability and incentives.

#银行挤兑 #存款保险 #道德风险

2008年全球金融危机中，“次级抵押贷款→证券化→影子银行”链条是如何放大冲击的？

In the 2008 global financial crisis, how did the chain “subprime mortgages → securitization → shadow banking” amplify the shock?

低利率与房价上涨催生次级贷款激增，银行把房贷打包成MBS/CDO出售以转移风险并回收资金继续放贷；证券化使风险层层叠加，评级被高估，投资者与机构用短期回购融资持有这些证券，杠杆高企。当房价见顶回落、违约率上升，证券价值暴跌，依赖短期融资的影子银行体系遭遇挤兑式去杠杆，被迫抛售资产，价格螺旋下跌；雷曼破产引发对交易对手风险的恐慌，信贷冻结，危机从金融蔓延到实体。监管缺位——低资本要求、表外工具、对评级机构的依赖——是危机放大的制度根源。
Low rates and rising house prices fueled a subprime surge; banks bundled mortgages into MBS/CDOs to offload risk and recycle funds into more lending. Securitization stacked risks and inflated ratings; investors and institutions held these securities funded by short-term repos at high leverage. When prices peaked and fell, defaults rose, securities collapsed, and the shadow-banking system dependent on short funding faced a run-like deleveraging—forced selling created a price spiral. Lehman's failure ignited counterparty panic, credit froze, and the crisis spread from finance to the real economy. Regulatory gaps—low capital requirements, off-balance-sheet vehicles, reliance on credit-rating agencies—were the institutional amplifiers.

什么是“影子银行”？为什么它既补充又威胁传统银行体系？

What is “shadow banking,” and why does it both complement and threaten the traditional banking system?

影子银行指在传统受监管银行之外从事期限转换、流动性转换和信用中介的机构与活动，如货币市场基金、对冲基金、资产证券化渠道、P2P借贷平台。它们能绕过资本充足率等监管、提供更便宜的融资，使储蓄流向更高风险高收益的渠道，补充信贷供给；但期限错配（用短期资金投长期资产）使其在恐慌时极易遭到挤兑式赎回，风险可能蔓延到正规银行，2008年危机中证券化链条就是导火索之一。
Shadow banking refers to institutions and activities that perform maturity, liquidity, and credit intermediation outside the regulated banking system—money-market funds, hedge funds, securitization conduits, and peer-to-peer lending. They bypass capital-requirement rules, offer cheaper funding, and channel savings into riskier, higher-return uses, complementing credit supply; but maturity mismatch (funding long assets with short money) makes them vulnerable to runs in a panic, and stress can spill over to regulated banks—the securitization chain was a trigger in 2008.

物价上涨的时候，为什么说“钱不值钱了”？

When prices rise, why do we say "money is worth less"?

货币的价值主要体现在购买力——同样一笔钱能买到多少东西。通货膨胀让同样的钱买到的东西变少，购买力下降，所以“钱不值钱了”。存款和固定工资不会自动跟着物价涨，实际购买力就被悄悄侵蚀，这也是人们关注通胀率、并在通胀高企时设法保值的原因。
Money's value shows mainly in purchasing power, how much a given sum can buy. Inflation makes that same sum buy less, so purchasing power falls and money is worth less. Savings and fixed salaries don't automatically track prices, so real purchasing power is quietly eroded, which is why people watch inflation and look for ways to preserve value when it runs high.

什么是购买力平价？为什么它能解释部分汇率变动却又常常失效？

What is purchasing power parity, and why does it explain some exchange-rate movements yet often fail?

购买力平价（PPP）认为，同一篮子商品在不同国家的价格经汇率换算后应趋同，因为套利者会在价低处买、价高处卖（一价定律）。长期看，通胀较高国家的货币倾向贬值，这一方向大致成立。但它假定商品可自由交易，而服务业、住房等不可贸易品价格差异大，加上贸易壁垒与运输成本，使汇率在短期内大幅偏离PPP水平。
Purchasing power parity (PPP) holds that the same basket of goods should cost the same across countries once converted at the exchange rate, because arbitrageurs buy low and sell high (the law of one price). Over the long run, high-inflation currencies tend to depreciate, so the direction broadly holds. But PPP assumes goods are freely tradable; services, housing, and other non-tradables differ hugely in price, and trade barriers and transport costs push exchange rates far from PPP in the short run.

“系统性风险”与普通风险有何不同？为什么单家银行的稳健不能保证整个体系的稳健？

How does systemic risk differ from ordinary risk, and why doesn't each bank's soundness guarantee the system's soundness?

普通风险是单个机构的损失风险，可由分散化管理；系统性风险是某机构或市场失灵通过传染链条拖垮整个金融体系的危险。银行间借贷、共同资产持有、支付链和信心外溢使风险相互缠绕——单个银行看似稳健，但若所有银行同时依赖同一类融资或资产，集体上就是脆弱的。危机时，每个机构“抛售资产自保”的理性行为反而压低资产价格、恶化彼此处境。因此宏观审慎监管（压力测试、逆周期资本、杠杆上限）着眼于体系而非单家机构。
Ordinary risk is loss risk at a single institution, manageable through diversification; systemic risk is the danger that one institution's or market's failure topples the whole system through contagion. Interbank lending, common asset holdings, payment chains, and confidence spillovers entangle risks—each bank may look sound, yet if all rely on the same funding or assets, they are collectively fragile. Also, in a crisis each institution rationally selling assets to protect itself pushes prices down and worsens everyone's position. Macroprudential regulation (stress tests, countercyclical capital, leverage limits) therefore targets the system, not individual firms.

What is inflation — why did fifty-cent popsicles cost three yuan now?

温和的通胀是经济的常态:货币总量增加、工资物价螺旋上升,钱慢慢变得不那么值钱。适度通胀激励人们消费投资,但恶性通胀(如历史上某些国家物价一年翻百倍)会摧毁经济秩序。你的压岁钱如果一直存着不动,购买力其实在被通胀悄悄稀释——这也是理财教育的第一课:让钱保值增值。
Mild inflation is economic normal: money supply grows, wages and prices spiral gently, cash slowly buys less. Moderate inflation encourages spending and investing, but hyperinflation (prices multiplying hundredfold yearly in some histories) wrecks order. New Year money parked idle quietly loses purchasing power — lesson one of financial literacy: make money hold or grow value.

钱为什么会从手机里付出去?明明没有看到硬币呀?

你手机里存的是一串数字账本:付款时,手机告诉银行把我的10元转到商店,银行核对后改两行数字——你的少10,商店的多10。钱没有飞走,只是账本换了页。所以要看管好手机密码,它就是账本的钥匙。
Your phone keeps a digital ledger: paying tells the bank move ten yuan from me to the shop; the bank checks then edits two lines — yours minus ten, theirs plus ten. Money never flies away; only ledger lines change. Guard your password well — it is the ledger key.
