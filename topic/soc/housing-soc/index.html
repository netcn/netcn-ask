为什么社会学常说住房同时是资产和居所？这种双重属性如何解释住房政策的内在矛盾？

Why do sociologists say housing is both an asset and a home, and how does this dual character explain the tensions built into housing policy?

作为资产，住房承载财富积累、代际转移与信贷抵押功能；作为居所，它是基本需要的满足物和归属感的物质基础。金融化使越来越多主体把住房当作收益来源，房价上涨因此同时意味着有房者增值和无房者被排斥。政府既要维护业主的资产价值，又要保障居住权利，两种目标难以兼得，政策便在供给补贴、租金管制与社会住宅之间反复摇摆。
As an asset, housing carries wealth accumulation, intergenerational transfer, and collateral functions; as a home, it meets basic needs and grounds belonging. Financialization turns housing ever more into a revenue stream, so price growth simultaneously enriches owners and excludes non-owners. States must protect owners' asset values while securing the right to dwell, two goals that conflict, which is why policy oscillates among supply subsidies, rent regulation, and social housing.

#住房 #金融化

为什么说住房不仅是一件商品,更是社会分层的重要机制?“住房阶级”概念揭示了什么?

Why is housing more than a commodity and a key mechanism of social stratification, and what does the concept of 'housing class' reveal?

住房兼具消费属性与财富属性,能否取得产权住房直接影响家庭的资产积累、子女教育机会乃至婚姻达成,因而成为阶层位置再生产的关键环节。雷克斯与摩尔提出住房阶级概念,指出按居住权属划分的自置业主、私房租户与公房租户在获取城市资源时境遇悬殊,形成近似阶级的对立。在中国,从单位分配到商品房的转变使住房成为市场转型时期不平等扩大最显著的渠道之一。
Housing fuses consumption with wealth: access to owner-occupied property shapes a family's asset accumulation, children's schooling chances, even marriage prospects, making it central to the reproduction of class position. Rex and Moore's idea of housing classes shows that owners, private renters, and public-housing tenants face starkly unequal access to urban resources, generating quasi-class antagonisms. In China, the move from work-unit allocation to commodity housing turned housing into one of the sharpest channels through which market transition widened inequality.

#住房阶级 #社会分层

雷克斯与摩尔提出“住房阶级”想说明什么?这个概念与传统阶级分析的关键差别在哪里?

What did Rex and Moore intend to show with their notion of housing classes, and how does it differ from conventional class analysis?

在对伯明翰斯帕克布鲁克社区的实地研究中,他们发现获取住房的不同途径——自有产权、市政公房、私人租赁——造就了利益与流动机会迥异的准群体。能否购房、能否排到公房决定了人们在围绕住房资源冲突中的位置,居住身份本身就构成一条分界线。这把消费领域的不平等引入了阶级分析,而不再只盯着生产场所的雇佣关系。
In their fieldwork on the Sparkbrook district of Birmingham, they found that different routes into housing—owner occupation, council tenancy, private rental—produced quasi-groups with sharply divergent interests and mobility chances. Access to purchase or to the waiting list for public housing positioned people differently in conflicts over housing resources, so residential status itself marked a dividing line. The concept brought consumption-side inequality into class analysis instead of focusing solely on employment relations at the point of production.
