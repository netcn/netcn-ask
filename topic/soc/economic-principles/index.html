什么是“黏性价格”？

What are "sticky prices"?

黏性价格指价格对供需变化反应迟缓，因菜单成本、合同与习惯难以即时调整。它解释为何货币政策短期能影响产出而非仅改变价格。新凯恩斯主义将其作为市场不完全的核心假设。
Sticky prices adjust slowly to supply-demand shifts due to menu costs, contracts, and habits. They explain why monetary policy affects output in the short run rather than only prices. New Keynesianism makes this the core assumption of market imperfection.

为什么会出现“吉芬商品”？

Why do "Giffen goods" exist?

吉芬商品是价格上升需求量反而增加的罕见商品，出现在极贫群体中：当主食涨价，实际收入骤降，人们被迫放弃肉菜、买更多廉价主食充饥，收入效应压倒替代效应。它违背一般需求定律，是理论上的特例。
A Giffen good is a rare case where higher price raises quantity demanded, seen among the very poor: when staple food prices rise, real income drops so sharply that people abandon meat and buy more cheap staples, the income effect overpowering the substitution effect. It violates the usual law of demand as a theoretical exception.

为什么中央银行上调利率能帮助压低通货膨胀？

Why does a central bank's interest-rate hike help bring inflation down?

利率上升意味着借贷更贵：企业和家庭减少借款与投资，买房买车办企业都变难变贵，总需求随之降温，物价上涨的压力随之减小。同时利率升高让储蓄更有吸引力，消费也会被抑制。这就是货币政策的传导机制，央行借此在控通胀与保增长之间权衡。但效果有滞后，加息过猛也可能把经济推向衰退。
Higher rates make borrowing costlier: firms and households borrow and invest less, big purchases become harder and dearer, aggregate demand cools, and price pressure eases. Higher rates also make saving more attractive, dampening consumption. This is the transmission mechanism of monetary policy, used to balance inflation control against growth. Effects lag, and over-tightening can tip the economy into recession.

为什么“助推”常常比强制规定更有效？

Why is a "nudge" often more effective than a mandate?

行为经济学发现人并不总按理性计算决策，而是深受默认选项、框架和惰性的影响。助推通过优化“选择架构”来引导行为，同时保留选择自由：比如把养老金设为默认参加，参与率会大幅上升，因为多数人懒得主动退出。强制则成本高、易引发抵触。助推顺应人性而非对抗人性，但也有人批评它带有“家长式操纵”，所以好的助推应当透明且允许退出。
Behavioral economics shows people don't always decide rationally; they're swayed by defaults, framing, and inertia. A nudge reshapes the choice architecture to guide behavior while preserving freedom of choice, and automatically enrolling workers in pension plans, for instance, lifts participation sharply because few bother to opt out. Mandates are costlier and breed resistance. Nudges work with human nature, though critics call them paternalistic, so good ones stay transparent and easy to exit.

为什么有些国家会出现恶性通货膨胀？

Why do some countries experience hyperinflation?

恶性通胀（月率超50%）多源于政府财政失控、靠印钞还债，叠加民众对货币失去信任的“预期螺旋”。一战后的德国与津巴布韦是典型案例。恢复需财政整顿与独立央行重建信任。
Hyperinflation (monthly rate above 50%) mostly stems from runaway fiscal deficits financed by printing money, plus an “expectation spiral” as public trust in the currency collapses. Post-WWI Germany and Zimbabwe are classic cases. Recovery needs fiscal repair and an independent central bank to rebuild trust.

为什么过去几十年全球极端贫困人口大幅减少？

Why has extreme poverty fallen so dramatically worldwide in recent decades?

最主要的原因是持续的经济增长，尤其是东亚和南亚，它带动了就业和收入提高；贸易开放、农业技术改良、基础设施投入，以及健康和教育普及也在同步发力。世界银行以每天生活费约2.15美元界定极端贫困，全球极端贫困率已从1990年的约38%降到近年的约9%，成绩主要来自中国、印度等国的大规模减贫。但这一进程并不均匀：撒哈拉以南非洲仍是难点，疫情和冲突还曾让全球减贫一度倒退，说明减贫既靠增长，也要靠社会保障和公平分配。
The dominant driver is sustained economic growth, especially in East and South Asia, which raised jobs and incomes, reinforced by open trade, better farming, infrastructure, and expanded health and education. Using the World Bank's extreme-poverty line of about 2.15 U.S. dollars a day, the global rate fell from roughly 38 percent in 1990 to about 9 percent recently, powered largely by large-scale poverty reduction in China and India. Progress is uneven, though: sub-Saharan Africa remains the hardest region, and pandemics and conflict set poverty reduction back, showing that both growth and social protection matter.

通货膨胀（物价普遍上涨）的成因主要有哪些？

What are the main causes of inflation?

主流经济学把成因分成两类：总需求超过总供给的“需求拉动”，以及原材料、工资上涨推高成本的“成本推动”。而在长期，货币供应增长过快是持续通胀的根本原因，弗里德曼因此说“通胀在任何时候任何地方都是一种货币现象”。预期也很关键：一旦人们预期物价要涨，就会提前消费、要求加工资，通胀便自我实现。温和通胀（约2%）被视为健康，恶性通胀则会摧毁储蓄和信用。
Mainstream economics splits the causes into demand-pull, where aggregate demand outstrips supply, and cost-push, where rising input costs and wages push prices up. In the long run, however, rapid money-supply growth is the fundamental driver, which is why Milton Friedman called inflation always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon. Expectations matter too: if people expect prices to rise, they spend early and demand raises, making inflation self-fulfilling. Mild inflation around 2 percent is considered healthy, while hyperinflation destroys savings and trust.

什么是量化宽松？

What is quantitative easing (QE)?

量化宽松是央行在利率近零时，直接购买国债等资产向市场注入流动性，以压低长端利率、刺激借贷与投资。它是非常规货币政策。过度使用可能推高资产泡沫与贫富差距。
Quantitative easing is when a central bank, with rates near zero, directly buys government bonds and other assets to inject liquidity, lowering long-term rates and stimulating borrowing and investment. It is unconventional monetary policy. Overuse can inflate asset bubbles and inequality.

为什么同一国家内部，不同地区的发展水平会差很多？

Why do regions within one country develop so unevenly?

区域差距来自地理区位（港口、资源）、产业集聚、历史政策布局和人力资本等因素的叠加。经济地理学强调“集聚效应”：企业和人才集中在少数城市形成正反馈，越集聚越有吸引力；而欠发达地区常常陷入产业单一、人才外流、设施不足的循环。政策可以通过转移支付、基础设施投资和培育本地产业来缩小差距，但完全拉平既不现实也未必高效——关键是在整体增长中让各地区的居民都能分享机会。
Regional gaps come from compounding factors: geography such as ports and resources, industrial agglomeration, historical policy, and human capital. Economic geography stresses agglomeration: firms and talent cluster in a few cities in a self-reinforcing loop, while lagging regions often get stuck in a cycle of single industries, out-migration, and weak infrastructure. Policy can narrow the gap through transfers, infrastructure, and developing local industries, but full equalization is neither realistic nor necessarily efficient; the key is letting people in all regions share opportunity amid overall growth.

为什么已经花掉的钱（沉没成本）不该影响之后的决定，可人常常做不到？

Why shouldn't sunk costs affect later decisions, and why do people struggle to ignore them?

沉没成本是已发生且无法收回的支出。理性决策只应比较未来的收益与成本，因为过去的钱无论如何都拿不回来。但人们出于“不想浪费”和“不愿承认失败”的心理，会继续给已无望的项目投入，这就是“沉没成本谬误”。它源于损失厌恶和维持自我形象的需要，理解了它才能及时止损。
Sunk costs are expenses already paid that can't be recovered. Rational choice compares only future benefits and costs, since past money is gone either way. Yet out of a wish not to waste and a refusal to admit failure, people keep investing in doomed projects, the sunk cost fallacy, rooted in loss aversion and self-image. Knowing it helps you cut losses.

为什么一个国家明明能自己生产的商品，还要从别国进口？

Why does a country import goods it could produce itself?

关键是比较优势而非绝对优势：即使某国生产所有商品都更高效，它也应当专攻机会成本最低的领域，再用产出交换其余商品。这样两国总产量和双方福利都会更大。比如美国仍进口服装，因为把劳动用在芯片和金融等高附加值领域更划算。这正是自由贸易能够互惠的原因，尽管转型期会带来产业调整和就业流失等代价。
The key is comparative advantage, not absolute advantage: even if one country is more efficient at everything, it should specialize where its opportunity cost is lowest and trade for the rest. Total output and welfare rise on both sides. The U.S. still imports clothing because its labor earns more in chips and finance. That's why free trade can be mutually beneficial, even though adjustment brings industry shifts and job losses.

什么是“汇率操纵”指控？

What is a “currency manipulation” accusation?

一国若长期干预外汇市场压低本币以提振出口，可能被他国指控“汇率操纵”。它通过贸易顺差积累储备、扭曲竞争。IMF与贸易伙伴会据此施压，但它与正常货币政策的边界常存争议。
When a country persistently intervenes to depress its currency and boost exports, others may accuse it of “currency manipulation.” It accumulates reserves via trade surplus and distorts competition. The IMF and trade partners pressure such behavior, though the line from normal monetary policy is contested.

鲍莫尔「成本病」如何解释服务业价格持续上涨？

How does Baumol's 'cost disease' explain the persistent rise in service-sector prices?

鲍莫尔成本病指：当制造业因技术进步而生产率快速提升时，理发、演出等难以自动化的服务业生产率停滞，却须支付同等工资，导致单位成本上升、价格相对变贵。该理论由鲍莫尔与鲍恩在 1966 年提出，揭示了「不平衡增长」的结构性来源。
Baumol's cost disease notes that as manufacturing productivity rises quickly through technology, hard-to-automate services like haircuts or live performances see stagnant productivity yet must pay comparable wages, pushing up their unit costs and relative prices. Baumol and Bowen proposed this in 1966, exposing a structural source of 'unbalanced growth'.

联合国“可持续发展目标”（SDGs）包含什么？为什么要设立这些目标？

What do the UN Sustainable Development Goals cover, and why were they created?

SDGs是2015年联合国通过、计划2030年实现的17项目标，涵盖消除贫困与饥饿、优质教育、性别平等、清洁水与卫生、气候行动等，每项目标下还有具体可衡量的指标。它们代表国际共识：发展不能只看经济数字，必须同时兼顾社会公平和环境可持续，不能让一部分人的进步以另一部分人或子孙后代的损失为代价。各国据此制定政策、跟踪进度并定期评估。
The SDGs are 17 goals adopted by the UN in 2015 for achievement by 2030, covering no poverty, zero hunger, quality education, gender equality, clean water and sanitation, climate action, and more, each with concrete measurable targets. They embody an international consensus that development is more than economic figures; it must balance social fairness with environmental sustainability, without letting some people's progress come at others' or future generations' expense. Countries use them to set policy, track progress, and review performance.

疫苗让接种者获得免疫力，同时降低病原体在人群中传播的概率，周围人也因此受益，这就是“正外部性”——你的行为给别人带来了额外的好处。因为个人决策时不会把自己带给别人的好处算进去，单靠市场时接种率会偏低，所以政府常通过补贴、免费接种甚至强制政策来提高覆盖率，直到形成“群体免疫”。
Vaccination gives you immunity and also lowers how easily the pathogen spreads to others, a positive externality, where your action benefits people beyond yourself. Since individuals don't count the benefits they confer on others when deciding, market outcomes leave vaccination too low, which is why governments subsidize or mandate it to reach herd immunity.

What is economy of scale?

规模经济指产量扩大时平均成本下降的现象，源于固定成本摊薄与专业化分工。汽车制造、芯片生产都依赖大规模生产降低成本。但规模过大也可能出现“规模不经济”。
Economies of scale occur when average cost falls as output expands, due to spreading fixed costs and specialization. Car manufacturing and chip production rely on large-scale output to cut costs. But oversized scale can lead to “diseconomies of scale.”

为什么会出现通货膨胀？

Why does inflation occur?

通货膨胀是整体物价水平持续上升、货币购买力下降。主因包括需求过热、成本推动（如能源涨价）与货币超发。温和通胀常见，恶性通胀则破坏经济秩序。

什么是供需定律？

What is the law of supply and demand?

供需定律指在其他条件不变时，价格上升需求量减少、供给量增加；价格下降则反之。二者交点决定市场均衡价与量。它是经济学理解价格形成的起点。
The law of supply and demand says that, other things equal, a higher price reduces quantity demanded and raises quantity supplied, and vice versa. Their intersection sets the market equilibrium price and quantity. It is the starting point for understanding price formation.

What are trade surplus and trade deficit?

贸易顺差指出口大于进口，逆差则相反。顺差意味对外净卖出、积累外汇；逆差则需用资本流入或储备弥补。长期大额逆差可能暴露竞争力问题。

什么是“买方市场”与“卖方市场”？

What are a "buyer's market" and a "seller's market"?

买方市场是供过于求、买家占优势、价格偏软的市场；卖方市场则供不应求、卖家占优、价格看涨。它是供需力量对比的直观体现。房地产旺季常呈卖方市场。
A buyer's market is one with excess supply where buyers hold the advantage and prices soften; a seller's market has excess demand where sellers dominate and prices rise. It reflects the balance of supply and demand. Hot real-estate seasons are often seller's markets.

什么是“看不见的手”？

What is the “invisible hand”?

“看不见的手”是亚当·斯密提出的概念，指个人在追求自身利益时，通过市场交换无意间促进了社会整体福利。价格信号引导资源流向最需要的地方。它说明自由市场能在没有中央指令的情况下实现有效配置。
The “invisible hand” is a concept from Adam Smith, meaning that individuals pursuing their own interest unintentionally promote overall social welfare through market exchange. Price signals guide resources to where they are most needed. It shows that free markets can achieve efficient allocation without central commands.

What is fiat money?

什么是失业？

What is unemployment?

失业指有劳动能力且积极找工作的人暂时没有工作的状态，失业率是其占总劳动力的比例。摩擦性、结构与周期性失业成因不同。适度失业被视为经济调整的正常摩擦。
Unemployment is the state of able, actively job-seeking people temporarily without work; the unemployment rate is their share of the labor force. Frictional, structural, and cyclical unemployment differ in cause. Some unemployment is seen as normal adjustment friction.

为什么钻石比水贵？

Why is a diamond more expensive than water?

这被称为“价值悖论”。水的用途大但供给充足，边际效用低；钻石用途小却稀缺，边际效用高。价格由边际效用和稀缺性决定，而非总效用。所以稀缺的钻石反而更贵。
This is called the “paradox of value.” Water is highly useful but abundant, so its marginal utility is low; diamonds are less useful but scarce, so their marginal utility is high. Price is set by marginal utility and scarcity, not total utility—so scarce diamonds cost more.

什么是沉没成本？

What is a sunk cost?

沉没成本是指已经发生且无法收回的支出，比如已买的电影票。理性决策只应考虑未来的成本和收益，而不该被沉没成本绑架。所谓“及时止损”就是忽略沉没成本。
A sunk cost is an expense that has already been incurred and cannot be recovered, such as a movie ticket already bought. Rational decisions should weigh only future costs and benefits, not be held hostage by sunk costs. “Cutting losses in time” means ignoring sunk costs.
