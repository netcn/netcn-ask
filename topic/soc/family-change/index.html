工业革命之后三代同堂渐少而夫妻加子女的小家庭普及，帕森斯怎样解释这场家庭结构转型？

After industrialization the large stem household faded while the nuclear family spread—how did Talcott Parsons explain this transformation?

用结构分化来解释：工业化迫使家庭交出大部分传统功能，小家庭反而最适应新环境。机理：生产移入工厂，教育移交学校，济贫转给政府，庞大的亲属共同体因功能被剥离而解体；家庭仅保留儿童初级社会化与成年人情感支持两项核心职能，而规模小、易流动的核心家庭恰好能追随劳动力市场的岗位迁徙。所以在帕森斯看来，核心家庭普及不是家庭衰败，而是对工业社会的功能适应。
Through structural differentiation: industrialization stripped the family of most inherited functions, and the small household proved best fitted to what remained. Production moved into factories, schooling into schools, relief into government agencies, so the large kinship community dissolved once its functions were taken away. The family kept two core tasks—primary socialization of children and emotional stabilization of adults—and a small, mobile nuclear unit could follow jobs through the labor market. Hence for Parsons the spread of the nuclear family was not decay but functional adaptation to industrial society.

夫妻两人都有全职工作，为什么下班后的家务和照看孩子仍然主要落在母亲身上？

When both spouses work full-time, why does the housework and childcare after hours still land mainly on mothers?

因为性别观念的变化赶不上就业结构的变化，霍克希尔德把这称为停滞的革命。机理：女性大量进入职场之后，家务分工背后的文化剧本依然陈旧，男主外女主内的默认继续运转；不少妻子为照顾丈夫的自我感受而主动放低标准，丈夫则以帮忙的身份做选择性的投入。追踪若干双职工家庭后她估算，职业母亲每年相当于多做约一个月的全天家务——这就是第二轮班，其代价是长期的疲惫与积怨。
Gender beliefs lag behind employment structures—what Arlie Hochschild called a stalled revolution. Women entered paid work en masse, but the cultural script governing housework stayed old, and the default of men outside, women inside kept running. Many wives lowered their own standards to spare their husband's feelings, while husbands chipped in selectively as helpers rather than partners. Following dual-earner couples over time, she reckoned that employed mothers did roughly an extra month of round-the-clock housework a year—the second shift—paid for in chronic fatigue and simmering resentment.

#第二轮班 #性别分工 #停滞的革命

霍克希尔德注意到不少全职上班的妻子下班回家后仍要独自包揽家务育儿，她将这种现象命名为什么？

Noticing that many full-time working wives still shouldered all the housework and childcare after coming home, what did Arlie Hochschild name this phenomenon?

命名为第二轮班，指职业女性下班后在家中继续从事的无偿家务与照料劳动。机理：丈夫下班走向休闲，妻子却开始当天的第二份工作，根源在于性别观念落后于就业结构——夫妻带着深浅不一的性别意识形态谈判家务，分工被当成衡量爱与感激的账本；职场又默认家里总有人值守，便利安排多为男性动用，缺口最终由女性的闲暇填补。
She named it the second shift: the unpaid housework and caregiving women performed at home after their paid day ended. Husbands headed for leisure while wives began the day's second job, because gender ideology lagged behind employment structure—spouses bargained over chores armed with deeply held beliefs about proper roles, turning the division of labor into a ledger measuring love and gratitude. Workplaces meanwhile presumed someone was always home, so family-friendly arrangements were mostly claimed by men, and the shortfall was covered out of women's leisure.

美国社会学家切林发现，规范婚姻行为的种种约定越来越失去约束力，他将这一趋势命名为什么？

Noticing that the norms governing how spouses ought to behave were losing their grip, what did Andrew Cherlin name this trend in American family life?

称为婚姻的去制度化。机理：婚姻曾是规定夫妻角色与人生轨道的一整套明确规范——何时结婚、婚后各自承担什么都有章可循；随着同居普及、离婚常态化与非婚生育增多，婚姻从人人必经的制度安排变成个体可选、可自行定义的关系项目，旧的共同行为脚本随之失灵。人们依旧结婚甚至更舍得办婚礼，但婚姻承载的意义已从经济合伙与社会成人礼转向情感陪伴与自我实现。
He named it the deinstitutionalization of marriage. Marriage once carried a clear rulebook for spouses' roles and life courses—when to wed, who owes what afterward. As cohabitation spread, divorce grew routine, and childbearing outside wedlock became common, marriage turned from a near-obligatory institution into an optional relationship project couples define for themselves, and the old shared script lost its force. Americans still marry and even splurge on weddings, yet what marriage signifies has shifted from economic partnership and adult status toward companionship and self-fulfillment.
