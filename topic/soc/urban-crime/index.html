对芝加哥三百多个社区的研究显示，比贫困更能预测暴力犯罪差异的是哪种邻里特质？

In the Chicago neighborhood studies, which trait predicted differences in violent crime better than poverty itself?

是集体效能，即邻里间的社会凝聚力与出面维护公共秩序的共同意愿。机理：芝加哥项目测量了343个社区簇的八千多名居民，在统计上控制贫困率、族裔构成与居住流动之后，居民互识互信、愿意干预青少年越轨的社区，凶杀与暴力犯罪率明显更低；成人共同看护街头少年、及时制止涂鸦与骚扰，使轻微失序无从累积成犯罪文化。可见贫困提供诱因，集体效能则决定诱因能否变成罪行。
Collective efficacy: neighbors' social cohesion combined with their willingness to intervene for public order. The Chicago project measured more than eight thousand residents across 343 neighborhood clusters; statistically controlling poverty rates, ethnic composition, and residential turnover, communities where residents knew and trusted one another and stepped in against teenage misbehavior showed markedly lower homicide and violence. Adults watching the streets together and stopping graffiti and harassment early kept minor disorder from snowballing into a criminal culture. Poverty supplies motives; collective efficacy decides whether motives become offenses.

威尔逊与凯林看到一扇迟迟无人修理的破窗似乎会拖垮整条街的秩序，他们的破窗理论核心主张是什么？

Wilson and Kelling watched a single unrepaired broken window seem to drag a whole street into decline—what is the core claim of their broken-windows theory?

主张轻微失序会一步步酿成严重犯罪，治理必须赶在前头。机理：破窗向路人宣告此处没人关心、没人管事，恐惧令守法居民撤出街头，非正式监控随之松动，潜在越轨者收到纵容信号，街区沿失序螺旋下滑。因此政策重心应是及时修缮环境、制止涂鸦与逃票等小过错，重建居民对秩序可预期的信心，避免零星混乱固化为重罪温床。
Minor disorder, left alone, ripens into serious crime, so policing must act early. A broken window tells passersby that nobody here cares and nobody is in charge; fear pulls law-abiding residents off the street, informal surveillance slackens, and would-be offenders read a license to misbehave as the neighborhood slides down a spiral of decline. Policy should therefore repair the environment promptly and check small offenses like graffiti and fare evasion, rebuilding residents' confidence that order can be expected before scattered mess hardens into fertile ground for felony.

#破窗理论 #失序信号 #非正式监控

在费城的部分内城街区，为什么连本性并不坏的青少年也要摆出一副惹不起的面孔？安德森用什么概念解释这一现象？

In parts of inner-city Philadelphia, why do teenagers—even essentially decent kids—feel compelled to wear a tough, untouchable front? What concept did Elijah Anderson use to explain this?

答案是街头规范：在正式权威失灵的街区，赢得他人敬重本身就是生存装备。机理：警察保护遥远且难以指望，年轻人相信谁显得好欺负谁就会先被挑中，于是靠衣着言谈、眼神姿态乃至先发制人地展示武力来立威，免得沦为软柿子；这套摆面子的规则反过来抬高冲突升级的概率，形成恶性循环。安德森强调多数家庭仍是教子有方的正派家庭，街头规范是恶劣环境逼出的自保策略，而非社区固有的价值观。
Through the code of the street: where formal authority fails, commanding respect becomes survival equipment. Police protection feels distant and unreliable, so teenagers assume that whoever looks easy to prey upon gets picked first; they build deterrence through dress, speech, eye contact, even preemptive shows of force, determined never to be taken for soft targets. Those same face-saving rules raise the odds that minor disputes escalate, locking the block into a vicious cycle. Anderson stresses that most households remain decent ones raising children right—the code is a survival adaptation to desperate conditions, not the community's native values.
