团购和集采为何常能拿到更低价格？

Why can group buying and centralized procurement get lower prices?

团购把分散的小需求聚成大订单，提高对卖方的议价能力并降低单位流通成本。集采进一步以规模压价，但也可能削弱小商户生存空间。
Group buying pools scattered small demands into big orders, raising bargaining power and lowering per-unit logistics cost. Centralized procurement presses prices via scale, though it may squeeze small sellers.

什么是“凡勃伦商品”？它和普通商品的需求规律有何不同？

What is a “Veblen good” and how does its demand differ from ordinary goods?

凡勃伦商品（如奢侈品）价格越高需求反而越大，因为高价本身成为身份与地位的象征。它违背了普通商品“价高需求降”的需求定律。
A Veblen good (e.g., luxury items) sees higher demand as its price rises, because the high price itself signals status. It violates the usual law of demand, where higher prices lower demand.

为什么试吃、试用能提高购买率？

Why do free samples and trials boost purchase rates?

试用降低尝试门槛，并触发“互惠”心理——人们接受小恩惠后更愿意回报以购买。直接体验也比广告更能建立信任。
Trials lower the try barrier and trigger “reciprocity”: after receiving a small favor, people feel inclined to repay by buying. Direct experience also builds more trust than ads.

什么是“计划性报废”？

What is “planned obsolescence”?

指产品在设计中故意缩短使用寿命，促使消费者更快更换，如早期灯泡寿命被限制。它推动消费但也引发资源浪费与环保争议。
It means products are deliberately designed with shorter lifespans to make consumers replace them sooner, as with early light bulbs built to fail. It drives consumption but sparks waste and environmental debate.

什么是“锚定效应”在购物中的表现？

What is the “anchoring effect” in shopping?

锚定效应指人会被最先看到的数字影响判断，例如先见“原价999”再看“现价399”会觉得便宜。商家常用划线价制造参照锚点。
Anchoring means people are swayed by the first number they see—e.g., seeing “was 999” then “now 399” feels cheap. Sellers use struck-through prices to set a reference point.

为什么“限时折扣”会让人更容易下单？

Why do limited-time discounts make people more likely to buy?

限时制造稀缺与紧迫感，触发“损失厌恶”——人们更怕错过优惠而非理性比较价格。商家借此缩短决策时间，减少比价。
Limited-time offers create scarcity and urgency, triggering “loss aversion”: people fear missing the deal more than they rationally compare prices. Sellers use this to shorten decision time and curb comparison.

什么是“从众消费”？它为什么会发生？

What is “herd consumption” and why does it happen?

从众消费指人们因看到他人购买或商家销量数据而跟随购买，而非基于自身需求判断。它源于社会证明心理和不想错过的焦虑（FOMO），在社交媒体时代更明显。
Herd consumption means people buy because others do or because of displayed sales numbers, rather than their own needs. It stems from social-proof psychology and fear of missing out (FOMO), amplified by social media.

为什么要排队，而不是一拥而上？

Why do we queue instead of rushing all at once?

排队是用一种非正式但被广泛接受的规则来分配稀缺资源，降低冲突与混乱。它体现社会秩序与公平预期，违反者会被群体压力约束。
Queuing is an informal but widely accepted rule for allocating scarce resources and reducing conflict and chaos. It reflects social order and a fair expectation, and violators face peer pressure.

为什么很多人喜欢“凑单”满减？

Why do many people chase “spend-more-to-save” thresholds?

满减让人在“省到优惠”的心理下多买本不需要的东西，实际支出反而增加。这是一种门槛促销利用了贪便宜心理。
Threshold discounts push people to buy unneeded items to “unlock” the saving, so actual spending often rises. It exploits a bargain-seeking mindset.

为什么超市常把糖果和口香糖放在收银台附近？

Why do supermarkets often place candy and gum near the checkout?

这是“冲动购买区”策略：顾客结账排队时容易顺手拿低价小商品。收银台是停留时间短但注意力集中的区域，能提升非计划性消费。
This is an “impulse-buy zone” tactic: shoppers waiting in line are likely to grab low-price small items. The checkout is a spot with short dwell time but focused attention, lifting unplanned purchases.
