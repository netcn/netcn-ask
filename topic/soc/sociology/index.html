什么是社会资本？为什么说人脉和社区关系也是一种“资本”？

What is social capital, and why are networks and community ties called "capital"?

社会资本指嵌入在社会网络中的资源——信任、互助规范和熟人关系，个人可以借此获得信息、工作机会和帮助。帕特南把“连接型”社会资本（志愿团体带来的跨圈层关系）和“凝聚型”社会资本（亲戚老乡式的紧密圈子）区分开：前者通常带来新机会，后者利于内部支持。社会资本像社区的粘合剂，影响公共参与和治理质量，但也和财富一样分布不均，网络广而强的人优势更大。
Social capital means the resources embedded in social networks, such as trust, norms of reciprocity, and acquaintance ties, that people draw on for information, jobs, and help. Putnam distinguishes bridging social capital, cross-group ties from groups like volunteer associations, from bonding social capital, tight circles of kin and hometown; bridging usually opens new opportunities while bonding provides internal support. Social capital works like community glue, shaping participation and governance, yet it's as unevenly distributed as wealth, favoring those with broader, stronger networks.

什么是社会流动？

What is social mobility?

社会流动指个人或家庭在社会阶层中的位置发生变化，比如从低收入移动到高收入。向上流动（阶层上升）和向下流动都存在；也可按代际（父母到子女）或一生内来划分。教育机会、公平性、经济结构和制度都会影响一个社会的流动难易程度。
Social mobility is the movement of individuals or families from one social position to another—for example, from low income to high income. Both upward and downward mobility exist, and it can be measured across generations (parent to child) or within a single lifetime. Education access, fairness, economic structure, and institutions all shape how easy mobility is in a society.

Cultural capital, coined by sociologist Pierre Bourdieu, is the intangible assets—tastes, speech, credentials—gained through education and upbringing that give advantages in school and society, helping reproduce class status.

教育为什么既被称为“向上流动的阶梯”，又被批评为“阶层再生产的机器”？

Why is education called a "ladder of upward mobility" and also criticized as a "machine for reproducing class"?

一方面，教育确实提升人力资本和收入，是普通人改变命运的主要通道。另一方面，教育机会和质量的分配并不平等：家庭背景影响学区、补习、课外投入和视野，优势阶层还能通过名校、人脉和“隐性课程”传递优势。布迪厄用“文化资本”解释这种机制——教育看似机会中立，却常把家庭背景的不平等转化成学业和职业的不平等。因此社会学既强调教育的重要性，也提醒人们看清它的双重面孔。
Education genuinely raises human capital and income, the main route for ordinary people to change their destiny. Yet access and quality are unequally distributed: family background shapes school districts, tutoring, extracurriculars, and horizons, and privileged families pass on advantages through elite schools, networks, and the hidden curriculum. Bourdieu's cultural capital explains the mechanism: an apparently neutral system converts family inequality into academic and career inequality. Sociology therefore stresses both education's promise and its double face.

韦伯的「理解」（Verstehen）方法论主张怎样研究社会行动？

What does Weber's 'Verstehen' methodology argue about how to study social action?

韦伯主张社会学应「理解」行动者赋予行为的意义，而非只统计外在行为；研究者要设身处地解释其动机与价值。这一「解释性社会学」路径强调文化与意义，与纯量化的自然科学研究取向形成对照。
Weber held that sociology must 'understand' (Verstehen) the meaning actors attach to their behavior, not merely count outward acts; the researcher should interpret motives and values from the actor's standpoint. This interpretive approach stresses culture and meaning, contrasting with purely quantitative natural-science methods.

学区制如何把居住隔离转化为教育机会不平等？“教育券”能解决吗？

How does the school-district system turn residential segregation into unequal educational opportunity, and can school vouchers solve it?

公立学校主要按学区就近招生，经费多依赖本地房产税，于是高房价社区集中了好学校与高收入家庭，形成“居住选择+学校质量”的循环强化：富裕家庭为学区溢价购房，进一步推高房价、挤出低收入家庭。学校选择政策（教育券、特许学校）旨在让低收入学区家庭“用脚投票”，但实证效果分化：竞争可能改善部分学校绩效，优质学额却常被信息与交通能力更强的家庭占据，可能加剧分层。研究强调真正打破循环要靠跨学区整合、公平拨款与住房去隔离政策的组合。
Public schools admit by catchment area and depend heavily on local property taxes, so high-priced neighborhoods concentrate good schools and affluent families—a self-reinforcing loop of residential choice and school quality: wealthy families pay premiums for good school districts, inflating housing prices and crowding out low-income families. School-choice policies (vouchers, charter schools) aim to let disadvantaged families “vote with their feet,” but evidence is mixed: competition may improve some school performance, yet better-informed and more mobile families capture the best seats, possibly deepening stratification. Research stresses that breaking the loop requires cross-district integration, equitable funding, and housing de-segregation together.

老街区被改造成高档街区（士绅化），为什么有人欢迎有人反对？

When a run-down neighborhood gentrifies, why do some welcome it and others resist?

士绅化指中高收入者迁入老旧街区，带动翻新、商业升级和房价上涨。支持者看到环境改善、治安好转、税收增加；反对者担心原住的低收入居民被房租和房价挤走，社区文化与熟人网络被破坏，即“驱逐性士绅化”。学者普遍认为，问题不在于改造本身，而在于有没有同步提供可负担住房、让原居民分享收益；否则升级就是以本地人的流失为代价。
Gentrification is when middle- and high-income residents move into older districts, driving renovation, upscale commerce, and rising property values. Supporters see better environments, safer streets, and more tax revenue; opponents fear low-income residents are priced out and local culture and networks are destroyed, so-called displacement gentrification. Scholars generally agree the issue isn't redevelopment itself but whether affordable housing and gains reach original residents; without that, upgrading comes at their expense.

Structural functionalism is a major sociological perspective viewing society as an organism whose parts work together to maintain stability, with figures like Parsons and Merton. It emphasizes the functions of institutions and social equilibrium. Critics note it tends to underplay conflict and change.

为什么说疾病不仅是一种生物学事件,也是被社会定义的状态?请用帕森斯的病人角色概念加以说明。

Why is illness not merely a biological event but also a socially defined status? Explain using Parsons' concept of the sick role.

帕森斯在1951年的《疾病的社会功能》一文及其《社会系统》中提出,病人被暂时免除正常社会角色义务,但相应地必须求医并配合治疗以求康复。这一角色的边界由医患互动与社会规范共同划定,不同文化对何为疾病、由谁来照护的规定差别很大。因此疾病的认定与应对深深嵌入职业制度、权力关系和文化期待之中,而非仅由病理决定。
In “The Social Function of Morbidity” (1951) and in The Social System, Talcott Parsons argued that being ill temporarily exempts a person from normal role obligations, provided they seek help and cooperate in recovering. The boundaries of this role are drawn jointly by doctor-patient interaction and prevailing norms, and cultures differ widely over what counts as illness and who should provide care. Recognition of and response to disease are thus embedded in professional institutions, power relations, and cultural expectations rather than dictated by pathology alone.

社会学是研究社会结构、社会关系和社会行为的一门学科，关注个人如何在群体中互动以及社会如何运行。它探讨家庭、教育、阶层、文化等主题。奥古斯特·孔德等人被视为社会学的早期奠基者。
Sociology is the study of social structures, relationships, and behavior, focusing on how individuals interact in groups and how society operates. It examines topics like family, education, class, and culture. Thinkers such as Auguste Comte are regarded as early founders of the discipline.

什么是社会规范？

What are social norms?

社会规范是一个群体中被广泛接受和期望的行为准则，包括习俗、礼仪和禁忌等。它们通过教育、榜样和舆论维持，帮助社会有序运转。违反规范通常会招致批评或排斥，但不一定违法。
Social norms are widely accepted and expected rules of behavior within a group, including customs, etiquette, and taboos. They are maintained through education, example, and public opinion, helping society function orderly. Violating them may bring criticism or exclusion but is not necessarily illegal.

什么是社区？

What is a community?

社区是一群生活在同一地方或拥有共同兴趣、身份的人，他们互相帮助、分享资源，比如小区、学校或同好圈子。
A community is a group of people sharing a place or common interest and identity who help and share with one another, like a neighborhood, school, or hobby group.
