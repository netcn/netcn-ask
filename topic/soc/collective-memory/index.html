哈布瓦赫为什么认为个人记忆依赖“社会框架”？集体记忆与历史有何不同？

Why did Maurice Halbwachs hold that individual memory depends on social frameworks, and how does collective memory differ from history?

哈布瓦赫主张，回忆虽发生在个体头脑之中，其组织方式却来自群体：语言、家庭、宗教与阶级为个人提供定位过去的框架，人们唯有在与他人的交谈和对照中才能稳定地记住事件。因此集体记忆不是历史的忠实存档，而是服务于当下群体需要的持续重构——每个世代都按自身关切重新讲述昨天，纪念仪式与共同叙事正是这种重构的制度化形态。
Maurice Halbwachs argued that although remembering happens inside individual minds, its organization comes from the group: language, family, religion, and class supply the frameworks through which people locate the past, and memories stabilize only in conversation and comparison with others. Collective memory is thus not a faithful archive of history but a continuing reconstruction serving present group needs—each generation retells yesterday according to its own concerns, with commemorations and shared narratives institutionalizing that reconstruction.

为什么社会学认为“文化创伤”并非来自事件本身，而是社会建构的产物？

Why do sociologists argue that “cultural trauma” stems not from events themselves but from social construction?

亚历山大等人于2004年提出，集体是否陷入创伤取决于意义建构而非事件本身：由知识分子组成的承载群体围绕事件性质、受害方与加害方展开宣称竞争，借助媒体、法律与仪式把特定叙事固化为公共记忆。唯有当创伤叙述被广泛接受、群体边界随之重划时，文化创伤才告成立。该理论解释了同一灾难为何在不同社群留下迥异的记忆后果。
As Jeffrey C. Alexander and colleagues argued in 2004, whether a collectivity becomes traumatized depends on meaning-making rather than the raw event. Carrier groups—typically intellectuals—compete in claims about the nature of the event, its victims, and its perpetrators, fixing chosen narratives into public memory through media, law, and ritual. Cultural trauma exists only once such narratives win broad acceptance and group boundaries are redrawn. The theory explains why one catastrophe can leave very different memory consequences across communities.

皮埃尔·诺拉的“记忆之场”（lieux de mémoire）指的是什么？

What does Pierre Nora mean by "sites of memory" (lieux de mémoire)?

诺拉主编的同名多卷本研究认为，当活态记忆环境随传统社会瓦解而消退，共同体便把记忆固定在特定场所：档案馆、纪念碑、周年庆典乃至教科书。因自发口传传承不再可靠，这类场所被刻意营造，用以凝结身份认同，却也常因纪念谁、遗忘谁而起争议。它揭示集体记忆必须依附物质与象征载体方能延续。
Pierre Nora's multi-volume project argues that as living environments of memory dissolved along with traditional society, communities fixed remembrance in particular places: archives, monuments, anniversary celebrations, even textbooks. Because spontaneous transmission could no longer be relied upon, such sites were deliberately created to condense group identity, yet they often become controversial over whom they commemorate and whom they omit. The concept reveals that collective memory persists only by attaching itself to material and symbolic supports.
