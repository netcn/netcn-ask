世界上许多语言正在消失，为什么？该不该挽救它们？

Many languages are dying out. Why, and should we try to save them?

语言随使用者的流失而消亡：经济、教育和媒体的强势语言吸引年轻一代放弃母语，父母不再教孩子少数民族语言，代际传承一旦断裂，语言就走向消亡——如今全球约7000种语言中，许多只剩下几千甚至几百名年长使用者。语言承载着特有的知识（草药、天文、生态经验）和文化记忆，失去往往不可逆。联合国因而把每年2月21日设为国际母语日，鼓励双语教育、语言记录和社区传承；但真正救活一种语言，靠的是让它有用、有尊严，而不是强制。
Languages die as their speakers drift away: economic, educational, and media pressure pulls the young toward dominant languages, parents stop passing the minority tongue on, and once intergenerational transmission breaks, the language fades. Many of the world's roughly 7,000 languages now have only a few thousand or a few hundred elderly speakers. Languages carry unique knowledge, such as herbal, astronomical, and ecological knowledge, and cultural memory, and loss is often irreversible. That's why the UN marks February 21 as International Mother Language Day, promoting bilingual education, documentation, and community transmission; yet a language is really saved by being useful and valued, not by force.

什么是阿里耶斯所说的“被禁止的死亡”？现代社会为何把临终与哀悼推进医院和私人领域？

What does Philippe Aries mean by the forbidden death, and why did modern society move dying and mourning into hospitals and private life?

阿里耶斯的历史研究显示，20世纪中叶的西方出现“被禁止的死亡”：临终从家庭迁入医院，由医学接管；哀悼仪式随之萎缩，谈论死亡成为近乎失礼的禁忌。这与个人主义兴起、生命末端的医疗化以及死亡率下降后公众对死亡的陌生有关——死亡不再是社区公开操演的事件，而被隔离为机构内的技术事务，悲伤也被压缩进私人领域。
Philippe Aries documented how mid-twentieth-century Western societies produced the forbidden death: dying was relocated from the household into hospitals under medical control, mourning rites shrank, and mentioning death came to seem almost indecent. He linked this shift to rising individualism, the medicalization of life's end, and public unfamiliarity with death once mortality fell. Death ceased to be a public event staged by the community, becoming instead a technical matter sequestered inside institutions, while grief was confined to private life.

格拉泽与施特劳斯提出的“觉察情境”指什么？它如何塑造临终病人的照护与沟通？

What are the “awareness contexts” identified by Glaser and Strauss, and how do they shape care and communication with dying patients?

在1965年的医院田野研究中，格拉泽与施特劳斯把医患双方对“病人将死”信息的知晓状态称为觉察情境，区分出封闭、怀疑、相互假装与开放四种类型。情境类型决定谁可谈论死亡、护士如何安排照护、家属怎样沟通。研究由此把临终照护呈现为一套协商而成的互动秩序，成为死亡社会学质性研究的奠基之作。
In hospital fieldwork reported in Awareness of Dying (1965), Barney Glaser and Anselm Strauss called the state of knowledge about approaching death held by patients, families and staff an “awareness context”, distinguishing closed, suspected, mutual pretense and open types. Which context obtains determines who may speak of dying, how nurses organize care and how relatives communicate. The study thus presented end-of-life care as a negotiated interactional order, laying the foundation of qualitative research in the sociology of death.

为什么一个人可以同时认同好几种文化？

Why can one person feel attached to several cultures at the same time?

因为文化认同不是单选题。移民家庭的孩子既说母语也说当地语言，既过传统节日又过当地节日，这些认同可以叠加而非互相排斥。全球化带来的流动性让跨文化成长越来越普遍，研究认为“混合认同”能带来更强的适应性和创造力，但有时也会伴随归属感的拉扯。认同是情境性的，不必强迫自己只选一边。
Because cultural belonging isn't a multiple-choice question. Children of immigrant families speak both languages and celebrate both sets of holidays; identities stack rather than exclude. Growing mobility makes cross-cultural lives common, and research suggests hybrid identity can bring adaptability and creativity, though sometimes with a pull of belonging. Identity is contextual; there's no need to force a single choice.

传统习俗与现代化生活一定冲突吗？

Do traditional customs and modern life have to conflict?

不一定。社会学研究发现，许多传统能在现代化中存续并自我更新：传统节日与商业结合，传统服饰进入时尚设计，民俗成为旅游和文创资源。冲突确实存在——当某些习俗与平等、个体权利等现代价值相抵触时就会引发争议。社会通常通过“创造性转化”重新诠释传统，让它在新条件下仍有意义。
Not necessarily. Sociological research finds many traditions survive and renew themselves amid modernization: festivals merge with commerce, folk dress enters fashion, and customs become tourism and cultural-creative resources. Real conflicts do occur when customs clash with modern values like equality and individual rights. Societies usually creatively transform traditions, reinterpreting them so they stay meaningful under new conditions.

人的“身份认同”是怎么形成的？

How does a person's "identity" take shape?

身份认同回答“我是谁”，包含国籍、民族、性别、职业、信仰等许多维度。它由家庭和社会化过程塑造——语言、习俗、教育和共同经历让我们把自己归入某些群体，他人的看法也影响自我认知。身份并不是单一固定的，而是多重的、动态的，在不同场合会凸显出不同的面向。
Identity answers the question of who I am, spanning nationality, ethnicity, gender, occupation, faith, and more. It's shaped by family and socialization: language, customs, education, and shared experience place us in groups, while others' perceptions shape our self-image. Identity isn't single and fixed; it's multiple and dynamic, with different facets coming to the fore in different settings.

文化全球化是让世界变得更相似，还是更多样？

Does cultural globalization make the world more similar or more diverse?

两股力量同时在发生。好莱坞电影、流行音乐、快餐和全球品牌让各国年轻一代共享大量相同符号，这是“同质化”的一面；但不同地区会在吸收外来文化时进行本地化改造，比如把西式食材做成当地风味，同时地方文化借助网络获得全球观众，文化组合反而更加多样。学者常用“混合化”来描述这种既有趋同又有创新的复杂过程。
Both forces are at work at once. Hollywood films, pop music, fast food, and global brands give young people everywhere many shared symbols, the homogenizing side. But regions adapt imported culture locally, such as turning Western ingredients into local dishes, and local cultures gain global audiences online, so cultural combinations multiply. Scholars describe this as hybridization: convergence and creativity intertwined.

Why do so many places have traditions of storytelling and song?

为什么同一个国家里还有方言？

Why are there dialects within one country?

山川阻隔和交通不便让各地的人长期少来往，说话的腔调和用词就各自变化。这些方言保留着当地的历史和生活味道，和通用语可以同时使用。
Mountains, rivers, and poor transport kept people in different areas apart for a long time, so their accents and word choices drifted in their own directions. These dialects hold local history and flavour, and can be used alongside a common language.

为什么各地的房子样子不一样？

Why do houses look different in different places?

房子要先对付当地的天气，多雨的地方屋顶很斜，好让雨水流走；很热的地方墙厚窗小，屋里才凉快。人们还会就近取材，用石头、木头或泥土来建。
A house must first cope with the local weather, so rainy places have steep roofs that let water run off, while very hot places have thick walls and small windows to stay cool. People also build with what is nearby, such as stone, wood, or earth.

#建筑 #气候

为什么有些节日的日期每年都变？

Why do some festivals fall on a different date each year?

这些节日按月亮的圆缺来算日子，也就是农历或类似的历法。月相周期和太阳年的长度不一样，换算到常用日历上，日期自然每年移动。
Those festivals are counted by the phases of the moon, using a lunar calendar or something similar. A cycle of moon phases does not match the length of a solar year, so the date shifts when converted to the everyday calendar.

Why do people in different places speak different languages?

以前人们走得不远，各地的人常年只和身边的人说话，说法就慢慢变得不一样。时间久了，就形成了各自的语言。
In the past people did not travel far, so each group mostly talked with those nearby and their way of speaking slowly drifted apart. Over a long time, separate languages formed.

为什么博物馆要收藏很旧的东西？

Why do museums collect very old objects?

旧物件是过去生活留下的实证，能告诉我们祖先怎么耕作、穿衣和交流。把它们妥善保存下来，后人才能亲眼看到历史，而不只是听说。
Old objects are physical evidence of past lives, showing how ancestors farmed, dressed, and communicated. Preserving them properly lets later generations see history with their own eyes rather than only hear about it.

为什么会有节日？

Why do festivals exist?

节日常常来自值得记住的事，比如丰收、季节变换或重要的历史故事。固定的日子让一代代人都记得这件事，也有机会团聚。
Festivals often come from something worth remembering, such as a harvest, a change of season, or an important story from history. A fixed date helps each generation remember it and gives people a chance to gather.
