什么是“监管俘获”？为什么监管者常保护被监管行业而非公众？

What is regulatory capture, and why do regulators often end up protecting the industries they regulate?

监管俘获指监管机构被其监管对象左右，出台的政策服务于行业利益而非公共利益。机制包括：行业信息不对称（监管者依赖企业提供技术数据）、“旋转门”（人员往来于监管部门与企业）、游说与竞选资金，以及“利益集中、成本分散”的结构——被监管企业数量少、利益集中、有强激励组织起来，而公众获益分散、组织成本高、关注度低。斯提格勒的规制经济理论甚至提出规制本身可能被行业“采购”。防范靠透明度、利益冲突规则、独立预算与公民参与。
Regulatory capture occurs when regulators are steered by those they regulate, serving industry rather than public interests. Mechanisms include information asymmetry (regulators depend on firms for technical data), revolving doors (staff moving between agency and industry), lobbying and campaign finance, and the “concentrated benefits, dispersed costs” structure—regulated firms are few, benefit intensely, and organize cheaply, while the public's gains are diffuse, costly to organize, and low-salience. Stigler's economic theory of regulation even argues regulation itself can be “purchased” by industry. Safeguards include transparency, conflict-of-interest rules, independent budgets, and public participation.

为什么各地方政府或国家会接连采纳相似政策？“政策扩散”有哪些机制？

Why do subnational governments or countries adopt similar policies in waves? What mechanisms drive policy diffusion?

政策扩散指一项政策从一地传播到其他地区的过程，主要机制有四：学习（借鉴他处经验以提高自身效果）、竞争（为吸引资本与人才而追随邻区政策，如减税）、模仿（因合法性与声望效仿“标杆”地区）和强制或外部压力（上级政府或国际组织推动）。扩散不是简单的“抄作业”，采纳与否取决于本地政治、财政与利益格局；扩散可以加速（成功案例被宣传），也可能失败（水土不服）。这一框架被用于解释最低工资、碳税、禁烟令等政策的跨国传播。
Policy diffusion is a policy's spread from one jurisdiction to others, driven by four main mechanisms: learning (borrowing experiences to improve outcomes), competition (following neighbors to attract capital and talent, e.g., tax cuts), imitation (emulating benchmark regions for legitimacy and prestige), and coercion or external pressure (higher-level governments or international organizations). Diffusion is not simple copying—adoption depends on local politics, fiscal capacity, and interest groups; it can accelerate when success stories circulate or fail when transplants do not fit. The framework explains the cross-national spread of minimum wages, carbon taxes, and smoking bans.

“现代化假说”认为经济发展会带来民主化，这一观点的证据与批评是什么？

The “modernization hypothesis” holds that economic development brings democratization—what is the evidence and the criticism?

李普塞特提出繁荣-教育-民主的关联，跨国数据确实显示高收入国家更可能维持民主，政治学共识是“经济发展虽不直接导致民主化，却是民主存活的最强预测因子”（“没有国家在贫困中保持民主”的论点）。但批评同样扎实：许多高增长国家长期保持威权（如海湾产油国，资源收入削弱了“国家依赖公民征税”的问责逻辑）；中产阶级崛起是否推动变革，取决于其与政权的利益绑定；反向因果同样存在——民主也可能促进教育投资。近期研究还发现，经济发展的作用更多是降低政变与威权回潮风险，而不是触发转型本身。
Lipset documented the prosperity-education-democracy correlation; cross-national data indeed show high-income countries are far more likely to sustain democracy, and the field's consensus is that development is the strongest predictor of democratic survival, even if it does not directly cause democratization (the “no nation in poverty stays democratic” argument). But criticisms are solid: many fast-growing countries stayed authoritarian for long periods (e.g., Gulf oil states, where resource revenue weakens the accountability logic of taxing citizens); whether a rising middle class pushes for change depends on its ties to the regime; reverse causality also exists—democracy may boost education investment. Recent research further shows development's role is more about lowering coup and authoritarian-reversal risks than triggering transition itself.

为什么有些国家采用联邦制？

Why are some countries federal?

联邦制通常出现在幅员广阔、族群或地区差异大的国家，它把权力在中央和地方之间分配。这样既能保持全国统一市场与国防，又能让各地区按自身情况立法（如教育、税收），减少单一集权引发的矛盾。历史上，联邦制也常是各方妥协、避免分裂的产物。
Federalism usually appears in large or internally diverse countries that split power between a central government and regional units. This keeps a unified market and defense while letting regions legislate on local matters like education or taxation, reducing conflicts from one-size-fits-all rule. Historically, federal systems also emerged as compromises that prevented breakup.

「民主和平论」主张民主国家之间为何较少爆发战争？

What does the 'democratic peace theory' claim about why democracies rarely fight one another?

民主和平论由迈克尔·多伊尔等学者在 1980 年代系统论证，认为民主国家因制度约束、相互透明的规范与和平解决争端的习惯，极少彼此开战。它已成为国际关系研究的经验性命题，但也长期伴随「选择性」与定义争议的批评。
Democratic peace theory, systematically argued by Michael Doyle and others in the 1980s, holds that democracies rarely wage war on one another because of institutional constraints, norms of transparency, and habits of peaceful dispute resolution. It is an empirical claim in international relations, though long debated over selection bias and definitions.

一部法律从想法到正式生效，一般要经过哪些环节？

What stages does a law typically pass through before it takes effect?

大致流程是：问题被识别并进入议程，立法机关提出议案，经委员会审议、公开辩论、修改后表决，通过后由行政首脑签署或批准，然后配套实施细则、设立执法机构并接受司法与公众监督。现实中的政策过程并不是直线，而是利益集团、媒体、专家和公众多方博弈的过程，很多法律在讨论中反复修改，执行环节往往最终决定政策的实际效果。
Roughly: a problem is identified and put on the agenda; a bill is introduced, reviewed in committee, publicly debated, amended, and voted on; after passage the chief executive signs or approves it; then come implementing regulations, enforcement agencies, and judicial and public oversight. In reality the process is not linear but a bargaining game among interest groups, media, experts, and the public; many bills change repeatedly during debate, and implementation often decides the real outcome.

普通公民能通过哪些方式参与公共事务？参与为什么重要？

In what ways can ordinary citizens take part in public affairs, and why does participation matter?

途径包括投票、参与社区事务、参加听证会和意见征集、加入公益或利益团体、写信和网络表达意见、参与志愿服务，甚至发起倡议或公益诉讼。参与的意义不仅是影响结果，更在于过程价值：让决策者了解多元需求、增强决策的合法性，同时培养公民的责任感和协商能力。但参与本身也不平等——时间、资源、信息多的人声音更大，因此制度设计需要为弱势群体留出表达空间。
Channels include voting, community affairs, public hearings and comment periods, joining civic or advocacy groups, writing and posting opinions, volunteering, and even launching petitions or public-interest lawsuits. Participation matters beyond outcomes: it surfaces diverse needs, strengthens the legitimacy of decisions, and cultivates civic responsibility and deliberation. Participation is itself unequal, since those with more time, resources, and information speak louder, so institutional design must leave room for disadvantaged voices.

关于“严刑峻法能否降低犯罪”，实证研究的主要结论是什么？

What do empirical studies conclude about whether harsh punishment reduces crime?

大量研究一致发现“确定性（被抓获概率）比严厉性（刑期长短）更能威慑犯罪”，因为潜在罪犯对被抓概率更敏感。关于死刑威慑的争议极大：早期“每执行一次可挽救若干生命”的估计后来多被批评存在方法缺陷，近年准实验研究（如邻州废除与保留死刑的比较）总体未发现死刑有明显额外威慑效应。较扎实的发现是：增加街头警力与破案投入有威慑作用，而单纯延长刑期边际效果递减，甚至可能通过“监狱学习”机制增加再犯。政策含义是惩罚资源应偏向确定性而非一味加重。
Research consistently finds that certainty of apprehension deters crime more than severity of sentence, because potential offenders respond more to detection probability. The death-penalty deterrence literature is deeply contested: early estimates claiming each execution saves several lives were later criticized for methodological flaws, and recent quasi-experimental comparisons (e.g., neighboring states abolishing versus keeping capital punishment) find no robust additional deterrent effect. Firmer findings are that visible policing and detection effort deter, while merely lengthening sentences has diminishing marginal effects and may even increase recidivism through “prison as a school of crime.” The policy implication: allocate punishment resources toward certainty, not pure severity.

联邦制与单一制有何区别？联邦制为什么常与“多样性大国”相联系？

How do federal and unitary systems differ, and why are federations often associated with large, diverse countries?

联邦制下，中央与地方（州、邦）的权力由宪法明确划分，双方都不能单方剥夺对方的权力，地方有自己的立法权、税收权和行政体系；单一制下地方权力由中央立法授予、可被收回。联邦制通过“让不同地区保留自治”来容纳语言、民族、宗教或经济差异，减少统一强压引发的冲突，同时保持共同市场与统一外交国防，但也可能带来政策碎片化、地区差距与相互“推卸责任”的问题。
In a federation, central and subnational (state) powers are divided by the constitution; neither can unilaterally take away the other's powers, and states have their own legislative, taxing, and administrative authority. In a unitary state, subnational powers are granted and revocable by central legislation. Federalism accommodates linguistic, ethnic, religious, or economic diversity by letting regions keep autonomy, reducing conflict from forced uniformity while preserving a common market and unified foreign and defense policy—but it can also bring policy fragmentation, regional gaps, and mutual blame-shifting.

为什么国家之间会结成联盟？

Why do countries form alliances?

国家结盟主要为了安全和共同利益。单个国家面对威胁时力量有限，结盟能互相承诺军事保护、分摊成本和威慑对手（集体安全）。联盟也方便贸易、情报共享和外交协调。本质上，结盟是“抱团”以降低风险、放大影响力的策略。
Countries form alliances mainly for security and shared interests. A single state facing a threat is limited, but an alliance offers mutual defense, shares costs, and deters adversaries through collective security. Alliances also ease trade, intelligence sharing, and diplomatic coordination. In essence, banding together is a strategy to lower risk and amplify influence.

多数代表制与比例代表制对政党和议会构成有何不同影响？

How do majoritarian and proportional electoral systems differ in their effects on parties and parliaments?

多数制（如英美的单名选区）按“赢者通吃”分配议席，倾向产生两党制和单一多数政府，政治清晰但小党难获议席、得票与议席可能严重失衡；比例制（如欧洲多国）按得票比例分配议席，倾向产生多党制与联合政府，代表性更高但政府更易碎片化、组阁谈判成本高。杜瓦杰定律总结了这种规律性联系，实践中各国还通过门槛、选区规模等参数加以调节。
Majoritarian systems (single-member districts in the UK and US) allocate seats winner-take-all, tending to produce two-party systems and single-party majorities—clear politics, but small parties get few seats and votes-to-seats ratios can be badly distorted. Proportional systems (common across Europe) allocate seats by vote share, tending to produce multiparty systems and coalition governments—more representative, but more fragmented and costlier to form. Duverger's law summarizes this regularity, and countries fine-tune it through thresholds and district magnitudes.

公民社会指政府和企业之外的民间组织和网络，如慈善团体、社团和志愿者组织，它们让民众能自发参与公共事务。
Civil society refers to the web of non-governmental, non-business groups like charities, associations, and volunteer organizations through which people engage in public life.

国际关系中的「安全困境」为什么会让两个都不想开战的国家走向对立？

In international relations, why does the 'security dilemma' push two states that want peace into confrontation?

赫茨在 1950 年提出，一国为自保而加强军备，会被邻国视为威胁，邻国随之扩军，结果双方都更不安全。这种「防御性行为引发对方恐惧」的循环，常使本无恶意的强国陷入对抗。
Herz argued in 1950 that when one state arms for defense, neighbors see a threat and rearm too, leaving everyone less secure. This cycle—where defensive acts provoke the other's fear—can drive powers with no hostile intent into rivalry.

民主制度的基本形式有哪些？

What are the basic forms of democracy?

主要有直接民主（公民直接表决）和代议民主（选举代表代为决策）两种形式。
The two main forms are direct democracy (citizens vote directly) and representative democracy (elected officials decide).

什么是公民权利？

What are civil rights?

公民权利是保障个人免受歧视、享有人身自由与平等保护的法律权利。
Civil rights are legal guarantees protecting individuals from discrimination and ensuring liberty and equal protection.
