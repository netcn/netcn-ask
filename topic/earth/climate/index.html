温室气体到底是怎么给地球"保暖"的？

How exactly do greenhouse gases keep Earth warm?

太阳辐射以可见光为主，穿过大气加热地表；地面则以红外线向外辐射。二氧化碳、水汽、甲烷等温室气体能吸收特定波段的红外光，再向各个方向重新辐射，其中射向地面的部分把热量截留在大气低层，这就是温室效应。没有它地球平均气温约-18摄氏度；温室气体浓度升高则使这个截留增强，地面升温。这种机制与玻璃温室的"捂热"不同，本质是大气吸收再回辐射。
Solar radiation, mostly visible light, passes through the atmosphere and warms the surface, which then radiates infrared outward. Greenhouse gases—carbon dioxide, water vapor, methane—absorb infrared at specific wavelengths and re-emit it in all directions; the downward part traps heat in the lower atmosphere. That is the greenhouse effect. Without it Earth's average surface temperature would be about -18 C, and rising greenhouse gas concentrations strengthen the trapping and warm the surface. The mechanism differs from a physical glass greenhouse; it is absorption and reradiation by the atmosphere.

格陵兰和南极冰盖中的快速冰流（如松岛冰川）为何比周围冰体移动快得多？

Why do fast-flowing ice streams such as Pine Island Glacier move far faster than the surrounding ice?

快速运动主要取决于底部条件：冰床位于海平面以下，地热或摩擦生热使底冰达到融点，形成润滑的融水层，冰流通过底部滑动和冰内变形快速前进；同时冰架支撑减弱时冰流加速。冰架变薄使冰流末端阻力减小，接地线随即后退，更多冰体浮起，又进一步加速冰流——这种冰架变薄—接地线后退—冰流加速的正反馈是当前南极冰量损失的关键机制。松岛冰川等快速冰流把内陆冰输送到海洋，其质量损失已构成南极对海平面上升的主要贡献之一。
Fast flow is controlled by basal conditions: the bed lies below sea level, and geothermal or frictional heat melts the basal ice, creating a lubricating water layer along which the stream surges forward by basal sliding and internal deformation; loss of ice-shelf buttressing then accelerates the stream. Thinning of the shelf reduces resistance at the ice front, the grounding line retreats, more ice floats, and the stream accelerates further—this feedback of shelf thinning, grounding-line retreat, and stream acceleration is the key mechanism behind current Antarctic mass loss. Fast-flowing streams such as Pine Island Glacier drain the interior to the ocean, and their losses are among the main contributors to Antarctica's sea-level contribution.

米兰科维奇（Milankovitch）理论如何解释冰期的周期性？

How does Milankovitch theory explain the periodicity of ice ages?

米兰科维奇理论指出，地球轨道的三类缓慢变化——离心率（约10万年）、转轴倾角/黄赤交角（约4.1万年）和岁差（约2.3万/1.9万年）——改变了各纬度接收的太阳辐射量，从而驱动更新世的冰期—间冰期循环。这些周期在深海沉积和冰芯记录中都能被识别。
Milankovitch theory holds that three slow changes in Earth's orbit—eccentricity (~100 kyr), axial tilt/obliquity (~41 kyr), and precession (~23 kyr/19 kyr)—alter the amount of solar radiation reaching different latitudes, driving Pleistocene glacial–interglacial cycles. These periodicities are recognizable in deep-sea sediments and ice cores.

树木年轮为何能重建过去数千年的气候？这一方法有什么局限？

How can tree rings reconstruct climate over past millennia, and what are the method's limitations?

在中高纬地区，年轮宽度、密度和同位素组成与生长季温度或降水密切相关，宽轮对应有利生长条件；通过交叉定年，可把活树、历史木材与地下残木的年轮序列拼接延长至上万年。为保证气候信号可靠，还需对每棵树进行去趋势（标准化），去除树木自身的年龄趋势（幼年轮宽、老年轮窄），使不同树之间的信号可比。局限在于：只覆盖中高纬陆地，分辨率通常为年，且年轮还受虫害、竞争等非气候因素影响；近百年CO₂施肥与氮沉降也可能改变树木的气候敏感性，使现代校准在更早时期失真。
At mid and high latitudes, ring width, density, and isotope composition correlate with growing-season temperature or moisture, wide rings marking favorable years; cross-dating stitches living trees, historical wood, and subfossil logs into chronologies spanning ten millennia. To extract a reliable climate signal, each series must be detrended (standardized) to remove the tree's own age trend—juvenile rings are wide, old rings narrow—so that trees are comparable. Limits: coverage is land-only at mid–high latitudes, resolution is typically annual, rings are also influenced by non-climatic factors such as pests and competition, and modern CO₂ fertilization and nitrogen deposition may have altered trees' climate sensitivity, degrading modern calibrations when applied to the past.

什么是气候敏感参数？

What is climate sensitivity?

气候敏感参数通常指在二氧化碳浓度翻倍时全球平均气温的平衡上升幅度，主流估计约为1.5至4.5摄氏度。它是综合云、冰雪和海洋反馈后的关键指标，用于评估长期增暖程度。
Climate sensitivity usually means the equilibrium global temperature rise when carbon dioxide concentration doubles, with a mainstream estimate of about 1.5 to 4.5 degrees Celsius. It is a key metric that integrates cloud, ice, and ocean feedbacks to gauge long-term warming.

什么是大西洋经向翻转环流（AMOC）？有哪些证据表明它在减弱？

What is the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) and what evidence suggests it is weakening?

AMOC 是北大西洋把温暖表层水向北输送、冷水向南深层回流的翻转环流，对欧洲气候至关重要。观测显示其流速自20世纪中叶以来减弱，且北大西洋冷斑与盐度变化提示可能逼近临界阈值。
The AMOC is the North Atlantic overturning that carries warm surface water northward and returns cold water southward at depth, crucial for European climate. Observations indicate a weakening since the mid-20th century, with a North Atlantic cold blob and salinity shifts hinting it may approach a tipping point.

什么是厄尔尼诺现象？

What is El Niño?

正常年份信风把暖水推向西太平洋，南美沿岸不断上涌冷水；信风一弱，暖水东回，赤道中东太平洋海温比常年高几度，上涌与营养盐随之减少。对流中心则向东移，沃克环流被重整，远方的旱涝就这样连成一片。名字来自秘鲁渔民，他们常在圣诞前后察觉暖流，以「圣婴」称之。要分清：厄尔尼诺是 ENSO 的暖位相，与南方涛动是一件事的海、气两面，冷位相叫拉尼娜；它是年际变率，叠加而非等同长期趋势。
In a normal year the trade winds pile warm water against the western Pacific and cold water keeps upwelling off South America; when those winds slacken the warm pool sloshes back east, sea surface temperature in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific runs a few degrees above usual, and upwelling and its nutrient supply thin out with it. The rain-bearing convection then shifts east, the Walker circulation is reorganised, and that is how distant droughts and deluges end up linked. The name comes from Peruvian fishermen, who usually noticed this warm current around Christmas and called it the Christ Child. What to keep straight: El Niño is the warm phase of ENSO, the ocean side of one phenomenon whose atmospheric side is the Southern Oscillation, with La Niña as the cold phase; it is year-to-year variability riding on top of the long-term trend, not the trend itself.

南极冰芯里的气泡和氧同位素怎样还原80万年气候史？

How do air bubbles and oxygen isotopes in Antarctic ice cores reconstruct 800,000 years of climate?

南极冰盖年复一年压实的雪封存着当年的空气气泡，其中的CO2与CH4浓度可直接测量，构成大气成分的直接档案。同时，降水中氧-18与氧-16的比值随温度变化，寒冷期较轻的氧-16更易留在冰盖中，使冰的δ18O曲线成为温度代用指标。欧洲南极冰芯计划（EPICA）的冰芯把记录延伸到约80万年前，显示CO2浓度与温度在冰期旋回中同步起伏，为理解大气、冰冻圈与碳循环的耦合提供了基准数据。
Layer after layer of compressed snow in ice sheets traps air bubbles whose CO2 and CH4 concentrations can be measured directly—a true archive of past atmospheres. The ratio of oxygen-18 to oxygen-16 in precipitation also tracks temperature, because the lighter isotope is more readily locked into ice during cold periods, making the ice's d18O curve a temperature proxy. The EPICA ice core extends this record back about 800,000 years and shows CO2 and temperature rising and falling together through glacial cycles, providing benchmark data on the coupling of the atmosphere, cryosphere, and carbon cycle.

冰期大气CO₂约低至180 ppm、间冰期约280 ppm，这约90 ppm的差额主要储存在哪里？

Glacial atmospheric CO₂ fell to roughly 180 ppm versus about 280 ppm in interglacials. Where was the roughly 90 ppm difference mainly stored?

主要储存在深海。冰期时生物泵效率更高，加上铁施肥增强、南极底水通风减弱、海冰覆盖面积扩大降低海气交换，使溶解无机碳和营养盐在深海积累。这一“深海碳库”增强机制是解释冰期CO₂低值的主流假说。
Mainly in the deep ocean. During glacials, a more efficient biological pump—strengthened iron fertilization, weaker Antarctic bottom-water ventilation, and wider sea-ice cover suppressing air–sea gas exchange—accumulated dissolved inorganic carbon and nutrients in the deep sea. This enhanced deep-sea carbon storage is the leading hypothesis for glacial CO₂ drawdown.

什么是海洋酸化？

What is ocean acidification?

海水吸收二氧化碳后生成碳酸并解离出氢离子，pH 随之下降；更麻烦的是氢离子会消耗碳酸根，压低碳酸钙饱和度。珊瑚、贝类和翼足类都靠碳酸根造壳与骨骼，饱和度一降，造壳更费能量，海水甚至可能溶掉已成的壳。两处易混：其一，海水仍属碱性，酸化指朝中性偏移而非变成酸；其二，pH 是对数标度，看着只降一点，氢离子浓度上升却明显得多。影响也不均匀：冷水、高纬和上升流区更早触及不饱和。
When seawater takes up carbon dioxide, the gas forms carbonic acid and releases hydrogen ions, so pH falls; the nastier part is that those ions also consume carbonate, lowering the saturation state of calcium carbonate. Corals, shellfish and pteropods all build their shells and skeletons out of carbonate, so a lower saturation state means spending more energy on a shell, and the water can even start dissolving shells already made. Two things get mixed up here: first, seawater is still alkaline, and acidification means drifting toward neutral rather than turning into acid; second, pH is a logarithmic scale, so a drop that looks small on the meter is a much larger rise in hydrogen-ion concentration. The effect is also uneven, with cold water, high latitudes and upwelling zones reaching undersaturation first.

树木的年轮能告诉我们什么气候信息？

What climate information can tree rings tell us?

为什么硅酸盐矿物风化被称为地球的长期“恒温器”？

硅酸盐风化消耗大气CO₂，例如CaSiO₃ + 2CO₂ + 3H₂O → Ca²⁺ + 2HCO₃⁻ + H₄SiO₄，风化产物经河流入海并与碳酸盐一起把碳封存在海底岩石中。温度升高会加速风化、增强碳吸收，形成负反馈；火山释放CO₂补充碳源，这一机制使地球在数十亿年间保持相对温和的气候。这一负反馈以大陆风化面积与火山碳源为条件：威尔逊旋回中的造山抬升（如喜马拉雅造山带）通过暴露新鲜岩石加速风化，可能触发新生代长期变冷，因此硅酸盐风化被视作维持地球宜居性的关键调控机制。
Silicate weathering consumes atmospheric CO₂—for example CaSiO₃ + 2CO₂ + 3H₂O → Ca²⁺ + 2HCO₃⁻ + H₄SiO₄—and weathering products carried to the ocean, together with carbonates, bury carbon in seafloor rocks. Warmer temperatures accelerate weathering and CO₂ uptake in a negative feedback; volcanic CO₂ emissions resupply carbon, a mechanism that has kept Earth's climate relatively temperate for billions of years. The feedback depends on the extent of continental weathering and on volcanic carbon input: orogeny in the Wilson cycle (such as the Himalaya) exposes fresh rock and accelerates weathering, possibly driving Cenozoic long-term cooling, which is why silicate weathering is regarded as a key regulator of Earth's habitability.

Venus' dense CO₂ atmosphere (surface pressure about 92 times Earth's) drives an extreme greenhouse effect; its sulfuric-acid clouds reflect sunlight, but beneath them CO₂ strongly traps outgoing heat, raising the surface temperature to about 465°C. Water vapor was photolyzed in the upper atmosphere and hydrogen escaped to space—Venus' D/H ratio about 120–150 times Earth's records this massive water loss—leaving Venus dry and scorching.

“天气”和“气候”有什么不同？

What is the difference between 'weather' and 'climate'?

天气是某一时刻、某一地点的大气瞬时状态（如今天是否下雨、气温多少）。气候则是某个地区天气在较长时间（世界气象组织以30年为一标准时段）里的平均状况和变率。简单说：天气是“你今天穿什么”，气候是“你衣橱里常备什么”。
Weather is the atmosphere's state at a specific time and place (whether it rains today, the temperature now). Climate is the long-term average and variability of weather in a region—the WMO uses a standard 30-year period. Simply put: weather is what you wear today; climate is what your wardrobe usually needs.

Sunlight warms the ground, which then radiates heat back as infrared light; gases like carbon dioxide and water vapor act like a blanket, trapping some of that heat and keeping Earth warm enough for life. Without it the planet would be far colder.

In summer the land heats faster than the ocean and forms low pressure, pulling in moist air from the sea. That vapor-laden air rises and cools after reaching land, producing heavy rainfall, so monsoon regions are typically rainy in summer.
