什么是“温盐环流”（thermohaline circulation），为什么被称作“全球传送带”？

What is the thermohaline circulation, and why is it called the 'global conveyor belt'?

温盐环流是由海水温度和盐度差异导致的密度驱动的大尺度深层洋流，北大西洋表层冷水下沉后沿深海洋流向南、最终在全球大洋循环并返回。它把热量和碳在各大洋间输送，对全球气候调节至关重要，其中“大西洋经向翻转环流（AMOC）”是核心环节。
Thermohaline circulation is a density-driven, large-scale deep ocean flow caused by differences in temperature and salinity; in the North Atlantic, cooling surface water sinks and travels south through the deep ocean before circulating globally and returning. It distributes heat and carbon across oceans and is vital to climate, with the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) at its core.

海底甲烷水合物为何被视为潜在的能源与气候风险因素？

Why are submarine methane hydrates seen as both a potential energy source and a climate risk?

甲烷水合物是甲烷分子被水分子笼形包裹的固态物质，稳定存在于低温高压的海底沉积物中。它蕴藏可观的天然气资源，但全球变暖使海底升温可能触发其不稳定分解，释放强效温室气体甲烷。克文沃尔登在1993年综述了其分布与环境意义。
Methane hydrate is methane trapped in cage-like water molecules, stable in cold, high-pressure seafloor sediments. It represents a sizable natural-gas resource, but warming that destabilizes the seabed could release potent methane greenhouse gas. Kvenvolden reviewed its distribution and environmental significance in 1993.

大西洋经向翻转环流（AMOC）对欧洲气候为何如此重要？

Why is the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation so important for European climate?

AMOC把表层暖水向北输送，在北大西洋冷却下沉后把热量释放到大气，为欧洲带来温和冬季。它一旦减弱，欧洲与北美东岸会变冷，并扰乱热带降雨带。拉姆斯托夫与IPCC第六次评估报告都警告全球变暖可能削弱这一环流。
The AMOC carries warm surface water northward, releasing heat to the atmosphere as it cools and sinks in the North Atlantic, giving Europe mild winters. If it weakens, Europe and the North American east coast cool and tropical rain belts shift. Rahmstorf and the IPCC AR6 warn that global warming may undermine this circulation.

海沟是怎么形成的？以马里亚纳海沟为例。

How do ocean trenches form? Take the Mariana Trench as an example.

海沟是板块俯冲时一块板块下插、在海底刻出的狭长深谷，是海洋的最深处。马里亚纳海沟位于太平洋板块俯冲到菲律宾海板块之下，最深处超过1.1万米。
An ocean trench is a long, narrow, deepest part of the sea carved where one plate dives beneath another during subduction. The Mariana Trench formed where the Pacific plate subducts under the Philippine Sea plate and reaches over 11,000 meters deep.

深海平原的沉积速率为何比近岸慢得多？

Why are sedimentation rates on abyssal plains much slower than near shore?

近岸有河流直送大量泥沙、生物碎屑也多，堆积快；深海远离陆源，主要靠极细的悬浮尘和浮游生物残骸缓缓飘落，所以每千年只积几厘米。
Nearshore areas get direct river mud and lots of debris, so they build fast; the deep sea is far from land sources and relies on fine dust and plankton remains drifting down, accumulating only centimeters per millennium.

上升流为何能造就世界上最高产的渔场？

Why does upwelling create some of the world's most productive fisheries?

上升流由风把表层水吹离海岸，深层富营养冷水随之上涌补充表层。这些营养盐触发浮游植物大量繁殖，支撑起庞大的食物链与渔获。斯韦德鲁普在1942年的《海洋》中系统描述了风生上升流的动力学。
Upwelling occurs when wind drives surface water away from coast and deep, nutrient-rich cold water rises to replace it. Those nutrients trigger blooms of phytoplankton that support a vast food chain and large catches. Sverdrup described the dynamics of wind-driven upwelling in The Oceans (1942).

什么是“海洋酸化”？它主要由什么引起？

What is 'ocean acidification,' and what mainly causes it?

海洋酸化指海水因吸收大气中过量二氧化碳，发生化学反应生成碳酸，使表层海水pH下降的现象。自工业化以来海洋已吸收了约30%的人为CO₂排放，表层pH从约8.2降至约8.1，威胁珊瑚和贝类等钙化生物。
Ocean acidification is the decrease in seawater pH as the ocean absorbs excess atmospheric CO₂, which forms carbonic acid. Since industrialization the ocean has taken up about 30% of human CO₂ emissions, lowering surface pH from about 8.2 to about 8.1 and threatening calcifying organisms like corals and shellfish.

大洋环流中的环流涡（gyre）是如何维持的？

How are the great ocean gyres sustained in the general circulation?

环流涡是受信风、西风与科里奥利力共同约束的副热带大型环流，风应力旋度在中心累积水柱高度。洋流沿等压线流动，海岸地形与西风漂流闭合整个环路。斯托梅尔在1965年对湾流的动力学做了开创性刻画。
A gyre is a large subtropical circulation shaped by trade winds, westerlies, and the Coriolis force, with wind-stress curl piling water at its center. Currents flow along pressure contours, and coastlines plus the Antarctic Circumpolar Current close the loop. Stommel gave a pioneering dynamical picture of the Gulf Stream in 1965.

海洋酸化是怎么发生的，它对珊瑚和贝类有何影响？

How does ocean acidification occur, and how does it affect corals and shellfish?

大气二氧化碳溶于海水生成碳酸，降低海水的pH与碳酸根离子浓度，使海洋酸化。珊瑚与贝类需要用碳酸根构建碳酸钙外壳，酸性增强会抑制钙化甚至溶解已有壳体。多尼等人在2009年综述指出这一化学转变正以地质尺度罕见的速度发生。
Atmospheric carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater to form carbonic acid, lowering pH and carbonate-ion concentration, which is ocean acidification. Corals and shellfish need carbonate to build calcium-carbonate shells, and rising acidity inhibits calcification and can dissolve existing shells. Doney and colleagues noted in 2009 that this chemical shift is happening at a rate rare in geologic history.

洋流如何帮助调节地球气候？

How do ocean currents help regulate Earth's climate?

由风驱动的表面洋流（如墨西哥湾流）把低纬度的暖水输向高纬度，把冷水带回热带，在全球重新分配热量，使沿海和大陆气候更温和。它们与深层温盐环流共同构成地球的“热量传送带”。
Wind-driven surface currents, such as the Gulf Stream, carry warm water from low latitudes toward high latitudes and return cold water toward the tropics, redistributing heat globally and moderating coastal and continental climates. Together with deep thermohaline flow they form Earth's 'heat conveyor.'

海水为什么是咸的？平均盐度大约是多少？

Why is seawater salty, and what is its average salinity?

河流把陆地岩石风化释放的溶解盐（主要是氯化钠）带入海洋，而蒸发只带走纯水、留下盐分，盐分便在海中长期累积。全球海洋平均盐度约为35‰（即每千克海水约35克盐），但会随蒸发、降水和结冰而变化。
Rivers carry dissolved salts—mainly sodium chloride—released by weathering of rocks into the ocean, while evaporation removes only pure water and leaves the salt behind, so salts accumulate over geologic time. The global average salinity is about 35‰ (roughly 35 g of salt per kg of seawater), varying with evaporation, precipitation, and ice formation.

The Moon's gravity bulges the ocean on both near and far sides; Earth's spin makes two tides daily.

How long does it take river sediment to build one layer on the seafloor?

在海底很多地方，泥沙每年只落下薄薄一层，积一厘米可能要几十年到上百年，所以海底的泥是一层层慢慢写成的“地球日记”。

什么是珊瑚礁？

What is a coral reef?

珊瑚礁是由珊瑚虫分泌的碳酸钙骨骼长期堆积形成的水下结构，常见于温暖清澈的热带浅海。它们只占海洋面积的一小部分，却养育了极高比例的海洋物种。
A coral reef is an underwater structure built from the calcium-carbonate skeletons secreted by coral polyps over time, common in warm, clear tropical shallows. Though covering only a small fraction of the ocean, reefs support a very high proportion of marine species.

为什么全球海平面正在上升？

Why is global sea level rising?

海平面上升主要源于两方面：全球变暖使海水受热膨胀，以及陆地冰川和冰盖融化把更多水注入海洋。这对沿海低地和岛屿构成淹没与风暴潮加剧的威胁。
Sea-level rise mainly comes from two effects: warming oceans expand in volume, and melting land ice and ice sheets add water to the sea. This threatens low-lying coasts and islands with inundation and stronger storm surges.

海浪是怎么产生的？

How are ocean waves generated?

海浪主要由风刮过海面、把能量传给海水而形成，风越大、吹得越久越远，浪就越高。海啸等巨浪则多由海底地震或火山引起，与日常风浪成因不同。
Ocean waves are mainly generated when wind blows across the surface and transfers energy to the water; stronger, longer, and farther winds make bigger waves. Giant waves like tsunamis, however, are caused by undersea earthquakes or volcanoes, not wind.

海洋可以分成哪几层？

What layers is the ocean divided into?

海洋按光照和温度大致分为透光层、弱光层（中层）和无光层（深层），深层又包括黑暗寒冷的深层与海底。不同水层光照、温度和生物群落差异显著。
By light and temperature the ocean is roughly split into the sunlit epipelagic, the dim mesopelagic (twilight), and the dark bathypelagic (deep), down to the seafloor. These layers differ sharply in light, temperature, and the life they host.

潮汐主要是由什么天体引起的，为什么每天通常有两涨两落？

Which celestial body mainly causes tides, and why are there usually two rises and two falls per day?

月球引力是潮汐的主要成因，它在地球近月侧和背月侧都拉起隆起，形成两个潮汐峰。地球自转使大多数海岸每天经过这两个峰，于是出现约两次涨落。太阳引力叠加则会放大或削弱这些潮汐，形成大潮与小潮。
The Moon's gravity is the main cause of tides, raising bulges on both the near and far sides of Earth and forming two tidal peaks. Earth's rotation carries most coasts past both bulges each day, giving roughly two rises and falls. The Sun's gravity adds to or subtracts from them, making spring and neap tides.

环礁中间为什么是一片海水?年轻的达尔文在船上想通了这个圈

Why do atolls ring open lagoons? Young Darwin solved the circle from a ships deck.

达尔文在贝格尔号航行中提出优雅解释:珊瑚礁贴着缓慢下沉的火山岛生长,岛屿逐渐没入海面,珊瑚为追光持续向上建造,最终留下环绕潟湖的一圈礁环。这一沉降理论预测钻探会在环礁底部打到玄武岩——1952年美国在埃尼威托克的钻孔于千米深处证实了浅水珊瑚化石叠在火山岩上,百年预言应验。如今叠加了冰期海平面波动的修正版本更加精细,但核心逻辑未变:造礁珊瑚对水深苛刻,只能用不间断的生长追平下沉的海底。
Aboard the Beagle, Darwin proposed elegantly: reefs cling to slowly subsiding volcanoes, corals building upward to keep in sunlight as islands drown, leaving a ring around a lagoon. Subsidence theory predicted drilling would hit basalt beneath atolls — the 1952 Enewetak borehole confirmed shallow-water coral fossils stacked on volcano at kilometer depth, validating a century-old prophecy. Ice-age sea-level swings refined the model later, but the core logic stands: reef corals tolerate only shallow light water, so they must outgrow the sinking seafloor without pause.
