为什么S波无法穿过地球的外核？这一事实说明了什么？

Why can't S-waves pass through Earth's outer core, and what does this reveal?

S波是横波，必须靠物质的剪切弹性传播，而液体无法承受剪切应力，因此S波在抵达液态外核时会被完全阻挡，形成“S波影区”。这一观测是地球外核为液态的最关键地震学证据。
S-waves are shear waves that require a material's ability to resist shear, but liquids cannot support shear stress, so S-waves are completely blocked at the liquid outer core, producing an 'S-wave shadow zone.' This observation is the key seismological evidence that Earth's outer core is liquid.

地震波里的P波和S波到底有什么区别？

What exactly distinguishes P-waves from S-waves?

P波（纵波）是介质沿传播方向来回振动的压缩波，能穿过固体、液体和气体，速度最快，地壳内约每秒6公里；S波（横波）是质点垂直于传播方向振动的剪切波，速度约为P波的六成，且不能穿过液体。由于S波无法通过液态铁镍组成的地核外核，地震图上出现S波盲区，据此科学家推断外核是液态的。利用P、S波到时差还能定位震中并确定震源深度。
P-waves are compressional waves with particles vibrating along the propagation direction; they pass through solids, liquids, and gases and are fastest, roughly 6 km/s in the crust. S-waves are shear waves with particles vibrating perpendicular to propagation, travel at about 60% of P-wave speed, and cannot pass through liquids. Because S-waves cannot cross Earth's liquid iron outer core, a shadow zone appears on seismograms—the evidence that the outer core is molten. The P-S arrival-time difference also locates epicenters and focal depths.

断层为什么会"十年不发，一朝爆发"？弹性回跳理论怎么解释？

Why do faults stay quiet for decades and then snap? How does elastic rebound explain it?

板块持续相对运动，断层两盘被摩擦锁住、不能滑动，于是周围岩石像橡皮筋一样慢慢积累弹性应变。当应力超过摩擦强度，断层突然滑动，累积的应变瞬间释放，产生地震，岩石弹回未变形的位置，这就是弹性回跳理论。断层以特征周期重复破裂，形成"地震空区"，用来评估未来大震风险。1906年旧金山地震后，里德据此提出该理论，成为现代地震学的基石。
Plates keep moving past each other, but friction locks the fault, so the surrounding rock stores elastic strain like a stretched rubber band. When stress exceeds the frictional strength, the fault slips suddenly, releasing the stored strain in an earthquake as the rock snaps back toward its undeformed shape—the elastic rebound theory. Faults rupture repeatedly with characteristic cycles, creating seismic gaps used to assess future large quakes. H.F. Reid proposed the theory after the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, and it remains a cornerstone of modern seismology.

什么是地震空区？

What is a seismic gap?

夹在反复破裂的活跃段之间、却长期安静的断层段，就是地震空区。两侧不断释放应力，这一段因闭锁而把板块运动的变形攒在地下，一旦破裂便可能成为强震震源。关键是闭锁而非单纯的安静：靠无震蠕滑慢慢释放应变的断层，再久不发震也不算空区。空区也只是相对邻段的统计定位，远超平均复发间隔仍不破裂的例子不少，给不出发震时间。
A seismic gap is a fault segment that stays quiet while those around it rupture again and again. Its neighbours keep releasing stress, but a locked gap stores the strain plate motion puts into the crust, so when it breaks it may host a large earthquake. The decisive ingredient is locking rather than quietness alone: a fault that sheds strain by aseismic creep is not a gap however long it goes without one. And a gap is only a position relative to its neighbours, since segments sitting far past their average recurrence interval without breaking are common, so it gives no time of occurrence.

什么是地震矩震级（Mw）？它为何取代里氏震级？

What is moment magnitude (Mw), and why did it replace the Richter scale?

地震矩震级基于地震矩，即断层面积、平均滑移量与岩石刚度的乘积，能更准确衡量巨大地震的总能量。里氏震级在大震时趋于饱和，因此现代科学改用Mw描述强震。
Moment magnitude is based on seismic moment, the product of fault area, average slip and rock rigidity, giving a more accurate total energy for huge quakes. The Richter scale saturates for large events, so science now uses Mw for major earthquakes.

什么是弹性回跳理论？

What is the elastic rebound theory?

弹性回跳理论由里德提出，解释断层地震机制：岩层在应力下弹性变形积累应变，当超过岩石强度时沿断层突然破裂错动，弹回原状并释放能量。该理论是现代地震成因的基础。
Proposed by Reid, elastic rebound theory explains fault earthquakes: rock elastically deforms and stores strain under stress, then suddenly ruptures and slips along a fault, snapping back and releasing energy. It is the foundation of modern earthquake mechanics.

What is the difference between earthquake magnitude and intensity?

Magnitude measures released energy (e.g. Richter) and is single per quake; intensity describes local shaking damage and decreases with distance.

什么是地震的「震级饱和」？

What is 'magnitude saturation' in earthquakes?

某些近源震级标度（如地方性震级ML）在大地震时因地震波周期与台站距离限制，测值不再随真实能量增大而上升，即饱和。因此特大地震须用矩震级Mw表述。
Some local magnitude scales (like ML) stop increasing with true energy for huge quakes due to wave-period and station-distance limits, a saturation. Very large earthquakes must therefore be expressed by moment magnitude Mw.

同样一次地震,为什么建在深厚软土盆地上的城市往往比邻近基岩地带受损严重得多?

Given one and the same earthquake, why do cities founded on deep soft-sediment basins usually fare far worse than districts on nearby bedrock?

软沉积层的剪切波速远低于基岩,地震波进入盆地后振幅被显著放大,长周期成分尤为突出。盆地的几何形态还会圈闭波动能量并造成多次反射,使强震动持续更久。当建筑的自振周期恰好落在放大的长周期范围内时,共振进一步放大反应——1985年墨西哥城的惨重震害正是这一机制的典型例证。
Soft sediments carry shear waves far more slowly than bedrock, so motions entering a basin are strongly amplified, with their long-period components boosted most. Basin geometry also traps seismic energy through repeated reflections, stretching out the duration of strong shaking. When buildings resonate with the amplified long-period motion, structural response grows still larger—the devastating damage in Mexico City in 1985 is the canonical demonstration.

What is a tsunami, and how is it related to undersea earthquakes?

什么是地震面波？

What are surface seismic waves?

面波由体波到达地表后激发，比最慢的S波还慢，因而震图上来得最晚、拖尾最长。能量锁在浅表、只朝水平扩散，不像体波在三维摊开，故随距离衰减慢，远震那种悠长晃动多半出自它。形态两种：勒夫波水平剪切、地面左右扭动；瑞利波质点在竖直面内画椭圆、如同滚浪。它振幅大、周期长，是低层房屋破坏的主因；但随深度衰减，深源地震仍以体波为主。
Surface waves are generated where body waves reach the free surface. They travel slower than even the slowest S-wave, so they arrive last on a seismogram and trail the longest. Their energy is trapped near the surface and spreads only horizontally instead of through three dimensions, so they weaken with distance far more slowly; the long rolling shake of a distant quake is mostly theirs. There are two forms: Love waves are horizontal shear, the ground wriggling side to side, while Rayleigh waves make particles trace ellipses in the vertical plane, like rolling surf. Their amplitude is large and their period long, which is what does most damage to low-rise buildings, but they die out with depth, so records of deep earthquakes are still dominated by body waves.

为什么地震常发生在夜间或凌晨的说法不科学？

Why is the claim that earthquakes often strike at night unscientific?

统计上地震在一天中的发生时间基本随机，与昼夜无关。人们觉得多在夜间，常因夜间安静更易察觉、记忆更深刻，属认知偏差而非真实规律。
Statistically, earthquakes occur roughly randomly through the day, unrelated to day or night. The impression they strike at night comes from quieter nights making them more noticeable and memorable, a cognitive bias rather than a real pattern.

地震仪是如何记录地震的？

How does a seismograph record earthquakes?

地震仪用悬挂的重锤配合固定基座，地面震动时基座随动而重锤因惯性基本不动，二者相对位移被记录下来形成地震波曲线。多个台站联合就能反推震源位置与震级。
A seismograph suspends a mass above a fixed base; when the ground shakes, the base moves but the mass tends to stay put by inertia, and their relative motion is recorded as a wave trace. Networks of stations then locate the source and estimate magnitude.

Does a fault always produce earthquakes?

地球的内核是固体还是液体？我们怎么知道？

Is Earth's inner core solid or liquid, and how do we know?

内核是固态的铁镍合金，外核是液态铁。主要证据：S波能穿过内核却无法穿过外核，而S波不能在液体中传播，说明外核为液态；P波穿过内核的传播时间随路径方向变化（各向异性），且地球自转的长周期变化与内核和地幔的差速旋转有关。
The inner core is solid iron–nickel, the outer core liquid iron. Key evidence: S waves pass through the inner core but not the outer core—S waves cannot travel through liquids, so the outer core must be liquid; P-wave travel times through the inner core vary with path direction (anisotropy), and long-term changes in Earth's rotation relate to differential rotation between the inner core and the mantle.

什么是“弹性回跳说”（elastic rebound theory）？

What is the 'elastic rebound theory' of earthquakes?

弹性回跳说由里德（Harry Reid）在研究1906年旧金山大地震后于1910年提出：地壳因板块挤压而缓慢变形、积累弹性应变，一旦超过岩石强度便突然断裂错动，释放的能量形成地震。它解释了断层两侧为什么会在震后“弹回”原状。
Proposed by Harry Reid after the 1906 San Francisco earthquake (1910), elastic rebound theory holds that crustal rocks deform slowly under stress, storing elastic strain until they break and slip suddenly, releasing energy as an earthquake. It explains why fault blocks 'spring back' to their original positions after a quake.

地震预警系统为什么能在地震波到达前几秒到几十秒发出警报？

How can earthquake early warning systems alert people seconds to tens of seconds before strong shaking arrives?

地震会发出速度不同的两种波：纵波（P波）跑得快但破坏小，横波（S波）跑得慢但破坏大。预警系统用靠近震中的台站先检测到P波，随即自动估算震级和位置，通过电信网络向更远地区广播警报，因电信号速度远快于S波，警报可比破坏性震动早到几秒到几十秒，足够人们躲避或让列车停车。由于从震中向外传播的S波与警报信息在竞赛，震中附近存在无法提前预警的盲区，系统只能提供有限的时间窗口。
Earthquakes radiate fast but gentle P-waves and slower, destructive S-waves. An early warning network detects the P-wave at stations near the epicenter, quickly estimates magnitude and location, and broadcasts alerts by telecommunications; because electrical signals outpace the S-waves by a huge margin, alerts arrive seconds to tens of seconds before the damaging shaking—enough to take cover or stop trains. Because the S-waves and the alert race outward from the same source, a blind zone exists near the epicenter and the warning window is necessarily limited.

里氏震级（Richter）和矩震级（moment magnitude）有什么不同？

How does the Richter scale differ from moment magnitude?

里氏震级由查尔斯·里克特于1935年提出，适用于地方性较小地震，对极大地震会“饱和”而低估。现代科学界改用矩震级（Mw），它基于地震矩（断层面积×滑移量×岩石刚度），能更准确地描述大型地震，二者均为对数尺度。
The Richter scale, introduced by Charles Richter in 1935, works well for local, smaller quakes but 'saturates' and underestimates very large ones. Scientists now use moment magnitude (Mw), based on seismic moment (fault area × slip × rock rigidity), which describes big earthquakes more accurately; both are logarithmic scales.

地震"震级"和"烈度"是一回事吗？

Are earthquake magnitude and intensity the same thing?

不是。震级衡量地震释放的能量，用仪器测得，一次地震只有一个震级；烈度描述地表和建筑受破坏的程度，随距离震中的远近而不同。烈度还受震源深度和当地地质条件影响，同一地震在不同地方烈度差别很大。我国采用十二级烈度表，而震级常用里氏或矩震级表示。
No. Magnitude measures the energy released, is determined from instruments, and a single earthquake has just one magnitude. Intensity describes the shaking and damage at the surface and varies with distance from the epicenter; it also depends on focal depth and local geology, so the same quake can produce very different intensities in different places. China uses a 12-grade intensity scale, while magnitude is commonly given as local (Richter) or moment magnitude.

地震波中的P波和S波分别是什么？它们有何区别？

What are P-waves and S-waves in earthquakes, and how do they differ?

P波（初波）是纵波，质点前后振动、传播最快，能通过固体、液体和气体。S波（次波）是横波，质点上下/左右振动、较慢，且只能穿过固体、不能通过液体。二者到达的时差常被用来估算震中距离。
P-waves (primary) are compressional waves that vibrate along the direction of travel, move fastest, and pass through solids, liquids, and gases. S-waves (secondary) are shear waves that vibrate perpendicular to travel, are slower, and can travel only through solids—not liquids. Their arrival time difference is used to estimate distance to the epicenter.

建在回填土和古河道之上的城区,为什么会在同等地震下付出比周边重得多的代价?

Why do districts built on artificial fill and buried river channels pay a far heavier price in the very same earthquake than surrounding areas?

松散沉积层剪切波速低、卓越周期长,会把基岩传来的震动选择性放大;当放大的频段与高层建筑的自振周期吻合时,共振使楼层晃动幅度剧增。1985年墨西哥城地震震中距市区约三百五十公里,湖相软土的长周期放大却令市中心高楼大面积倒塌,此后各国普遍要求按场地类别调整设计反应谱。
Loose sediments have low shear-wave velocities and long characteristic periods, selectively amplifying incoming motions; when the amplified band matches a building's natural period, resonance sharply multiplies floor accelerations. The 1985 Mexico City event struck from roughly 350 km away, yet long-period amplification in the old lakebed toppled mid-rise towers downtown — prompting codes worldwide to tune design spectra to site class.

什么是震级和烈度？

What are magnitude and intensity?

震级衡量地震释放能量的大小，如里氏震级，每增加1级能量约增32倍。烈度描述某地实际感受到的破坏程度，与距离震中和地质条件有关，同一地震不同地点烈度不同。
Magnitude measures the energy released, as in the Richter scale, where each step up means about 32 times more energy. Intensity describes the actual felt shaking and damage at a place, depending on distance from the epicenter and local geology, so one quake has different intensities in different places.

地震来临前可以用什么手段预警？

What methods can give early warning before an earthquake?

地震预警利用地震波中速度快但破坏小的P波先到达、再抢在破坏性S波之前发出警报。虽然无法预测地震何时发生，但可在震后数秒到数十秒内向远处发出预警。
Earthquake early warning uses the fact that fast but less damaging P-waves arrive before destructive S-waves to issue alerts. It cannot predict when a quake will strike, but can warn distant areas seconds to tens of seconds after one begins.

地震是怎么产生的？

What causes earthquakes?

地震多发生在板块边界，地壳中的岩层长期受力变形，当应力超过岩石强度时突然断裂错动，释放的能量以地震波传向四周。地下核爆或水库蓄水也能诱发地震。
Most earthquakes occur at plate boundaries, where rocks strained by long-term forces suddenly fracture and slip, releasing energy that travels outward as seismic waves. Underground nuclear tests or reservoir filling can also trigger them.

地震波还没到手机先响了,预警系统到底在和什么赛跑?

Your phone beeps before the shaking arrives — what race is an earthquake early-warning system actually running?

三场赛跑同时进行。第一场与破坏性S波赛跑:P波以约6–7公里每秒先行,S波与面波速度慢一半以上、才是晃动主力,两者时间差随距离拉大,离震中越远预警窗口越长。第二场与电波赛跑:无线电比地震波快数万倍,台网数秒内定位定级后,警报可抢在强震动前送达上百公里外。第三场与自己赛跑:最初几秒P波信息有限,系统须权衡快报与误报——墨西哥城与东京的实践证明,几十秒足够让高铁减速、电梯平层。
Three races at once. First, against destructive S waves: primary P waves race ahead at six to seven kilometers per second while S and surface waves lag by half or more, so cities far from the epicenter get longer warning windows. Second, against radio: signals travel tens of thousands of times faster than seismic waves, letting networks locate the event within seconds and alert places a hundred kilometers away before strong motion arrives. Third, against itself: early P waves carry limited information, so systems trade speed against false alarms. Mexico City and Tokyo show tens of seconds suffice to slow trains and level elevators.
