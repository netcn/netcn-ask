为什么低收入国家更需要"基层医疗"而不是更多大医院？

Why do low-income countries need primary care more than more big hospitals?

疾病负担中占比最大的往往是可预防或可早期处理的问题：产前保健、儿童腹泻与肺炎、疫苗接种、高血压与糖尿病管理。基层网络能以较低成本覆盖更多人口、更早发现问题，减少昂贵住院。1978年《阿拉木图宣言》确立初级卫生保健为核心策略，这也是"全民健康覆盖"的基础。
The largest share of disease burden is usually preventable or manageable early: antenatal care, childhood diarrhoea and pneumonia, immunisation, hypertension and diabetes control. Primary care networks reach more people at lower cost and catch problems sooner, avoiding expensive admissions. The 1978 Alma-Ata Declaration made primary health care the central strategy, and it underpins universal health coverage today.

仿制药如何影响药品的全球可及性？

How do generic drugs shape global access to medicines?

专利保护给创新药一段独占期以回收研发成本，期满后仿制药进入市场，价格常下降到原研药的一小部分。21世纪初印度等国生产的抗艾滋病仿制药把年治疗费用从上万美元降到百美元级，是治疗规模化的转折点。仿制药需通过生物等效性审评，其活性成分与疗效应与原研一致。
Patents give innovator drugs a period of exclusivity to recoup R&D costs; once they expire, generics enter and prices often fall to a small fraction of the original. In the early 2000s HIV generics from India and elsewhere cut annual treatment costs from tens of thousands of dollars to the low hundreds, a turning point for scaling up treatment. Generics must pass bioequivalence review so their active ingredient and effect match the original.

为什么抗生素耐药被称为"全球性"危机？

Why is antibiotic resistance called a global crisis?

耐药基因不受国界限制：细菌可通过质粒在不同菌种间水平转移耐药性，人员流动、食品贸易与畜牧业用药让耐药株迅速扩散。任何一地的滥用都会削弱所有人的药物库，而新抗生素研发缓慢、商业激励不足。应对需要处方管理、感染控制、农业限用与国际监测网络共同推进。
Resistance genes ignore borders: bacteria transfer resistance horizontally on plasmids between species, and travel, food trade and livestock antibiotic use spread resistant strains fast. Misuse anywhere erodes everyone's drug arsenal, while new antibiotic development is slow and commercially unattractive. The response needs prescription stewardship, infection control, agricultural limits and international surveillance together.

脊髓灰质炎接近根除多年，为什么最后一步这么难？

Polio has been near eradication for years — why is the last step so hard?

多数感染者无症状，一例瘫痪病例背后可能有数百例隐性感染，监测必须依靠环境污水检测与主动搜索；口服疫苗的减毒病毒在低接种率社区可能变异为致病的疫苗衍生毒株；剩余流行区常伴冲突、人口流动和对接种的不信任，接种队伍甚至面临安全风险。
Most infections are asymptomatic — one paralytic case can mean hundreds of silent ones — so surveillance depends on environmental sewage testing and active case search. The live oral vaccine's attenuated virus can, in under-vaccinated communities, mutate into circulating vaccine-derived strains. Remaining endemic areas also face conflict, population movement and distrust, with vaccination teams sometimes at risk.

什么是"被忽视的热带病"？

What are neglected tropical diseases?

世界卫生组织列出的一组主要影响低收入地区的疾病，如血吸虫病、淋巴滤过性肿（象皮病）、沙眼、登革热、利什曼病、土壤传播蠕虫病。它们致残致贫却研发投入长期不足，因为患者购买力低、商业回报有限。大规模药物投放、载体控制和清洁水卫生是主要应对手段。
A WHO-listed group of diseases affecting mainly low-income regions — schistosomiasis, lymphatic filariasis, trachoma, dengue, leishmaniasis, soil-transmitted helminths and more. They cause disability and poverty yet long attracted little R&D because affected populations have low purchasing power and commercial returns are limited. Mass drug administration, vector control and clean water and sanitation are the main responses.

为什么血吸虫病的流行与特定淡水钉螺宿主密切相关？

Why is schistosomiasis spread so tightly linked to specific freshwater snail hosts?

血吸虫的幼虫（尾蚴）必须在特定种类的淡水螺体内发育到感染阶段，人才在接触含尾蚴的水体时经皮肤感染。没有中间宿主螺，寄生虫就无法完成生活史，因此螺的地理分布直接决定疾病流行范围。控制策略常通过灭螺、改善灌溉和提供安全用水来切断传播。
Schistosome larvae (cercariae) must develop inside particular freshwater snail species before they can infect people through the skin in contaminated water. Without the right snail host the parasite cannot complete its life cycle, so snail geography sets the disease's range. Control targets snails, safer water and better irrigation to break transmission.

为什么说很多传染病其实是"贫困病"？

Why are many infectious diseases called "diseases of poverty"?

拥挤住房便于呼吸道传播，缺乏清洁水与厕所导致粪口传播，营养不良削弱免疫力，医疗可及性差使确诊与治疗延迟，而疾病又造成收入损失，形成恶性循环。结核、疟疾、肠道寄生虫的地理分布因此与经济水平高度重叠。这也说明改善住房、营养与基本医疗本身就是防疫措施。
Crowded housing eases respiratory spread, lack of clean water and toilets enables faecal-oral transmission, malnutrition weakens immunity, poor access delays diagnosis and treatment, and illness then destroys income — a vicious cycle. That is why the geography of tuberculosis, malaria and intestinal parasites tracks economic conditions closely, and why better housing, nutrition and primary care are themselves infection control.

空气污染对健康的影响主要有哪些？

What are the main health effects of air pollution?

细颗粒物PM2.5能进入肺泡甚至血液，长期暴露与哮喘、慢性阻塞性肺病、肺癌、心肌梗死、中风、儿童肺发育受损和不良妊娠结局相关。世界卫生组织估计每年数百万例早死与室内外空气污染有关，其中相当部分来自使用固体燃料做饭取暖。改善燃料、通风与交通排放是关键干预。
Fine particles (PM2.5) reach the alveoli and even the bloodstream; long-term exposure is linked to asthma, COPD, lung cancer, heart attack, stroke, impaired lung development in children and adverse pregnancy outcomes. WHO attributes millions of premature deaths a year to outdoor and household air pollution, a large share from solid fuels used for cooking and heating. Cleaner fuels, ventilation and transport emission controls are key interventions.

天花是唯一被消灭的人类传染病，靠的是什么？

Smallpox is the only eradicated human disease — how was it done?

关键条件包括：只感染人类、没有动物宿主；症状明显易识别；有效且耐热的疫苗；以及"环围接种"策略——发现病例后迅速给其接触者与周边人群接种，切断传播链。再加上跨越冷战阵营的国际合作，1980年世界卫生组织宣布天花被消灭。
Key conditions: it infected only humans with no animal reservoir; the rash was distinctive and easy to spot; an effective heat-tolerant vaccine existed; and ring vaccination — rapidly immunising contacts and surrounding communities around each case — broke transmission chains. With international cooperation that crossed Cold War lines, WHO declared eradication in 1980.

为什么清洁饮水和厕所被视为最有效的"医疗"投入之一？

Why are clean water and toilets considered among the most effective health investments?

腹泻病曾是儿童死亡的主要原因之一，多由粪口途径传播的病原引起。安全供水、卫生厕所与洗手设施能同时阻断霍乱、痢疾、甲肝、伤寒和多种寄生虫病，还能减少营养不良与生长迟缓。19世纪伦敦通过改善供水控制霍乱，就是公共卫生胜过个体治疗的经典案例。
Diarrhoeal disease was long a leading cause of child death, mostly from faecal-oral pathogens. Safe water, sanitary toilets and handwashing facilities simultaneously block cholera, dysentery, hepatitis A, typhoid and many parasites, and reduce malnutrition and stunting. Nineteenth-century London controlling cholera by fixing its water supply is the classic case of public health outperforming individual treatment.

精神疾病的"病耻感"为什么会影响治疗效果？

How does stigma around mental illness affect outcomes?

病耻感让人推迟或放弃求助，隐瞒症状，也更容易中断用药与随访；求助时间越晚，功能损害与住院风险越高。它还影响就业、社交与自我评价，形成"自我病耻"。用描述行为而非标签的语言、公开谈论康复经历、把心理服务纳入基层医疗，都是被证明有效的减少病耻感的做法。
Stigma delays or prevents help-seeking, encourages hiding symptoms, and makes people more likely to drop medication and follow-up; later help-seeking means more functional impairment and higher hospitalisation risk. It also affects employment, relationships and self-image, producing internalised stigma. Using behaviour-describing rather than labelling language, sharing recovery stories, and integrating mental health into primary care are proven ways to reduce it.

结核病治疗为什么要连续吃药好几个月？

Why does tuberculosis treatment take many months of pills?

结核分枝杆菌生长缓慢、细胞壁富含脂质，还能在巨噬细胞内和肉芽肿中处于代谢低下的"休眠"状态，短期用药只能杀死活跃菌群。因此标准方案需多药联合并持续约6个月，以清除持留菌、防止复发与耐药。中断治疗是产生耐多药结核的主要原因之一。
Mycobacterium tuberculosis grows slowly, has a lipid-rich wall, and can persist in a low-metabolism dormant state inside macrophages and granulomas, so short courses only kill the actively dividing population. Standard regimens therefore combine several drugs for about six months to clear persisters and prevent relapse and resistance. Interrupted treatment is a major driver of multidrug-resistant TB.

为什么有些地区疫苗接种率明显偏低？

Why is vaccination coverage much lower in some places?

原因通常是多层的：供应与资金不足、冷链和交通困难、卫生人员短缺、战乱或流离失所打断服务，以及信息不足与信任缺失带来的疫苗犹豫。改善需要供给端（可及性、成本）和需求端（沟通、社区参与）同时着力，单靠宣传或单靠捐赠都不够。
Reasons are usually layered: limited supply and funding, cold chain and transport barriers, shortages of health workers, conflict or displacement interrupting services, plus vaccine hesitancy from poor information and low trust. Progress needs both supply-side work (access, cost) and demand-side work (communication, community engagement) — campaigns or donations alone are not enough.

疟疾为什么还没有被消灭？

Why hasn't malaria been eliminated?

疟疾由疟原虫经蚊子传播，防控要同时对付寄生虫、蚊子和人类行为。疟原虫已对多种药物（包括部分地区的青蒿素类）产生耐药，蚊子对杀虫剂耐受性上升；疟原虫复杂的生命周期使疫苗研发困难，现有疫苗保护率有限。同时高负担地区卫生系统资源紧张。中国在2021年获认证消除疟疾，说明持续投入可以成功。
Malaria is a parasite spread by mosquitoes, so control must tackle parasite, vector and human behaviour at once. Parasites have developed resistance to several drugs, including partial artemisinin resistance in some regions, and mosquitoes to insecticides; the parasite's complex life cycle makes vaccines hard, and current ones offer limited protection. High-burden countries also face stretched health systems. China was certified malaria-free in 2021, showing sustained effort can succeed.
