腹腔镜（微创）手术的优势和局限是什么？

What are the advantages and limits of laparoscopic (minimally invasive) surgery?

腹腔镜通过几个小切口置入摄像头和器械，并向腹腔充入二氧化碳建立操作空间。与开腹手术相比，它的切口小、疼痛轻、住院时间短、切口感染和疝的发生率低，患者恢复日常活动更快。局限在于设备和培训成本高、学习曲线长，触觉反馈减少；对严重腹腔粘连、大量出血或血流动力学不稳定的病人并不适合，术中必要时需转为开腹手术，这属于安全考虑而非失败。
Laparoscopy inserts a camera and instruments through a few small incisions and inflates the abdomen with carbon dioxide to create working space. Compared with open surgery it means smaller wounds, less pain, shorter hospital stays, fewer wound infections and hernias, and a faster return to normal activity. Its limits are costly equipment and training, a long learning curve and reduced tactile feedback; it is unsuitable for dense adhesions, heavy bleeding or haemodynamically unstable patients, and converting to an open operation mid-procedure is a safety decision rather than a failure.

手术前为什么要禁食禁水？

Why must you fast before surgery?

全身麻醉会抑制咽喉部的保护性反射，胃内容物一旦反流就可能被吸入肺内，造成严重的化学性肺炎，这是麻醉的重要风险之一。现代指南并不主张“前一晚起什么都不能碰”：清饮料一般可饮至术前2小时，母乳4小时，清淡固体食物6小时。过长时间禁食反而带来脱水、低血糖、口渴烦躁，儿童和老年人尤其明显，因此应按麻醉医生的具体交代执行。
General anaesthesia suppresses the protective reflexes of the throat, so if stomach contents reflux they can be inhaled into the lungs and cause severe chemical pneumonitis - one of anaesthesia's important risks. Modern guidelines do not demand nothing at all from the night before: clear fluids are generally allowed until two hours before, breast milk until four hours, and a light solid meal until six hours. Excessively long fasting brings dehydration, low blood sugar, thirst and distress, especially in children and older adults, so follow the specific instructions of your anaesthetist.

全身麻醉和局部麻醉的根本区别是什么？

What is the fundamental difference between general and local anaesthesia?

全身麻醉通过药物作用于中枢神经系统，产生可逆的意识丧失、遗忘、镇痛和肌肉松弛，患者失去自主呼吸保护能力，因此必须建立气道、机械通气并全程监测生命体征。局部与区域麻醉（如浸润麻醉、神经阻滞、椎管内麻醉）主要阻断钠离子通道，使某一区域的神经信号无法上传，患者可以保持清醒。选择取决于手术部位与时长、患者合并症和偏好，两类麻醉的风险谱也不同，由麻醉医生综合评估。
General anaesthesia uses drugs acting on the central nervous system to produce reversible unconsciousness, amnesia, analgesia and muscle relaxation; the patient loses the ability to protect their own breathing, so the airway must be secured, ventilation supported and vital signs monitored throughout. Local and regional techniques - infiltration, nerve blocks, spinal or epidural anaesthesia - mainly block sodium channels so nerve signals from one region cannot get through, and the patient can stay awake. The choice depends on the site and length of surgery, comorbidities and preference, and because the risk profiles differ, an anaesthetist weighs them together.
