先导编辑（prime editing）能做什么传统 CRISPR 做不到的事？

What can prime editing do that classic CRISPR cannot?

先导编辑用改造的 Cas9 和一段 pegRNA 直接写入新的 DNA 序列，可做点突变、小片段插入或删除，而不依赖 DNA 双链断裂，灵活度和精度更高。
Prime editing uses a modified Cas9 plus a pegRNA to write new DNA sequences—point mutations, small insertions, or deletions—without relying on double-strand breaks, offering more versatility and precision.

碱基编辑（base editing）与 CRISPR 有何不同？

How does base editing differ from CRISPR?

传统 CRISPR 靠切断 DNA 双链来编辑，而碱基编辑不切断双链，直接用酶把单个碱基“改写”（如 C→T 或 A→G），更像精准的笔误修正，有望降低意外损伤的风险。
Classic CRISPR cuts both DNA strands to edit, while base editors do not cut them; they use an enzyme to directly rewrite a single base (e.g., C→T or A→G), like precise typo correction, potentially lowering unintended damage.

mRNA 癌症疫苗的原理是什么？

How do mRNA cancer vaccines work?

这类疫苗把肿瘤特有的“新抗原”对应的 mRNA 注入体内，训练免疫系统识别并攻击表达这些抗原的癌细胞；目前多用于黑色素瘤等恶性肿瘤的个性化辅助治疗。
These vaccines inject mRNA encoding tumor-specific “neoantigens” so the immune system learns to recognize and attack cancer cells carrying them; they are studied mainly as personalized adjunct therapy in cancers like melanoma.

诱导多能干细胞（iPSC）为什么重要？

Why are induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) important?

iPSC 是把成年体细胞“重编程”回类似胚胎干细胞的状态，能再分化为多种细胞。它绕开了胚胎干细胞的伦理争议，并可用于个体化药物测试和疾病建模。
iPSCs are adult cells reprogrammed to an embryo-like state, able to become many cell types. They avoid embryo ethics concerns and enable personalized drug testing and disease modeling.

CRISPR 基因编辑是怎样“剪切”DNA 的？

How does CRISPR gene editing “cut” DNA?

CRISPR-Cas9 用一段引导 RNA 找到目标 DNA 序列，再由 Cas9 蛋白像分子剪刀一样切断 DNA 双链；细胞修复断口时可能引入或修正基因。它用于科研与作物改良，治疗应用仍在谨慎推进。
CRISPR-Cas9 uses a guide RNA to find a target DNA sequence, and the Cas9 protein cuts both strands like molecular scissors; cellular repair of the break can disrupt or correct a gene. It is used in research and crops, with therapeutic use advancing cautiously.

什么是精准医疗（precision medicine）？

What is precision medicine?

精准医疗根据个体的基因、环境和生活方式来预防和治疗疾病，例如用基因检测结果选择更可能有效的靶向药，而不是“一刀切”的方案。
Precision medicine tailors prevention and treatment to an individual's genes, environment, and lifestyle—e.g., using genetic testing to pick targeted drugs more likely to work, rather than one-size-fits-all approaches.

什么是 CAR-T 细胞免疫疗法？

What is CAR-T cell immunotherapy?

CAR-T 是把患者自身的 T 细胞取出，在实验室装上识别癌细胞的“嵌合抗原受体”后再回输体内，让免疫细胞精准攻击肿瘤。它已用于某些白血病和淋巴瘤。
In CAR-T therapy, a patient's own T cells are collected, engineered in the lab to carry a chimeric antigen receptor that recognizes cancer cells, then infused back to attack tumors. It is used for some leukemias and lymphomas.

什么是 siRNA 和小干扰 RNA 药物？

What are siRNA and small interfering RNA drugs?

siRNA 是一小段 RNA，能与目标信使 RNA 结合并促其降解，从而在“翻译之前”关闭某个基因的表达。基于它的药物已用于治疗某些遗传性转甲状腺素蛋白淀粉样变性。
siRNA is a short RNA that binds a target messenger RNA and triggers its destruction, silencing a gene before it is translated into protein. Such drugs are used for some hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis.

药物基因组学如何影响用药？

How does pharmacogenomics influence medication use?

药物基因组学研究基因差异怎样改变人对药物的反应，例如某些人代谢特定药物过快或过慢，据此调整剂量可减少副作用、提高疗效。
Pharmacogenomics studies how genetic differences change drug response—e.g., some people metabolize a drug too fast or too slowly—so dosing can be adjusted to reduce side effects and improve efficacy.

什么是类器官（organoid）？

What is an organoid?

类器官是在实验室里用干细胞培养出的、能模拟真实器官结构和功能的小型三维组织，可用于药物筛选、发育研究和罕见病机制探索。
Organoids are tiny 3D tissues grown from stem cells that mimic the structure and function of real organs, used for drug screening, development studies, and rare-disease mechanisms.

什么是 mRNA 技术？它只能用来做疫苗吗？

What is mRNA technology, and is it only for vaccines?

mRNA（信使核糖核酸）是把遗传指令送到细胞里、让细胞临时生产特定蛋白的技术。除新冠疫苗外，它正被研究用于癌症疫苗、蛋白质替代疗法和罕见病治疗。
mRNA (messenger RNA) delivers genetic instructions that make cells temporarily produce a specific protein. Beyond COVID vaccines, it is being studied for cancer vaccines, protein-replacement therapy, and rare-disease treatment.

什么是再生医学？

What is regenerative medicine?

再生医学试图用干细胞、组织工程和生物材料修复或替换受损的组织与器官，例如培养皮肤用于烧伤、用患者细胞造“类器官”来模拟疾病。
Regenerative medicine aims to repair or replace damaged tissues and organs using stem cells, tissue engineering, and biomaterials—e.g., lab-grown skin for burns, or “organoids” from a patient's cells to model disease.
