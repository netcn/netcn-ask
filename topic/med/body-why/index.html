癌细胞和正常细胞的关键区别是什么？

What are the key differences between cancer cells and normal cells?

癌变往往源于原癌基因或抑癌基因（如p53）的累积突变。癌细胞失去接触抑制，能无限增殖、逃避凋亡，还诱导新生血管供给养分，并可脱离原发部位转移。免疫系统能清除一部分异常细胞，这就是"免疫监视"的概念。
Cancer usually arises from accumulated mutations in oncogenes or tumor suppressors such as p53. Cancer cells lose contact inhibition, proliferate indefinitely, evade apoptosis, recruit new blood vessels for nutrients, and can spread to distant sites. The immune system clears many abnormal cells along the way — a concept known as immune surveillance.

器官移植后为什么会排斥？如何控制？

Why does organ rejection happen, and how is it controlled?

受者免疫系统把供者器官细胞表面的HLA（人类白细胞抗原）分子识别为“外来”，CD4+T细胞被激活后辅助B细胞产生抗体并激活杀伤性T细胞，攻击移植物，这就是排斥反应。临床对策有三层：术前HLA配型尽量匹配，术中术后用免疫抑制剂（钙调磷酸酶抑制剂、糖皮质激素、抗增殖药）抑制T细胞活化，长期监测以处理慢性排斥。急性排斥多数可逆；慢性排斥常与抗体介导的血管损伤有关，是移植物远期丢失的主因。
The recipient's immune system recognizes donor HLA (human leukocyte antigen) molecules on graft cells as foreign; CD4+ T cells are activated and help B cells make antibodies while activating CD8+ killers, which attack the graft—this is rejection. The clinical strategy has three layers: HLA matching before surgery to minimize mismatch, immunosuppressants (calcineurin inhibitors, glucocorticoids, antiproliferatives) to blunt T-cell activation, and long-term monitoring for chronic rejection. Acute rejection is mostly reversible; chronic rejection, often driven by antibody-mediated vascular injury, is the leading cause of late graft loss.

磁共振成像是靠什么"看见"软组织内部的？

What lets MRI "see" inside soft tissues?

人体含大量水分子，其中的氢质子像小磁针。MRI用强磁场让它们整齐排列，再用射频脉冲把质子激发到高能态；停止脉冲后质子按各自的"弛豫时间"（T1、T2）恢复，不同组织的T1/T2长短不同，计算机据此生成对比鲜明的图像。由于软组织的含水量和质子环境差异大，MRI在脑、脊髓、关节和软组织病变的显示上尤为突出；它没有电离辐射，主要考虑的是强磁场带来的安全禁忌（如体内金属植入物）。
The body is full of water, and its hydrogen protons behave like tiny magnets. MRI aligns them with a strong magnetic field, then flips them into a high-energy state with a radiofrequency pulse; when the pulse stops, protons relax at different rates (T1, T2), and tissues differ in these relaxation times, letting the computer build a high-contrast image. Because soft tissues differ widely in water content and proton environment, MRI is especially good at showing the brain, spinal cord, joints, and other soft-tissue lesions; it uses no ionizing radiation, with the main concerns being magnetic-field safety rules such as implant restrictions.

免疫系统为什么会"攻击自己"？

Why does the immune system sometimes attack the body itself?

正常情况下，发育中的T细胞和B细胞会在胸腺和骨髓里接受"教育"，自我反应的细胞被清除或抑制，这叫免疫耐受。耐受机制失效后，自身反应性淋巴细胞被激活，误将自身蛋白当作敌人攻击，便出现类风湿关节炎、1型糖尿病、桥本甲状腺炎等自身免疫病。遗传易感性和环境因素（如感染、药物）共同参与触发，治疗多围绕抑制异常免疫反应展开。
Normally, developing T and B cells are educated in the thymus and bone marrow, where self-reactive cells are deleted or suppressed — a process called immune tolerance. When tolerance fails, self-reactive lymphocytes become activated and attack self-proteins, producing diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, type 1 diabetes, and Hashimoto's thyroiditis. Genetic susceptibility plus environmental triggers such as infections or drugs participate, and treatment generally focuses on dampening the aberrant immune response.

血糖是怎样被胰岛激素精确调节的？

How do pancreatic hormones precisely regulate blood glucose?

血糖升高时胰岛β细胞分泌胰岛素，促进肌肉和脂肪细胞摄取葡萄糖、促进糖原与脂肪合成，使血糖回落；血糖降低时α细胞分泌胰高血糖素，促进肝糖原分解和糖异生，使血糖回升。胰岛素与胰高血糖素作用相反，二者的比例决定肝脏输出葡萄糖的方向。交感兴奋和皮质醇、肾上腺素在应激时也协同升糖。这套系统失灵就表现为糖尿病。
Rising glucose makes β cells secrete insulin, which drives muscle and fat cells to take up glucose and promotes glycogen and fat synthesis, bringing glucose down; falling glucose makes α cells secrete glucagon, which stimulates hepatic glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis to raise it. Insulin and glucagon oppose each other, and their ratio determines whether the liver releases or stores glucose. Sympathetic activation plus cortisol and adrenaline also raise glucose during stress. When this system fails, diabetes results.

血压是怎么升高的？为什么高血压常被称为"沉默杀手"？

How does blood pressure rise, and why is hypertension called a "silent killer"?

血压约等于心输出量乘以外周阻力。血管变硬变窄、肾素-血管紧张素系统过度激活、盐摄入过多等原因都会使阻力升高，血压随之上升。早期高血压几乎没有症状，但它长期损伤心、脑、肾和视网膜血管，等到出现症状时往往已有器官损害。
Blood pressure roughly equals cardiac output times peripheral resistance. Stiffer and narrower arteries, an overactive renin–angiotensin system, and excess salt all raise resistance and push pressure up. Early hypertension is symptom-free, yet it silently damages the heart, brain, kidneys, and retinal vessels; by the time symptoms appear, organ damage is often already present.

为什么受伤当下常常不太疼，过后才疼起来？

Why does an injury often not hurt at first, with pain arriving later?

这是应激性镇痛：危险或剧烈应激时，脑干的下行抑制系统（如中脑周围灰质—延髓通路）被激活，释放内源性阿片肽压制脊髓的痛信号传递，让人先逃生或应对。等应激缓解、炎症介质逐渐积累，疼痛便显现并可能加重。这说明疼痛不是简单的"损伤计量表"，而是被神经系统主动调节的体验。
This is stress-induced analgesia: danger or intense stress activates brainstem descending inhibition (the periaqueductal grey–medulla pathway), releasing endogenous opioids that suppress pain transmission in the spinal cord so you can escape or cope first. As stress subsides and inflammatory mediators accumulate, pain emerges and may intensify. Pain is not a simple damage meter but an experience actively modulated by the nervous system.

神经信号在神经元之间是怎样传递的？突触是怎么工作的？

How does a nerve signal travel between neurons, and how does a synapse work?

动作电位到达突触前膜后，触发含神经递质的小泡与膜融合并释放递质（如乙酰胆碱、谷氨酸）。递质扩散穿过突触间隙，与突触后膜上的受体结合，打开离子通道，产生兴奋性或抑制性突触后电位。递质随后被酶降解或回收，保证信号单向、可控地传递。
When an action potential reaches the presynaptic terminal, vesicles fuse with the membrane and release neurotransmitters such as acetylcholine or glutamate. These diffuse across the synaptic cleft and bind receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, opening ion channels and generating an excitatory or inhibitory postsynaptic potential. Enzymatic breakdown and reuptake then clear the transmitter, keeping signaling one-way and controlled.

下丘脑-垂体-肾上腺（HPA）轴是如何工作的？

How does the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis work?

应激时下丘脑分泌CRH，促使垂体释放ACTH，ACTH再刺激肾上腺皮质合成皮质醇；皮质醇升高后反过来抑制CRH和ACTH的分泌，形成负反馈，让应激反应适度收尾。长期慢性应激或长期使用糖皮质激素会扰乱这一平衡，可能出现库欣样表现或肾上腺皮质抑制，这也是为什么激素类药物不能突然停用。
Under stress the hypothalamus releases CRH, prompting the pituitary to secrete ACTH, which drives the adrenal cortex to synthesize cortisol; rising cortisol then feeds back to suppress CRH and ACTH, closing the loop so the stress response tapers off. Chronic stress or long-term glucocorticoid use can disrupt this balance, producing Cushing-like features or adrenal suppression—which is why steroid drugs must never be stopped abruptly.

人工智能（AI）怎样帮助医生诊断疾病？

How does artificial intelligence (AI) help doctors diagnose disease?

深度学习模型在大量标注的医学影像上训练后，可以学习识别胸片上的病灶、病理切片中的肿瘤、眼底照片中的糖尿病视网膜病变等，部分研究显示其灵敏度与专科医生相当甚至更高。AI还能整合电子病历做风险预测、加速药物发现。当前它定位为“辅助工具”，可以降低漏诊率、提高效率，但诊断责任和最终决策仍由医生承担。
Trained on large sets of labeled medical images, deep-learning models learn to spot lesions on chest X-rays, tumors in pathology slides, and diabetic retinopathy in retinal photos; some studies report sensitivity on par with or above specialists. AI also integrates electronic records for risk prediction and accelerates drug discovery. Today it is positioned as an assistive tool that reduces missed diagnoses and raises efficiency, while the final decision and responsibility remain with physicians.

1型和2型糖尿病在机制上有什么不同？

How do type 1 and type 2 diabetes differ in mechanism?

1型糖尿病是自身免疫破坏了胰岛β细胞，导致胰岛素绝对缺乏，常在青少年期发病，必须终身依赖外源胰岛素。2型糖尿病以胰岛素抵抗为主——细胞对胰岛素的反应变钝，血糖难以进入细胞——并伴随β细胞分泌功能逐渐下降，与肥胖、久坐和遗传有关。因此2型患者往往可以先从饮食、运动和减重等生活方式干预入手，效果不足再升级到口服药或胰岛素。
Type 1 diabetes results from autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells, causing absolute insulin deficiency; it usually begins in youth and requires lifelong insulin. Type 2 diabetes is driven mainly by insulin resistance — cells respond sluggishly to insulin, so glucose cannot enter them easily — along with progressive beta-cell failure, and is linked to obesity, inactivity, and genetics. For this reason type 2 is often managed starting with diet, exercise, and weight loss, escalating to oral drugs or insulin only when needed.

为什么呼吸深浅比频率更重要？

Why does breathing depth matter more than frequency?

每次吸气约有150毫升停留在气道内不参与气体交换，称为解剖无效腔。真正到达肺泡参加换气的是“肺泡通气量”，等于（潮气量－无效腔）×呼吸频率。浅快呼吸时大量气量浪费在无效腔，即使每分钟总通气量不变，肺泡通气量也会明显下降甚至导致缺氧。这也是为什么呼吸窘迫时浅快呼吸比深慢呼吸更危险。
Each breath leaves about 150 mL in the airways where no gas exchange occurs—the anatomic dead space. What actually reaches the alveoli is alveolar ventilation, equal to (tidal volume − dead space) × respiratory rate. With rapid shallow breathing, a large fraction is wasted in dead space, so even if total minute ventilation stays the same, alveolar ventilation can fall sharply and cause hypoxia. That is why rapid shallow breathing is more dangerous than slow deep breathing in respiratory distress.

为什么甲状腺激素水平能保持稳定？

How does the body keep thyroid hormone levels stable?

下丘脑分泌TRH刺激垂体释放TSH，TSH再促使甲状腺分泌T3和T4；当血中T3、T4升高时，它们反过来抑制TRH和TSH的分泌。这一负反馈回路让甲状腺功能自动维持在合适范围。甲状腺功能检查（TSH）正是因为这套机制而非常敏感。
The hypothalamus releases TRH, which makes the pituitary secrete TSH, and TSH stimulates the thyroid to produce T3 and T4. When blood levels of T3 and T4 rise, they feed back to suppress TRH and TSH. This negative-feedback loop keeps thyroid function automatically within range, which is why TSH is such a sensitive thyroid function test.

幽门螺杆菌和胃溃疡有什么关系？

How is Helicobacter pylori related to stomach ulcers?

幽门螺杆菌是能在胃酸中生存的细菌，通过分泌尿素酶中和局部胃酸并损伤胃黏膜，是绝大多数消化性溃疡的病因，也与慢性胃炎和胃癌相关。多数感染者没有症状，检出且合并溃疡或高风险时，规范的“四联疗法”可以根除。治疗后复查确认根除能显著降低溃疡复发。
Helicobacter pylori is a bacterium that survives stomach acid by secreting urease to neutralize acid locally while damaging the gastric lining; it causes the majority of peptic ulcers and is linked to chronic gastritis and gastric cancer. Most infected people have no symptoms, but when found with an ulcer or high risk, standard quadruple therapy can eradicate it. A follow-up test confirming eradication greatly lowers ulcer recurrence.

为什么人突然站起来时会头晕眼花？

Why do people sometimes feel dizzy when they stand up suddenly?

站立时血液因重力暂时积聚在下肢，回心血量和血压短暂下降。颈动脉窦和主动脉弓的压力感受器感知血压降低后，通过自主神经反射加快心率并收缩血管，让血压在几秒内恢复。若这一反射较慢或代偿不足（如老年人、脱水或服用某些降压药时），就会出现眼前发黑甚至晕厥，医学上称为体位性低血压，起身时放慢速度可以有效预防。
When you stand, gravity briefly pools blood in the lower limbs, so venous return and blood pressure dip for a moment. Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch detect the drop and, via autonomic reflexes, speed up the heart and constrict vessels so blood pressure recovers within seconds. If this reflex is slow or insufficient—in older adults, dehydration, or with certain blood pressure medications—you may feel light-headed or even faint, a condition called orthostatic hypotension. Standing up slowly can help prevent it.

为什么长时间盘腿或压着手臂会"麻"？

Why does a limb go numb and tingly after sitting cross-legged too long?

持续压迫使局部神经的血液供应减少、神经传导暂时受阻，感觉信号变得混乱，出现麻木与针刺感。恢复供血后，神经末梢一起"重新上线"，产生密集的异常放电，就是那种发麻刺痒的感觉。偶尔发生无需担心，若频繁出现或伴肌力下降，应就医评估神经受压情况。
Sustained pressure reduces blood supply to a nerve and temporarily blocks conduction, so sensory signals become garbled and you feel numbness and pins and needles. When flow returns, nerve endings come back online together, firing abnormally in a burst — that prickly tingle. Occasional episodes are harmless, but frequent numbness or weakness deserves evaluation for nerve compression.

我们吃进去的脂肪是如何被消化吸收的？

How is dietary fat digested and absorbed?

脂肪先在胆盐的乳化作用下分散成小油滴，再由胰脂肪酶分解为脂肪酸和甘油一酯。这些产物与胆盐形成微胶粒，被小肠上皮细胞吸收后重新合成甘油三酯，组装成乳糜微粒，经淋巴系统最终进入血液。因此脂肪的消化吸收比碳水更复杂，速度也更慢。
Dietary fat is first emulsified into small droplets by bile salts, then broken down by pancreatic lipase into fatty acids and monoglycerides. Together with bile salts these form micelles that enter intestinal cells, where triglycerides are rebuilt and packaged into chylomicrons that travel via the lymph into the bloodstream. That is why fat digestion and absorption are more complex and slower than those of carbohydrates.

尿液是怎么在肾脏里形成的？

How does the kidney make urine?

血液流经肾小球时，水和小分子被滤出形成原尿；肾小管随后把葡萄糖、氨基酸和大部分水分重新吸收回血液，同时分泌一部分废物。健康人的尿液基本不含糖和蛋白质，这正是肾小管重吸收功能强大的体现。
As blood flows through the glomerulus, water and small molecules are filtered out to form a primary filtrate. Tubules then reabsorb glucose, amino acids, and most of the water back into the blood while secreting extra waste. Healthy urine contains essentially no sugar or protein — a sign of how efficient tubular reabsorption is.

手指碰到热水为什么会先缩回来，然后才感觉到烫？

Why does your hand pull back from hot water before you actually feel the heat?

这是一种脊髓反射。感受烫伤的信号沿感觉神经进入脊髓后，脊髓直接发出指令让手臂肌肉收缩，整个过程比信号传到大脑再下达命令快得多。与此同时信号继续上传大脑，所以你随后才产生"烫"的感觉。
This is a spinal reflex. The heat signal travels up a sensory nerve to the spinal cord, which immediately sends a command for your arm muscles to pull away — much faster than routing the signal up to the brain and back. The signal also continues to the brain, which is why you feel the heat a moment later.

心房颤动（房颤）是什么？为什么需要重视？

What is atrial fibrillation and why does it matter?

房颤时心房不再规律收缩，而是快速、不规则地颤动，导致心跳快慢不一。最大的风险是心房内血流淤滞形成血栓，血栓脱落随血流堵塞脑血管就会引起脑卒中。房颤患者通过抗凝治疗、控制心率和恢复节律，可以大幅降低卒中风险。
In atrial fibrillation the atria stop contracting rhythmically and quiver rapidly and irregularly, making the heartbeat uneven. The main danger is that stagnant blood in the atria can form clots; if a clot breaks off and blocks a brain vessel it causes a stroke. With anticoagulation, rate control, and rhythm management, stroke risk can be greatly reduced.

为什么得过一次水痘之后通常就不会再得？

Why do people who have had chickenpox usually never get it again?

初次感染时，免疫系统会生成专门识别水痘病毒的记忆B细胞和记忆T细胞。病毒再次入侵时，记忆细胞能在几天内迅速增殖并产生大量抗体，在病毒大量繁殖前就把它们清除。疫苗能带来类似的记忆，所以接种后也能获得保护。
During the first infection, the immune system creates memory B cells and T cells that recognize the chickenpox virus. If the virus returns, these memory cells multiply within days and produce antibodies that clear it before it can spread widely. Vaccines create the same kind of memory, which is why vaccination offers protection too.

血液中的“补体”是什么？它有什么作用？

What is the "complement" in blood, and what does it do?

补体是一组存在于血浆中的蛋白质，平时处于“待命”状态，遇到病原体或被抗体结合后会被逐级激活。激活后的补体主要有三件事可做：标记病原体让吞噬细胞吞掉（调理）、招募免疫细胞到感染部位、以及直接穿孔溶解细菌。它是固有免疫的重要防线，也帮助适应性免疫发挥作用。
Complement is a group of plasma proteins that stand by in the blood and are activated in a cascade when they encounter pathogens or antibody-antigen complexes. Once activated, complement does three main jobs: tags pathogens for phagocytes to engulf (opsonization), recruits immune cells to the infection site, and punches holes in bacterial membranes to kill them. It is a key arm of innate immunity and also assists adaptive immunity.

伤口愈合分哪几个阶段？

What are the stages of wound healing?

大致四步：止血（血管收缩、血小板聚集形成血栓）、炎症（白细胞清理细菌与坏死组织）、增生（成纤维细胞铺设胶原、新生血管长入、上皮细胞爬行覆盖）、重塑（胶原重新排列、瘢痕逐渐变淡，可持续数月到一年）。营养不良、血糖控制差、吸烟和局部血流不足都会拖慢这个过程。
Roughly four: haemostasis (vessels constrict, platelets clot), inflammation (white cells clear bacteria and dead tissue), proliferation (fibroblasts lay collagen, new vessels grow in, epithelium migrates across), and remodelling (collagen realigns and the scar fades over months to a year). Poor nutrition, poor glucose control, smoking and low local blood flow all slow it down.

Results should be interpreted by professionals to avoid unnecessary anxiety or false reassurance.

肺功能检查里的FEV1和FVC代表什么？

What do FEV1 and FVC mean in a lung function test?

FEV1是第一秒用力呼出的气量，FVC是尽力吸足后一口气呼出的总量。两者的比值反映气道通畅程度：哮喘和慢阻肺等阻塞性肺病中FEV1/FVC下降，而肺纤维化等限制性肺病中比值接近正常但总气量减少。医生根据这两个指标帮助区分不同性质的肺部疾病。
FEV1 is the volume forcibly exhaled in the first second; FVC is the total volume exhaled after a full breath in. Their ratio reflects how open the airways are: in obstructive diseases like asthma and COPD the FEV1/FVC ratio falls, while in restrictive diseases like pulmonary fibrosis the ratio stays near normal but total volumes are low. Doctors use these numbers to tell the two types of lung disease apart.

CT和普通X光有什么不一样？

How is a CT scan different from a plain X-ray?

CT让X光管绕身体旋转扫描，在不同角度拍摄大量投影，再由计算机重建出横断面的断层图像。它能分辨密度差别很小的软组织结构，定位更精确，但辐射剂量通常比单张X光高，所以医生只在必要时才开CT。
A CT scanner rotates the X-ray tube around the body, taking many projections at different angles, and a computer reconstructs cross-sectional images. It can tell apart soft tissues with very similar density and locates structures precisely, but the radiation dose is usually higher than a single X-ray, so doctors order CTs only when needed.

两只眼睛从不同角度看世界,大脑把两个画面合成一个立体的——这样你才能判断皮球离你多远、楼梯有多高。试试闭上一只眼去接球,会发现特别难接。两只眼睛还是备份:一只累了或受伤了,另一只还能继续工作。
Your two eyes see from different angles and your brain merges them into one 3D picture — that is how you judge how far the ball is or how tall the step. Close one eye to catch a ball and it gets surprisingly hard. Two eyes are also backups for each other.

跑步的时候为什么心跳得咚咚快?心脏一直在工作不累吗?

Why does my heart thump when I run? Does it ever get tired?

跑步时肌肉需要更多氧气,心脏就像水泵加速工作,把含氧血液更快送到全身,所以你听见咚咚声。心脏是超级劳模:从出生前就开始跳,一天约十万次,但它每次跳动之间都有短暂休息,而且越锻炼越强壮。
Running muscles need extra oxygen, so your heart pumps faster to deliver it — that is the thump. The heart is a super worker: beating about a hundred thousand times a day since before you were born, with tiny rests between beats, growing stronger the more you exercise.

为什么手指泡水久了会起皱褶?像小梅子一样

Why do fingertips wrinkle like little prunes after a long bath?

这不是皮肤「吸水变大」,而是神经系统主动让指尖血管收缩、皮肤皱起来——形成像轮胎花纹一样的排水槽,让你在湿滑的地方抓得更牢。实验证明皱褶的手指拿湿弹珠比平滑的手指快得多。这是身体自带的防滑装备!
Wrinkly fingers are not swelling — your nervous system shrinks blood vessels to fold the skin into tire-tread channels that drain water away, gripping wet things better. Experiments show wrinkled fingers pick up wet marbles much faster. It is built-in anti-slip gear.
