01河流是怎样搬运泥沙的？悬移质、推移质和溶解质有什么区别？How does a river transport sediment, and how do suspended load, bedload and dissolved load differ?

悬移质悬浮于水流，推移质沿床面滚动跃移，溶解质以离子形式随水输移；二者相加为总输沙量，推移质约占 5–20%。Suspended load floats in flow, bedload rolls or saltates on the bed, and dissolved load moves as ions; suspended plus bedload is total load, bedload about 5–20%.

河流搬运的物质通常分为悬移质、推移质和溶解质三类。降雨和地表径流把坡面风化产物带入河道，流域内岩石风化、土壤侵蚀和河岸崩塌提供原始物质，人类活动扰动地表也会增加入河泥沙。水体以流量和流速决定能够搬运多大粒径的颗粒。USGS 把河流输沙量定义为由流量与含沙量推算出的泥沙通量，通常以每年吨数表示；多数输沙量发生在洪水期，平水期河道搬运的物质相对有限。Material carried by a river is usually divided into suspended load, bedload and dissolved load. Rainfall and surface runoff bring weathered material from slopes into the channel, and discharge and flow velocity determine the grain size a body of water can move. USGS defines sediment load as the sediment flux estimated from discharge and sediment concentration, usually expressed in tons per year; most of the load moves during floods, while transport in low-water periods is relatively limited.[1][3]

悬移质是悬浮在水流中的粉砂和黏土，随水体一起向下游移动，含沙量可用悬沙采样器在水柱中测定；推移质是粗砂、砾石沿河床滚动、滑动或跳跃式前进的颗粒；溶解质是岩石风化后溶解进入水中的离子，随径流输往湖泊和海洋。悬移质与推移质相加构成总输沙量，溶解质则以化学溶解的形式另行计量。Suspended load consists of silt and clay held up in the flow and carried downstream with the water; its concentration can be sampled in the water column. Bedload consists of sand and gravel rolling, sliding or saltating along the riverbed. Dissolved load consists of ions released by weathering and carried toward lakes and the ocean. Suspended load plus bedload forms the total sediment load, while dissolved material is counted separately in chemical solution.[5]

颗粒起动所需的流速随粒径增大而升高。只有坡度陡、流速大的山区河流能够搬运砾石、卵石乃至漂石，平缓平原河道的水流主要携带粉砂和黏土。河道坡度、流量大小和流域岩性共同决定一条河流的粒径组成，因此同一条河在不同断面、不同水文期的搬运对象并不相同；同一断面在涨水和退水时搬运粒径也不同，粒径组成随流量阶段变化。The velocity needed to entrain a grain rises with particle size. Only steep, fast mountain rivers can move gravel, cobbles and even boulders; gentle lowland channels mostly carry silt and clay. Channel slope, discharge and basin lithology together determine the grain-size composition of a river, so a single river moves different materials at different sections and hydrologic periods.[1][2]

侵蚀、搬运和沉积在河道上连续发生。上游坡陡，河流以下切侵蚀为主；中游侧向搬运；下游坡缓、流速降低时泥沙沉积。洪水期高流量可以搬运更粗的物质，退水时粗颗粒最先沉降，沉积顺序遵循分选规律：流速下降时颗粒按由粗到细先后沉降。USGS 资料指出，推移质约占总输沙量的 5–20%，而多数已公布的输沙量只测量悬移质，因此这些数值是总输沙量的下限估计。Erosion, transport and deposition occur in sequence along the channel. Steep headwaters are dominated by downward erosion, the middle course by lateral transport, and the gentle lower course by deposition as velocity falls. High flood discharge moves coarser material, and the coarsest grains settle first as flow recedes. USGS sources note bedload accounts for about 5–20 percent of total load, while many reported loads measure suspended load only, so they represent a minimum estimate of total load.[2][5]

边界：输沙量估算受测量时段、断面位置和水文条件限制，短年限数据未必代表长期平均；溶解质份额因流域岩石类型差别很大。引用时应给区间，不能把某一条河流的比例推广到所有河流；当测量以悬移质为主时，总输沙量会被系统性低估。The boundary: sediment-load estimates are limited by measurement period, section location and hydrologic conditions, and short records may not represent the long-term average; dissolved fractions vary widely with basin rock type. Figures should be quoted as ranges rather than generalized from one river to all; when measurement focuses on suspended load, total load is systematically underestimated.[5]

02河道为什么会弯曲？凹岸侵蚀、凸岸堆积怎样形成曲流和牛轭湖？Why do channels meander, and how does concave-bank erosion with convex-bank deposition form meanders and oxbow lakes?

凹岸外侧水流快而侵蚀后退，凸岸内侧水缓堆积成边滩；弯曲度增大后洪水裁弯取直，废弃弯环封堵成牛轭湖，古城多选稳定凸岸。Fast flow erodes the outer concave bank while slow flow builds the inner convex bar; cutoffs abandon loops into oxbow lakes, and towns favor stable convex banks.

在平原或缓坡河段，河道常呈弯曲形态。凹岸是弯顶外侧，水流受离心力推向岸壁，该处流速快、冲刷强，岸坡逐渐被侵蚀后退；凸岸是弯顶内侧，水流较缓，粗颗粒泥沙沉积下来，形成向河道凸出的边滩。弯道内形成螺旋状横向环流，表层水流冲向凹岸，底层水流从凹岸流向凸岸，带动底沙向凸岸搬运。一岸蚀、一岸积，是曲流发育最基本的空间格局。On plain or gentle-slope reaches, channels often follow a winding path. The concave bank lies on the outer bend, where centrifugal flow presses against the wall; faster currents erode it and push the bank back over time. The convex bank lies on the inner bend, where slower water deposits coarser grains to build a point bar protruding into the channel. Erosion on one side and deposition on the other form the basic pattern of meander development.[2]

随着时间推移，弯曲度不断增大：凹岸继续被冲刷后退，凸岸继续淤长，河弯越来越弯，弯道两端的颈部逐渐收窄。洪水期高流量水流可能直接切穿颈部，发生裁弯取直，河水改走新的直道；裁弯后新河道流程缩短、比降增大，废弃弯道内水动力减弱，逐渐被淤浅成湖沼，两端随后被泥沙封堵，残留的弯环形成牛轭湖。Over time the bend becomes more curved: the concave bank keeps retreating while the point bar keeps growing, the loop tightens, and the neck at its two ends narrows. During high flood discharge water may cut straight through the neck in a cutoff, the river takes the new straighter course, and sediment seals the two ends of the abandoned loop, leaving an oxbow lake.[2][6]

古城多选择落在凸岸一侧，有明确的地貌原因。凸岸是长期堆积形成的平坦河滩，边滩不断向外生长，可形成可供耕作和居住的河漫滩，地基相对稳定，不易被水流掏蚀；凹岸虽然水深、利于舟船停靠，但岸坡持续后退、容易崩塌。历史聚落选在堆积岸一侧，同时也要避开洪水高水位淹没范围，渡口、水源和防御条件会一并纳入考虑。Historical settlements often sit on the convex bank for clear geomorphic reasons. The convex side is a flat floodplain built by long-term deposition, with relatively stable ground that is not readily undercut; the concave bank, though deep and useful for boats, retreats continuously and is prone to collapse. Settlements on the depositional side also chose ground above the high-water flood level, with crossing, water supply and defense taken into account.[1]

曲流发育需要相应条件：坡度平缓、泥沙供应适中，河岸物质既可以被侵蚀、也可以被堆积。曲流在冲积平原和软弱河岸物质上发育最典型，河流摆动加宽河谷、留下多条废弃弯道遗迹。河岸植被根系可以加固岸坡，抑制局部后退，植被清除后侵蚀往往加快。基岩山区的河流多为深切河曲或峡谷，河道难以自由摆动；人工护岸、裁弯和渠道化工程则会显著改变自然演化的速度和路径。Meander development needs suitable conditions: gentle slope, moderate sediment supply, and bank material that can be eroded and deposited. Bedrock mountain rivers tend to be incised meanders or gorges that cannot swing freely; artificial bank protection, cutoffs and channelization substantially alter the pace and path of natural evolution.[2][6]

边界：凹岸侵蚀速率受河岸土质、流量和植被覆盖影响，并非每一段弯岸都稳定后退；牛轭湖是特定条件下的产物，不是每条河弯都会快速废弃。聚落选址还受交通、防御和水源等因素共同约束，不能只用地貌一条解释所有历史城址。The boundary: concave-bank retreat depends on bank soil, discharge and vegetation, and not every bend retreats steadily; oxbow lakes form under specific conditions, and not every loop is abandoned quickly. Site selection also involved transport, defense and water supply, so geomorphology alone cannot explain every historical settlement.[2][6]

03一场洪水的流量过程怎样变化？涨水段、洪峰和退水段由什么决定？How does discharge change through a flood, and what determines the rising limb, peak and recession limb?

降水经坡面与支流汇入干流，形成先涨后落的过程线；蓄水、地形与不透水面决定峰高和出现时刻。Rainwater concentrates from slopes and tributaries into a rise-and-fall hydrograph; storage, terrain and cover set peak height and timing.

洪水过程线记录某一河道断面流量或水位随时间的变化。降雨或融雪到达流域后，先经过坡面漫流、洼地填蓄和土壤下渗，再汇入溪沟与支流，最终汇集到干流断面，形成一条先上涨、后回落的曲线。断面记录的水位换算为流量需要水位流量关系曲线，不同水文站的计量基准与断面形态各不相同。过程线是理解洪水如何从流域传向河道的基本水文记录。A flood hydrograph records discharge or stage at a channel section over time. After rainfall or snowmelt reaches a basin, it spreads over slopes, fills depressions and infiltrates soil before entering rills and tributaries and finally gathering at the main section, producing a curve that rises and then falls. The hydrograph is the basic hydrologic record showing how floodwater travels from the basin to the channel.[2][3]

一条典型过程线分为涨水段、洪峰和退水段。降水初期河槽蓄水、坡面滞蓄使流量上涨较缓；雨前土壤干燥时下渗量大、初期径流小，土壤饱和后下渗减少，坡面径流比例上升，洪峰来得更快。随后支流陆续汇入，流量快速上升到峰值；雨停以后流域蓄水逐步泄出，退水段通常比涨水段平缓，持续时间也更长。峰值高低和出现时刻取决于降雨强度、分布与流域汇流条件。A typical hydrograph divides into rising limb, flood peak and recession limb. Early on, channel storage and slope detention hold back the rise; tributaries then join one after another and discharge climbs rapidly to its peak. After rain stops, stored basin water drains out gradually, so the recession limb is usually gentler and longer than the rising limb. Peak height and timing depend on rainfall intensity, spatial distribution and basin runoff conditions.[2][4]

流域属性塑造曲线形状。流域面积大、河网密度高、植被和土壤蓄水能力强，洪峰出现较晚，峰值相对较低；地形陡峭、不透水面多、土壤饱和时汇流迅速，洪峰陡涨陡落。河网汇流时间受河道长度和坡度影响，支流先后汇入使干流过程线叠加多个来水时段。积雪融水洪水受气温日变化和季节升温影响，过程线常表现为持续数周的季节性抬升，而不是单次暴雨式尖峰。Basin attributes shape the curve. Large basins with dense drainage networks, vegetation and soil storage produce later, relatively lower peaks; steep terrain, impervious surfaces and saturated soil yield fast concentration and sharp rise-and-fall peaks. Snowmelt floods respond to daily temperature and seasonal warming, often rising over weeks rather than forming a single storm-driven spike.[2][3]

洪水频率与风险评估建立在长期水文记录之上。USGS 通过水文站网实时监测水位和流量，配合洪水淹没制图和高水位标记，帮助理解局部洪水风险。重现期是统计概念：给定频率的事件在任一年份都可能发生，并不代表到期必然出现；风险表达的是长期平均可能性，不是确定性预报。Flood frequency and risk assessment rest on long-term hydrologic records. USGS monitors stage and discharge in real time through a streamgage network, combined with flood inundation mapping and high-water marks, to help understand local flood risk. A return period is a statistical concept: an event of a given frequency can occur in any year, not at a fixed appointed date; it expresses long-term average likelihood rather than a deterministic prediction.[4]

边界：单场过程线受前期土壤湿度、降雨时空分布和人工工程影响，不能只凭峰值大小判断严重程度；山洪与平原洪水的过程线形态差异很大，不能用同一套峰值标准比较不同流域。短记录推算的频率存在不确定性，城市化和土地利用变化会改变汇流响应。洪水预报依赖实时数据和模型，结论需要随着新的观测不断更新。The boundary: a single hydrograph is affected by antecedent soil moisture, the timing and distribution of rainfall, and engineered works, so severity cannot be judged from the peak alone. Frequency estimates from short records carry uncertainty, and urbanization or land-use change alter runoff response. Flood forecasting relies on real-time data and models, and conclusions must be updated as new observations arrive.[3][4]

04三角洲是怎样形成的？基准面和泥沙补给怎样控制河口地貌？How do deltas form, and how do base level and sediment supply control estuary morphology?

入水体流速骤减，泥沙按粗细分带沉积；基准面以上趋侵蚀、近基准面趋堆积，泥沙减少叠加沉降与海平面上升会导致岸线后退。Velocity drops at the receiving water and sediment sorts into zones; above base level erosion dominates, near it deposition; reduced load plus subsidence drives retreat.

河流进入湖泊或海洋时，受纳水体的摩擦使流速骤减，搬运能力下降，泥沙在河口附近堆积形成三角洲。当河流入海遇波浪、潮汐或湖流顶托时，泥沙卸载位置和扩散方向随之改变，沙嘴与沿岸流也会重新分配近口门的泥沙。分流河道把泥沙送往不同方向，粗颗粒先在河道口门附近沉积，细颗粒被带到更远的前缘和前三角洲区域。三角洲是河流输沙在静水环境中卸载的地貌产物。When a river enters a lake or the ocean, friction in the receiving water sharply reduces velocity and transport capacity, so sediment deposits near the mouth to form a delta. Distributary channels carry sediment in different directions; coarse grains settle near the channel mouths, while finer particles travel to the more distal front and prodelta zones. A delta is the landform left when river-borne sediment unloads in still water.[2][6]

三角洲沉积具有分带特征：近口门的分流河道与天然堤沉积较粗的砂，口门附近快速堆积的沙坝会迫使水流分汊，形成多级分流河道；外侧泛滥平原和三角洲前缘以粉砂、黏土为主，更远的前三角洲沉积细泥；三角洲向水域推进时，老的细泥沉积被上覆较粗沉积覆盖，形成前细后粗的垂向层序。波浪强弱和潮汐改造程度不同，会塑造出扇形、鸟足状或朵状等不同的三角洲平面形态。Delta deposits are zoned: distributary channels and natural levees near the mouth hold coarser sand, the outer floodplain and delta front carry silt and clay, and the farther prodelta receives fine mud. As sedimentation advances, the vertical sequence coarsens upward. Differences in wave strength and tidal reworking produce fan-shaped, bird-foot or lobate planform shapes.[2][6]

基准面控制着侵蚀与沉积的下限。入海河流大致以海平面为侵蚀基准面；基准面以上的河道有向下切蚀的趋势，接近基准面时则趋于堆积。构造下沉会为沉积提供容纳空间，构造抬升则使河流增强下切，河口区域基准面与构造共同作用。基准面升降、构造沉降或抬升、沉积物供给以及波浪潮汐改造，共同决定岸线是向前推进还是向后退蚀。Base level sets the lower limit for erosion and deposition. A river entering the sea roughly uses sea level as its erosion base level; channels above base level tend to downcut, while near base level they tend to deposit. Base-level rise or fall, tectonic subsidence or uplift, sediment supply, and wave and tidal reworking together determine whether the shoreline advances or retreats.[1][2]

泥沙补给减少会改变三角洲的发育方向。上游建库、引水和河道取沙使输沙量下降，叠加相对海平面上升和地面沉降，部分三角洲出现海岸侵蚀和湿地损失；湿地植被可以滞留泥沙、促淤造地，植被破坏后岸线侵蚀往往加快。当泥沙补给充足、沉降与堆积保持平衡时，三角洲才能继续向水域推进。Reduced sediment supply changes delta trajectory. Reservoirs, water diversions and in-channel sand extraction lower load; combined with relative sea-level rise and land subsidence, some deltas show coastal erosion and wetland loss. Deltas continue to advance only when sediment supply is sufficient and subsidence stays balanced by deposition.[6]

边界：三角洲形态是多种动力长期作用的结果，单次洪水或风暴只改造局部范围；基准面是分析框架，实际河道还受岩性、植被和工程约束。沉积速率估算受钻孔和测绘方法限制，不同地点的推进或后退趋势不能互相套用。The boundary: delta shape results from long-term interaction among several forces, and a single flood or storm modifies only a local reach; base level is an analytical framework, and actual channels are also constrained by lithology, vegetation and engineering. Deposition-rate estimates depend on drilling and survey methods, so trends of advance or retreat at one site cannot be transferred to another.[2][6]

05地下水是怎么来的？为什么河流一直有水？Where does groundwater come from, and why do rivers keep flowing?

降水入渗补给含水层，地下水缓慢流动并以基流排泄回河流；开采长期超过补给会消耗老水、水位下降。Rainfall infiltrates into aquifers; groundwater moves slowly and discharges back as river baseflow. Pumping beyond recharge depletes stored water and lowers the table.

降水落到地表后分成两路：一部分沿坡面汇入溪沟和河流，形成地表径流；另一部分向下渗入土壤，先填满表层孔隙，再继续穿过风化壳和岩层裂隙，进入地下饱和带，成为地下水。入渗能力取决于土壤质地、植被覆盖、坡度和前期含水量：疏松多孔的砂土下渗快，板结黏土和硬质铺装下渗慢；根系与枯枝落叶拦蓄雨水，留出下渗时间。地下水不是藏在地下的暗河里，而是储存在岩石和土壤颗粒之间的孔隙、裂隙与溶洞空间中，像海绵一样分散在介质里。When precipitation reaches the ground, it splits into two paths: part runs down slopes into rills and rivers as surface runoff, while the rest infiltrates downward through soil, fills near-surface pores, then crosses weathered rock and fractures into the saturated subsurface to become groundwater. Infiltration depends on soil texture, vegetation cover, slope and prior moisture: loose porous sand soaks water quickly, while compacted clay and hard paving infiltrate slowly; roots and leaf litter hold rain and buy time for infiltration. Groundwater is not an underground river but stored in pores, fractures and cavern spaces between rock and soil grains, dispersed through the medium like water in a sponge.[3][7]

能够储水并透水的岩层或砂砾层叫含水层，上方不透水的黏土、基岩构成隔水层。补给区多在地势较高的山区和丘陵，降水入渗后顺着孔隙和裂隙向低处缓慢流动；在河谷、洼地或泉眼位置，地下水位露出地表，以泉或渗流形式排泄回河流、湖泊和沼泽。地下水流动速度很慢，多数每天只移动几米到几厘米，深层地下水可能在地下停留几十年甚至数千年；相邻含水层之间还可能通过弱透水层发生越流补给。Rock layers or gravel beds that store and transmit water are aquifers, underlain by impermeable clay or bedrock as aquitards. Recharge zones sit mostly in higher hills and mountains; infiltrated water moves slowly through pores and fractures toward lower ground, where the water table meets the surface in valleys, depressions or springs, discharging back into rivers, lakes and wetlands. Groundwater moves slowly, often centimeters to a few meters per day, and deep groundwater may reside underground for decades to millennia; adjacent aquifers can also exchange water through leakage across semi-permeable layers.[1][7]

枯水期河流不断流，主要靠地下水基流托底。雨季河流水位高于地下水位时，河水补给两岸地下水；旱季河流水位下降，地下水位高于河面，地下水反过来持续渗入河道，维持稳定的基流流量。流域蓄水能力强、含水层厚度大的河流，枯水期基流更平缓；以雨水和冰雪融水季节性补给为主的河流，枯水期水量则明显减小。泉水常年不断，正是含水层稳定排泄的直接表现。Rivers keep flowing in dry seasons mainly on groundwater baseflow. In rainy periods river level sits above the water table and recharge flows from river to bank groundwater; in dry periods the river drops, the water table rises above the channel, and groundwater seeps back steadily into the stream to hold up baseflow. Basins with strong storage and thick aquifers maintain gentler dry-season flow, while rivers fed mainly by seasonal rain or snowmelt shrink noticeably in low water. Perennial springs are the direct expression of steady aquifer discharge.[1][3]

地下水是可更新资源，但更新速度远慢于地表水。开采量长期超过补给量，会消耗含水层里储存的老水，地下水位持续下降，泉眼干涸、河流基流减少，严重时出现地面沉降和海水入侵。人工补给、减少开采和保护流域植被，可以让采补趋于平衡；深层地下水年龄老、循环慢，一旦开采过度，恢复需要数十年到上百年。判断一条河流或一处含水层能不能持续用水，要看多年补给与开采的平衡，而不是某一年的水量。Groundwater is renewable, but renews far more slowly than surface water. When pumping exceeds recharge over the long term, stored fossil water is depleted, the water table keeps falling, springs dry up and river baseflow declines, in severe cases with land subsidence or seawater intrusion. Artificial recharge, lower withdrawals and watershed vegetation can bring extraction and replenishment toward balance; deep groundwater is old and slow, so overpumping takes decades to centuries to recover. Whether a river or aquifer can sustain water use depends on the long-term balance of recharge and withdrawal, not a single year's supply.[7]

边界：地下水分布和流速受岩性、构造和地形控制，不同流域差别很大，不能把一处含水层的产量套到别处。基流只是河流常年有水的来源之一，冰川融水、湖泊调蓄和人工调水也在起作用；河流弯曲形态由凹岸侵蚀凸岸堆积形成，属于河道地貌过程，与地下水补给是两套机制。本章讲入渗、储水与基流排泄的循环平衡，不展开水井设计、区域地下水数值模型或取水许可管理。The boundary: groundwater distribution and flow speed vary with lithology, structure and terrain, so one aquifer's yield cannot be transferred elsewhere. Baseflow is only one source of perennial river flow; glacial melt, lake storage and artificial transfers also contribute, and channel meanders form by concave-bank erosion and convex-bank deposition, a separate landform process from groundwater recharge. This chapter covers infiltration, aquifer storage and baseflow discharge cycles and balance, not well design, regional groundwater numerical models or water-rights administration.[1][7]

06峡谷是怎样被流水切出来的？How does running water carve a canyon?

河流靠下切侵蚀把河床逐段加深，两侧岩层崩塌后退，形成上宽下窄的峡谷。A river deepens its bed by downward erosion while banks collapse and retreat, forming a wide-topped narrow-bottomed canyon.

峡谷是河流长期向下切割出来的地貌。河水在重力作用下沿坡面流动，坡度越陡、落差越大，流速就越快，能够推动泥沙、砾石乃至卵石前进；这些挟带的碎屑像天然磨料一样反复撞击、磨蚀河床基岩。河流的下切能量主要来自地形落差，上游山区河段坡度集中、水量与流速叠加，向下切割的能力远强于平缓河段。一段河床被切深多少，取决于流量、流速、碎屑粒径和基岩硬度的组合。A canyon is carved by a river cutting downward over a long period. Water flows downhill under gravity; the steeper the slope and the larger the drop, the faster the current, and the more sand, gravel and cobbles it can push forward. These carried fragments act as natural abrasive tools, striking and grinding the bedrock again and again. A river's downcutting energy comes mainly from topographic relief. In steep mountain headwaters, discharge and velocity combine, and the capacity to cut downward is far stronger than on gentle lowland reaches. How deeply a bed is lowered depends on discharge, velocity, fragment size and bedrock hardness together.[2]

河水对河床的这种作用叫下切侵蚀。当水流足以推动砾石撞击和磨蚀床面时，河床被一点点加深；两岸岩石失去坡脚支撑后发生崩塌，谷坡逐步后退，河谷横剖面慢慢变成上宽下窄的 V 形。若岩层坚硬、垂直节理发育，崩塌后留下陡直的谷壁，就形成两侧岩壁夹峙的峡谷。谷底通常只在洪水期被水流淹没，平水期露出砾石滩。This action on the riverbed is called downcutting erosion. When the current can drive gravel into the bed, the channel is deepened bit by bit; once the foot of each bank loses support, rock collapses and the slope retreats. The valley cross-section gradually becomes a V shape, wide at the top and narrow at the bottom. Where beds are hard and jointed vertically, collapse leaves steep walls, producing a canyon bounded by rock on both sides. The valley floor is usually flooded only during high water and exposed as a gravel bar in normal flow.[2][5]

不同岩性和发育阶段决定峡谷形态。软弱岩层更容易被蚀去，坚硬岩层形成突出的岩坎、阶地和窄壁；同一条河从源头到河口，上游以向下切割为主，中游展宽摆动，下游坡缓则以堆积为主。峡谷多出现在地壳抬升、河流仍在继续下切的河段：抬升越快，河流相对基准面的落差越大，河谷被切得越深，两岸坡壁也越陡。Rock type and stage of development set the canyon shape. Weak strata erode more easily, while hard beds leave ledges, terraces and narrow walls. Along one river from source to mouth, the headwaters mainly cut downward, the middle reach widens and swings, and the gentle lower reach deposits sediment. Canyons most often form where the crust is rising and the river keeps incising: the faster the uplift, the larger the drop relative to the base level, the deeper the valley and the steeper its walls.[2][3]

下切、崩塌、搬运和堆积配合完成塑造。峡谷坡面崩落的岩块，被洪水期高流速水流带走；若泥沙来源多、坡度趋缓，搬运不动的物质就在谷底堆积成滩地。河流同时把侵蚀产物输往下游，使坡面后退和谷底加深能够持续进行。断面窄深、坡壁陡峻的峡谷，通常对应侵蚀基准面较低、下切占优的河段。Downcutting, collapse, transport and deposition work together. Blocks fallen from canyon slopes are carried away by high-velocity flood water; where sediment supply is high and the slope flattens, material that can no longer be moved accumulates as a floor bar. The river carries erosion products downstream, so slope retreat and bed deepening can continue. A narrow, deep section with steep walls usually corresponds to a reach where the base level is low and incision dominates.[2][5]

边界：峡谷不是某一次洪水一夜之间切出来的，而是以万年到数十万年计的累积侵蚀结果；单次暴雨洪水只能在既有谷壁上扩大崩塌，不能单独形成深谷。本章讲流水下切的一般机制，不展开某条峡谷的地质年代、构造抬升速率，也不评价局部边坡稳定性或崩塌灾害风险。Boundary: a canyon is not cut overnight by one flood; it is the accumulated result of erosion over tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of years. A single storm can only widen a landslide on an existing wall, not create a deep valley on its own. This chapter explains the general mechanism of fluvial incision, not the geological age of any specific canyon, its uplift rate, or local slope stability and landslide risk.[2][3]

07溶洞（钟乳石）是怎样形成的？How do limestone caves and stalactites form?

弱酸性水溶蚀石灰岩成洞，渗出的水滴逸出二氧化碳后析出钙质，长成钟乳石和石笋。Weak acid dissolves limestone into a cave; seeping droplets lose carbon dioxide and deposit calcite as stalactites and stalagmites.

溶洞是可溶性岩石被水长期溶蚀后形成的地下空间。降雨落到地表，渗入土壤和裂隙时吸收二氧化碳，形成含碳酸的弱酸性水流；石灰岩的主要成分碳酸钙遇酸发生溶解，被水带走。沿岩层节理、断层和层面裂隙，水流逐步溶出通道，通道扩大后连成洞穴系统。这类以溶蚀作用为主的地貌统称喀斯特，常见于石灰岩、白云岩分布区；地表常配套出现漏斗、落水洞和干涸河床等形态。A cave is an underground space formed when soluble rock is dissolved by water over a long time. Rain reaches the surface and, as it seeps through soil and fractures, absorbs carbon dioxide to form a weakly acidic water charged with carbonic acid. Calcium carbonate, the main component of limestone, reacts with the acid and is carried away. Along joints, faults and bedding cracks, the water gradually etches passages that enlarge and connect into a cave system. This dissolution-dominated terrain is called karst and is common in limestone and dolomite regions.[2][8]

溶蚀依靠化学平衡而不是机械冲刷。水中二氧化碳与水结合成碳酸，解离出氢离子，把难溶的碳酸钙转化为可溶的碳酸氢钙；当水在裂隙中流动、饱和程度变化时，溶解持续进行。岩石中的裂隙越发育、径流越活跃，洞穴扩展越快；地下水位附近是溶蚀最强烈的带，水位下降后，原有溶洞被抬高到水面以上。Dissolution rests on chemical equilibrium rather than mechanical scouring. Carbon dioxide in the water combines with water to form carbonic acid, which releases hydrogen ions and turns sparingly soluble calcium carbonate into soluble bicarbonate; as water flows through cracks and saturation shifts, dissolution continues. The more developed the fractures and the more active the runoff, the faster the cave expands. The zone near the water table dissolves most strongly; after the water table drops, the older cave is left above the water line.[3][8]

钟乳石和石笋是溶洞内的沉积产物。含碳酸氢钙的水从洞顶裂隙渗出，进入空气流通的洞穴后压力降低、二氧化碳逸出，溶解度下降，碳酸钙重新析出沉淀。水滴悬挂在洞顶时逐层堆积，向下长成锥状钟乳石；水滴落到洞底，钙质在底面向上堆积成石笋，两者对接后形成石柱。钙华沿水滴路径逐层叠加，先析出的一层被后析出的一层覆盖，锥体随滴水持续加长。Stalactites and stalagmites are deposits formed inside the cave. Water carrying bicarbonate seeps from ceiling cracks; once it enters the ventilated cave, pressure falls, carbon dioxide escapes, solubility drops, and calcium carbonate precipitates again. A droplet hanging from the ceiling builds layer by layer into a downward cone, the stalactite; when the droplet reaches the floor, calcium accumulates upward into a stalagmite, and where the two meet they form a column.[2][8]

沉积速度很慢，形态由滴水方式决定。钟乳石每年通常只增长数毫米到一厘米左右，断面可见一层一层的同心纹；滴水稳定、水量小形成细长锥，水量大或水流沿壁铺开则形成石幔、石帘。洞内还常见流石、钙华台阶和细小的石枝，它们共同记录滴水、蒸发与二氧化碳逸出的平衡过程。Deposition is slow, and the shape depends on how the drip behaves. A stalactite usually grows only a few millimetres to about one centimetre per year, and its section shows concentric layers. Steady, small drips make slender cones; heavier flow or water spread along the wall forms draperies and curtains. Flowstone, travertine terraces and delicate helictites are also common, and together they record the balance of dripping, evaporation and carbon dioxide escape.[8]

边界：溶洞形成同时包含溶蚀与沉积两个相反方向，水流搬运泥沙只是辅助过程；本章讲碳酸盐岩溶蚀和钙华沉积的一般化学机制，不测定具体洞穴的年代与古气候记录，也不涉及洞内游览开发或地质灾害评估。洞穴内二氧化碳积聚、落石和浸水风险属于安全话题，不在本章机制范围内。Boundary: cave formation includes both dissolution and deposition, two opposite directions, while sediment transport by water is only a secondary process. This chapter explains the general chemistry of carbonate dissolution and travertine deposition; it does not date any specific cave or its palaeoclimate record, and it does not cover tourism development or geological hazard assessment. Carbon dioxide buildup, falling rock and flooding inside a cave are safety topics outside this chapter's mechanism.[2][3]
