01冰块为什么能浮在水面上，湖水又为什么从表面开始结冰？Why does ice float, and why does a lake freeze from the surface down?

水结冰时氢键搭成开放骨架，体积膨胀约 9%，密度降到 0.917，所以冰浮在水上。Freezing locks water into an open hydrogen-bonded lattice, raising volume by about 9% and lowering density to 0.917, so ice floats.

冰块浮在水面，是因为水结冰时体积反而变大、密度变小：0 °C 时冰的密度约 0.917 g/cm³，同温液态水约 0.9998 g/cm³，相差约 9%，所以浮起的冰块约有十分之一体积露出水面。这也解释了池塘为什么从表层开始结冰、冰盖为什么能一直浮在上面，以及冰块放进饮料后为什么总是漂着。An ice cube floats because water expands when it freezes. At 0 °C, ice has a density of about 0.917 g/cm³, while liquid water at the same temperature is about 0.9998 g/cm³. The gap is roughly nine per cent, so about a tenth of a floating block sits above the surface. The same physics explains why a pond freezes from the top down, why its ice lid stays afloat, and why ice cubes rise to the surface of a drink.[1][2]

根源在氢键。液态水中分子仍能相对滑动，平均每个水分子周围约四个邻居，排列较紧；降温到 4 °C 附近，热运动减弱、氢键的方向性开始占上风，密度达到最大，约 1.000 g/cm³。再冷到 0 °C 结冰，分子被锁进六角冰 Ih 的开放骨架：每个氧原子按四面体方向连四个氢键，环与环之间留下空腔，体积约膨胀 9%。所以在冰点附近，水违反了“固体比液体密”的常规；不是水分子变轻了，而是同样多的分子占了更大的体积。The special behaviour comes from hydrogen bonds. In liquid water the molecules can still slide past one another, and each molecule has on average about four neighbours, so the packing is fairly tight. As water cools toward 4 °C, thermal motion weakens while the directional hydrogen bonds begin to dominate, and density reaches a maximum of almost 1.000 g/cm³. Below that temperature the molecules lock into the open hexagonal framework of ice Ih, with each oxygen atom pointing four hydrogen bonds toward tetrahedral neighbours. That framework leaves empty channels, so the volume grows by about nine per cent. Near the freezing point, water runs against the usual rule that solids are denser than liquids: the molecules do not get lighter, the same molecules simply occupy more space.[2]

这些数字可以核对。USGS 给出 0 °C 冰约 917 kg/m³、4 °C 水约 999.9 kg/m³；OpenStax 化学教材用氢键和六角晶格解释同一事实。阿基米德原理把密度差变成露出比例：排开水的质量等于冰的质量，露出部分约 8.3%。天然冰含气泡和杂质，密度更低，浮得更高；实验室纯水冰则接近 0.917 这个上限。The numbers can be checked. The USGS lists ice at 0 °C as about 917 kg/m³ and water at 4 °C as about 999.9 kg/m³, and OpenStax uses hydrogen bonding and the hexagonal lattice to explain the same fact. Archimedes' principle turns the density difference into a floating fraction: the mass of displaced water equals the mass of the ice, leaving about 8.3 per cent above the surface. Natural ice contains bubbles and impurities, is less dense and floats higher, while clean laboratory ice approaches the 0.917 limit.[1]

对湖泊而言，这一条决定了冬季分层。表层先降到 0 °C 结冰，冰盖密度小，停在上面，挡住风的搅动；冰下 4 °C 左右的水密度最大，沉在底部。鱼因此在相对稳定的深水越冬，而不是被冻成一块。如果冰像多数固体那样比水重，它会沉底，湖面继续结冰，水体从下往上冻结，水生生物面对的局面会完全不同。春季融冰时，4 °C 的水再次成为密度最大的一层，湖水的春季翻转也由这条密度曲线启动。For a lake, this single fact controls winter layering. The surface reaches 0 °C first and freezes; the ice lid is lighter than the water, stays on top and blocks wind-driven mixing. Below it, water near 4 °C is the densest and sits at the bottom, so fish overwinter in relatively stable deep water instead of being frozen solid. If ice behaved like most solids and sank, the pond would freeze from the bottom up and the whole water column would be at risk. When the ice melts in spring, water at 4 °C becomes the densest layer again, and that density curve also sets up the spring turnover.[1][2]

边界要写清：这是淡水的情形。海水含盐，冰点约 -1.8 °C，结冰时把盐排出去，形成卤水；糖水和酒精溶液的密度曲线各不相同。高压下还会出现冰 II、冰 VI 等晶型，其中一些确实比液态水重。把“冰浮水”当作所有固体的通律会出错：多数物质固态更密，水是重要例外；把“冰一定比水轻”当作所有冰的通律，同样会错。The boundary matters: this is fresh water. Seawater is salty, freezes near -1.8 °C and rejects salt as it freezes, forming brine; sugary and alcoholic liquids follow their own density curves. At high pressure, other ice phases such as ice II and ice VI appear, and some of them really are denser than liquid water. Treating floating ice as a law for all solids would be wrong, because most solids are denser than their liquids; treating 'ice is always lighter than water' as a law for all ice would be wrong too.[2][7]

02冰面为什么那么滑？真的是冰刀把冰压融化了吗？Why is ice so slippery, and does a skate blade really melt it under pressure?

冰表面天然有一层准液体水膜，滑行摩擦又让它变厚；压力融化只是配角。Ice carries a natural quasi-liquid surface film that friction thickens; pressure melting plays only a minor role.

冰面滑，不是“冰刀把冰压化”这么简单。现代测量显示，在约 -20 °C 到 0 °C 之间，冰的表面天然存在一层几纳米到几十纳米厚的准液体层，分子可以像液体一样滑动；滑行时的摩擦热又让这层水膜变厚、变连续。真正让人打滑的，是这层界面水加上摩擦热，而不是整块冰被压力融化。Ice is slippery not simply because a skate blade 'melts it under pressure'. Modern measurements show that between about -20 °C and 0 °C an ice surface carries a natural quasi-liquid layer a few nanometres to tens of nanometres thick, in which molecules can slide like a liquid. Skating adds frictional heating, which thickens that film and makes it continuous. The slipperiness comes from this interfacial water plus friction, not from the whole block being pressure-melted.[3][4]

冰内部的水分子被氢键固定，但最表层分子只在一侧有邻居，能量更高，所以在低于熔点的温度也会“提前融化”。表面科学把这层叫准液体层：温度越高越厚，接近 0 °C 时可达几十纳米；它既不是完整液体，也不是完整固体，黏度介于两者之间。2018 年 Weber 等人用和频振动光谱测得，-20 °C 时表面仍有两三个分子层在活动。Inside the ice, every water molecule is held by hydrogen bonds, but molecules at the very surface have neighbours on one side only and therefore higher energy. That is why the surface can pre-melt below the bulk melting point. Surface science calls it a quasi-liquid layer: it thickens as temperature rises and can reach tens of nanometres near 0 °C. It is neither a complete liquid nor a complete solid, and its viscosity lies between the two. In 2018, Weber and colleagues used sum-frequency generation spectroscopy to show that at -20 °C roughly two or three molecular layers are still mobile.[4]

压力融化的账也要算。冰刀或鞋底施加的压强通常只有几百千帕到几兆帕，按克拉珀龙关系，熔点最多下降零点几度到一两度，远不足以解释 -10 °C 冰面的滑。真正的主力是摩擦热：滑行把机械能变成热，局部升温，准液体层变厚，摩擦系数下降。这也是为什么同一块冰，快滑时更顺、慢走时更涩。The pressure-melting account does not add up. The pressure under a skate blade or a boot is usually a few hundred kilopascals to a few megapascals, and the Clapeyron relation says the melting point falls by only tenths of a degree, or at most a degree or two. That cannot explain a slippery surface at -10 °C. Frictional heating is the main actor: sliding converts mechanical energy into heat, the local temperature rises, the quasi-liquid layer thickens and the friction coefficient drops. This is why the same patch of ice feels faster when a skater pushes hard and stickier when movement slows.[3]

对走路的人，危险来自低摩擦且不可预测：阳光照到的位置水膜更厚，阴影处更薄；撒盐会降低冰点、让表面出现盐水膜，但极低温时盐的效果变差。防滑鞋靠软橡胶和深纹增加真实接触面积、给水留出排走的通道；冰爪则让金属齿直接刺入冰体，绕过水膜。真正要小心的是薄薄一层“黑冰”，它看起来只是湿，摩擦却接近极限。走路时步幅越短、落脚越轻，鞋底与冰面的接触时间越短，摩擦热越来不及积累，反而更不容易打滑。For a walker, the danger is low and unpredictable friction. On the same pavement, a sunlit patch may carry a thicker water film while shade keeps it thin. Salt lowers the freezing point and creates a brine film, but its effect weakens in very cold weather. Winter boots use soft rubber and deep treads to increase real contact area and give water somewhere to escape; crampons bypass the film entirely by driving metal points into the ice. The real hazard is a thin layer of black ice, which looks merely wet while friction is close to zero.[1][3]

边界：准液体层解释的是光滑冰面，不解释所有“滑”。冰面有砂、有雪、有裂纹时，摩擦会大幅变化；低于约 -30 °C，表面层显著变薄，冰反而不那么滑。把“压力融化”当成唯一原因，是过时的教科书说法；把“冰永远滑”当常识，也会忽略温度、光照和表面状态。The boundary: the quasi-liquid layer explains smooth ice, not every slippery surface. Sand, snow and cracks change friction dramatically, and below about -30 °C the surface layer becomes much thinner, so ice is less slippery. 'Pressure melting' as the sole cause is an outdated textbook answer; 'ice is always slippery' is equally misleading, because temperature, sunlight and surface state decide how slippery it really is.[3][4]

03水的沸点为什么不是固定 100 °C？高原和高压锅差在哪里？Why is water's boiling point not fixed at 100 °C, and how do altitude and a pressure cooker change it?

沸点是蒸气压等于外界气压的温度：气压低沸点低，气压高沸点高。Boiling occurs when vapour pressure equals ambient pressure, so lower pressure lowers the boiling point and higher pressure raises it.

水的沸点不是固定的 100 °C，而是“蒸气压等于外界气压”的温度。海平面一个大气压下是 100 °C；海拔 1500 米处气压约 84 kPa，沸点约 95 °C；两个大气压的高压锅里，沸点可到约 120 °C。气压越低，水越容易沸腾；气压越高，就要更高温度才能让蒸气压追上外界压力。这三档数值不是背下来的，它们落在同一条蒸气压曲线上。Water does not always boil at 100 °C. It boils at the temperature where its vapour pressure equals the surrounding pressure. At sea level, with one atmosphere, that is 100 °C. At an altitude of about 1,500 metres, where the pressure is roughly 84 kPa, water boils near 95 °C; inside a pressure cooker at two atmospheres, it reaches about 120 °C. Lower pressure makes boiling easier, higher pressure makes it harder.[5][6]

沸腾发生在液体内部形成气泡并长大。气泡里是水蒸气，其压强必须至少等于周围液体的压强，否则会被压瘪。外界气压降低，气泡更容易达到这个门槛，所以沸点下降。相图上，固、液、气三线交于三相点（0.01 °C，611 Pa）；沿液-气共存线，压力与温度一一对应，这条线就是沸点随气压变化的曲线。Boiling happens when bubbles of vapour form inside the liquid and grow. The vapour inside a bubble must push back with at least the pressure of the surrounding liquid, or the bubble collapses. When the outside pressure falls, a bubble reaches that threshold at a lower temperature, so the boiling point falls. On a phase diagram the solid, liquid and vapour lines meet at the triple point (0.01 °C, 611 Pa). Along the liquid–vapour coexistence line, pressure and temperature are locked together, and that line is exactly the boiling-point-versus-pressure curve.[5]

OpenStax 的相图章节给出水的临界点：374 °C、22.1 MPa，超过它液气不再分界。日常证据也很直接：高原上煮饭夹生，因为水在八十多度就沸腾，淀粉糊化不充分；高压锅把压力提到 1.8–2 个大气压，沸点升到约 116–120 °C，胶原分解更快；真空冷冻干燥则利用低压下冰直接升华。冰也可以不经过液态直接变成水蒸气，即升华：低于三相点压力（611 Pa）或在日常低温干燥环境中，冰表面水分子获得足够动能后脱离晶格直接进入气相；冬天冰冻的衣服慢慢变干、冰雕逐渐变小，都是升华现象。OpenStax's chapter on phase diagrams puts the critical point of water at 374 °C and 22.1 MPa: beyond it there is no separate liquid and gas. Everyday evidence is just as direct. Rice cooks badly on a high plateau because water boils in the low eighties and starch never gelatinises properly. A pressure cooker raises the pressure to about 1.8–2 atmospheres, pushing the boiling point to roughly 116–120 °C so that collagen breaks down faster. Freeze-drying uses the opposite trick, letting ice sublime under low pressure. Ice can also turn directly into vapour without passing through liquid, a transition called sublimation: below the triple-point pressure of 611 Pa, or in ordinary cold dry air, surface molecules gain enough kinetic energy to leave the crystal lattice and enter the gas phase; frozen clothes drying slowly in winter and ice sculptures shrinking over time are everyday examples.[2][5][7]

所以“大火烧开”和“小火慢炖”不是一回事：一旦沸腾，继续加大火力不会让水温超过当地沸点，只会加快蒸发；想提高温度，必须提高压力或改用油浴。海拔每升高约 300 米，沸点大约下降 1 °C，这是近似值，受天气和湿度影响。压力锅的安全阀，本质上就是给压强设了一个上限，让沸点停在设计值附近。So a rolling boil and a gentle simmer are not the same thing. Once water is boiling, adding more heat does not raise its temperature above the local boiling point; it only speeds evaporation. To cook hotter you must raise the pressure or use an oil bath. Boiling point drops by about 1 °C for every 300 metres of altitude, an approximation that weather and humidity modify. The safety valve on a pressure cooker is simply a pressure ceiling that keeps the boiling point near its design value.[6]

边界：沸点还受溶质影响。加盐会升高沸点，约每千克水每摩尔溶质半度，但厨房里的加盐量带来的变化不到 1 °C，靠加盐“让水更热”意义有限；糖浆、浓汤的沸点会明显升高。测沸点要用校正过的气压计，不能凭“冒大泡”判断；过热会让水在沸点以上仍不沸腾，一搅就可能剧烈暴沸。The boundary: dissolved substances also shift the boiling point. Salt raises it by roughly half a degree per mole of solute per kilogram of water, but the amount used in cooking changes it by less than 1 °C, so salting the water to make it hotter achieves little; syrups and thick soups do rise noticeably. Measure boiling with a calibrated barometer rather than by watching for big bubbles, and remember that superheating can keep water liquid above its boiling point, sometimes with a violent burst when disturbed.[6][7]

04冰棍和开水为什么都会冒白气？Why do both an ice pop and boiling water give off a white mist?

白气不是水蒸气，而是无色水蒸气遇冷液化成的微小水滴悬浮在空气中；开水上方和冰棍周围都是同样的液化过程。The white mist is not water vapor but tiny droplets formed when invisible vapor condenses on cooling; this happens above boiling water and around an ice pop by the same process.

先要分清水蒸气和白气。水蒸气是气态的水，无色透明，肉眼根本看不见；我们看到的“白气”已经不是气体，而是水蒸气遇冷后液化成的微小水滴，悬浮在空气里形成雾状。判断冒不冒白气，关键看空气中的水汽有没有被降温到凝结成液滴的程度。First distinguish water vapor from white mist. Water vapor is gaseous water, colorless and transparent, and invisible to the eye; the white mist we see is no longer gas. It is tiny droplets that form when vapor cools and condenses, suspended in air as a fog. Whether mist appears depends on whether the water vapor in the air is cooled enough to form liquid droplets.[2]

开水之所以冒白气，是因为水面持续汽化产生高温水蒸气。这些刚离开水面的水汽本来透明，一旦进入温度较低的周围空气，温度降到露点以下，就凝结成直径约 1–10 μm 的小水滴，大量水滴聚在一起，散射光线，看上去就是一团白雾。白气最浓的位置，正是水汽与冷空气交界处。判断白气位置有个经验：热水上方白气离开水面一段距离才出现，因为紧贴水面的水汽还没被冷却；冰棍周围白气贴得更近，是冷空气直接裹在冷源外面。Boiling water gives off mist because the surface keeps vaporizing hot water vapor. This vapor, transparent right above the water, enters the cooler surrounding air, drops below the dew point, and condenses into droplets about 1 to 10 μm across. Many droplets together scatter light and look like a white cloud; the mist is densest exactly at the boundary between vapor and cold air.[1][2]

冰棍周围冒气是同一过程，方向相反。冰棍温度很低，把紧贴它周围的空气冷却，空气里原本含有的水汽遇到这层冷空气，同样液化成小水滴，于是在冰棍周围看到向下飘的白雾。冬天对着冷玻璃哈气、浴室镜子蒙上一层水雾，也都是水汽遇冷液化；夏天冷饮杯外壁挂水珠，是杯子附近水汽在冷壁上凝结。An ice pop works the same process in reverse. The pop is very cold and cools the air right around it; the water vapor already in that air meets the cold layer and condenses into droplets, forming a white mist that drifts downward around the pop. Breathing onto cold glass in winter or steaming up a bathroom mirror are also vapor condensing on cooling; beads on a cold drink cup form when nearby vapor condenses on the chilled wall.[1][2]

液化过程要放出热量。气态水变成液态水时放出汽化潜热，100 °C 水的汽化潜热约 2260 kJ/kg；反过来，液滴被气流带走或重新受热蒸发，白气就消散。空气越干燥，小水滴越容易被未饱和空气吸收，白气散开得越快；潮湿天气里白气能飘得更远、更久。汽化和液化互为逆过程：1 kg 水在 100 °C 汽化要吸收约 2260 kJ 热量，凝结成水时又把这份潜热放给周围空气。Condensation releases heat. When gaseous water turns liquid it releases the latent heat of vaporization, about 2260 kJ/kg for water at 100 °C; droplets carried away by airflow or reheated and evaporated make the mist disappear. Drier air absorbs small droplets more easily, so the mist spreads and clears faster; in humid weather it drifts farther and lasts longer.[2][5]

边界要写清：不要把白气当成水蒸气，真正的水蒸气藏在白气与水面或冷源之间那段透明区域。液化是气变液，与凝华，也就是气直接变成冰晶，属于不同阶段；本章讲的是白气、雾气和镜面膜这类液态小水滴的形成，不讨论霜、雪等固态降水的凝华过程。The boundary matters: do not mistake the mist for water vapor; the true vapor sits in the transparent region between the mist and the water surface or cold source. Condensation is gas to liquid, different from deposition, gas directly to ice crystals; this chapter covers liquid droplets such as mist, fog and film on a mirror, not the deposition that forms frost or snow.[1][5]

05窗户上为什么会结霜？Why does frost form on windows?

冬天室内水汽接触温度低于 0 °C 的玻璃，不经液态直接凝华成冰晶，沿六方晶格分叉生长成霜花。Indoor vapor meets glass below 0 °C and deposits directly as ice crystals without a liquid stage, growing along the hexagonal lattice into feathery frost.

冬天清晨，朝北或保温差的窗户内侧常结一层白色霜花。它的形成过程是凝华：室内空气里的水蒸气，碰到温度已经降到 0 °C 以下的玻璃表面，不经液态阶段，直接由气态变成固态冰晶附着在玻璃上。这与先结成水珠、再冻成冰的路径不同。霜不是先结露再冻成冰，而是水汽在低于冰点的表面上直接完成气固转换；这一点和冬天草地上的白霜、树枝上的雾凇属于同一类凝华。On winter mornings, frost often coats the inside of north-facing or poorly insulated windows. It forms by deposition: indoor water vapor reaches a glass surface already below 0 °C and turns directly from gas to solid ice crystals, skipping the liquid stage, rather than first forming droplets that then freeze. Frost is not dew that freezes afterward; vapor completes the gas-to-solid transition on a surface below freezing, the same deposition class as ground frost and rime on branches.[2][5]

要先区分结露和结霜。如果玻璃表面温度还在 0 °C 以上，水汽降温后先液化成一层液态水膜，就是冬天窗上常见的雾气或水珠；只有玻璃冷到冰点以下，水汽才能在表面直接凝华成冰晶。室内湿度越高、玻璃越冷，可供凝华的水汽越多，霜就越厚。Distinguish dew from frost first. If the glass stays above 0 °C, vapor condenses into a liquid film first, the mist or beads seen on winter windows; only when the glass cools below freezing can vapor deposit directly as ice crystals. Higher indoor humidity and colder glass provide more vapor for deposition, making thicker frost.[2][5]

霜花的花纹来自冰晶生长方向。冰属于六方晶系，冰晶沿着特定晶向优先生长；玻璃表面又有细微的温差、划痕和粗糙度，水汽在这些位置优先凝结，晶体沿枝晶方向分叉延伸，长成蕨叶状、羽毛状或树枝状的霜花。不同部位晶向和过冷度不同，花纹也就疏密不一。六方冰晶相邻棱面夹角约 60°，枝晶沿这些晶向分叉，所以霜花常带有对称的夹角和羽状分枝；玻璃上的灰尘、划痕和冷凝先后，会让每处花纹长得不完全一样。The frost pattern comes from crystal growth directions. Ice has a hexagonal crystal system, and crystals grow preferentially along certain lattice directions. The glass surface also has subtle temperature differences, scratches and roughness where vapor condenses first, and branches extend along the dendrite direction into fern-like, feathery or tree-like patterns. Lattice direction and supercooling vary by spot, so patterns differ in density.[2][7]

结霜需要几个条件凑在一起：室内湿度较高、窗户保温差导致内表面温度低、外界气温很低。双层中空玻璃在两层之间留有干燥空气层或惰性气体，内表面不容易降到冰点以下，霜就很轻甚至不结；单层薄玻璃、窗框缝隙漏风的窗最容易结霜。通风好、室内干燥的房间水汽少，玻璃上多是薄霜或不结霜；密闭潮湿、又靠单层玻璃的房间，霜层最厚。Frost needs several conditions together: fairly high indoor humidity, poor insulation that keeps the inner surface cold, and very low outside temperature. Double-glazed units hold a dry air or inert-gas layer between panes, so the inner surface rarely drops below freezing and frost is light or absent; single thin glass and windows with drafty frames frost most easily.[2][5]

边界要写清：霜是气直接变固的凝华过程，与露的气变液、雾凇在物体表面冻附等过程阶段不同。本章只解释窗霜怎样形成、花纹从何而来，不讨论除霜方法、供暖设计或保温改造；窗户结霜也不代表室内空气本身异常，主要是玻璃表面温度条件造成的。The boundary matters: frost is gas directly to solid by deposition, differing from dew, gas to liquid, and from rime that freezes onto objects. This chapter explains how window frost forms and where the pattern comes from, not defrosting methods, heating design or insulation retrofits; frost on a window does not mean the indoor air itself is abnormal, it mainly results from the glass surface temperature.[7]
