金属与材料Metals and Materials

从块炼铁到黄金，再到锂矿：金属和材料的故事不只是“熔点”和“颜色”。这篇 Hub 用高温还原、化学稳定性、卫星隔热毯和伟晶岩—盐湖富集，回答六个常见问题，并区分化学、核反应与工程材料的边界。From bloomery iron to gold and lithium ores, the story of metals and materials is not just about melting points and colour. This Hub uses high-temperature reduction, chemical stability, spacecraft insulation and pegmatite–brine enrichment to answer six common questions and to separate chemistry, nuclear reactions and engineered materials.

01古人怎样在高温下炼铁？没有氧气，木头会熔化吗？How did ancient people smelt iron at high temperature? Would wood melt without oxygen?

木炭和鼓风把炉温升到 1100–1300°C，铁矿石在固态下被一氧化碳还原；木头缺氧加热先热解，不会熔成液体。Charcoal and forced air raise a bloomery to about 1100–1300 °C, where carbon monoxide reduces iron ore in the solid state; wood has no fixed melting point and, without oxygen, pyrolyses

古人炼铁并不是先把铁熔成铁水。最常见的块炼铁炉是一个竖立的黏土或石砌炉身，里面交替装木炭和铁矿石，侧面用风箱鼓风。木炭燃烧把炉温推到大约 1100–1300°C，铁矿石在这一温度下被一氧化碳还原成海绵状的金属铁，最后趁热锻打成块铁。纯铁的熔点约 1538°C，所以块炼铁的关键是“还原”，不是“熔化”。Ancient ironmakers did not begin by melting iron into a liquid. The most common bloomery was a vertical clay or stone furnace charged alternately with charcoal and iron ore, with bellows blowing air in from the side. Burning charcoal raised the temperature to roughly 1100–1300 °C, where carbon monoxide reduced the ore into a spongy mass of metallic iron that was then hot-forged into a bloom. Pure iron melts at about 1538 °C, so the key to bloomery smelting was reduction, not melting.[1][2]

为什么木炭能提供这样的条件？木炭燃烧产生二氧化碳，但在缺氧或高温的炉内，二氧化碳又会与灼热的碳反应生成一氧化碳；一氧化碳上升时把氧化铁中的氧带走，生成铁和二氧化碳。鼓风既补充氧气，也把热量送进炉心；炉身越高、保温越好，热效率越高。矿石中的脉石与助熔剂形成炉渣，从下部排出，留下较纯的铁块。Why could charcoal create these conditions? Charcoal burns to carbon dioxide, but in an oxygen-poor or hot furnace the carbon dioxide reacts with glowing carbon to form carbon monoxide; as the gas rises, it strips oxygen from iron oxide and leaves iron and carbon dioxide. Forced air supplies oxygen and pushes heat into the furnace; a taller, better-insulated shaft uses heat more efficiently. The gangue in the ore combines with a flux to form slag, which drains from the bottom and leaves a relatively pure iron bloom.[1][2][3]

把木头放进没有氧气的极高温环境，情况完全不同。木头不是单一物质，而是纤维素、半纤维素和木质素的混合物，没有固定的“熔点”。加热到约 200–400°C，这些大分子先断裂，挥发物逸出，留下木炭骨架；继续升温，焦油会裂解、缩聚，最后剩下碳和少量灰分。整个过程叫热解，不是熔化。Place wood in a very hot environment without oxygen and the situation is completely different. Wood is not a single substance but a mixture of cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin, and it has no fixed melting point. At about 200–400 °C the large molecules begin to break apart, volatiles escape and a charcoal skeleton remains; with further heating, tars crack and repolymerise, leaving carbon and a little ash. The process is pyrolysis, not melting.[6]

考古和实验都能看到这条路径。古代块炼铁遗址里常见炉壁、鼓风口和含铁炉渣；现代复原炉用木炭和风箱，也能在较低温度下得到可锻的铁块。相比之下，高炉把铁水加热到 1400°C 以上，让碳进入铁中形成铸铁，那是另一条工艺路线。判断“炼铁是否需要极高温度”，要先问目标是固态还原还是液态熔炼。Archaeology and experiments both show this pathway. Ancient bloomery sites often contain furnace walls, tuyères and iron-rich slag; modern reconstruction furnaces using charcoal and bellows can also produce malleable iron at relatively modest temperatures. By contrast, a blast furnace heats liquid iron above 1400 °C and lets carbon enter the metal to make cast iron — a different process route. To ask whether iron smelting “requires extremely high temperature”, first ask whether the goal is solid-state reduction or liquid melting.[2][3]

边界：块炼铁的炉温受矿石、木炭、鼓风量和炉型影响，不是每个遗址都达到同一数字；纯铁熔点 1538°C 也不等于所有铁合金的熔点。木头在无氧条件下不会熔成“液态木头”，但它的热解产物可以冷凝成焦油、燃烧成气体；若混入氧气，木头会直接燃烧。把“热解”和“熔化”分开，才能理解铁与木头的两种高温故事。The boundary: bloomery temperatures depend on ore, charcoal, airflow and furnace design, so not every site reached the same number; the 1538 °C melting point of pure iron is also not the melting point of every iron alloy. Wood does not melt into “liquid wood” without oxygen, but its pyrolysis products can condense into tar or burn as gas; with oxygen present, wood simply burns. Keeping pyrolysis and melting separate is what makes the two high-temperature stories intelligible.[1][6]

02黄金能人工制造吗？为什么耐腐蚀？卫星金箔又是什么？Can gold be made artificially? Why is it corrosion-resistant? And what is the gold foil on satellites?

化学不能改变原子序数，造不出金；核反应可造微量金但成本极高。金还原电位正，耐腐蚀；卫星“金箔”多是隔热毯，不是实心黄金。Ordinary chemistry cannot change atomic number and cannot make gold; nuclear reactions can produce trace amounts but at enormous cost. Gold has a strongly positive reduction potential

先分清两条路：化学和核反应。普通化学反应只重新排列原子、交换电子，不能把一种元素的原子核变成另一种；所以用铅、汞或其他元素“炼”出黄金，在化学上做不到。核反应可以改变原子序数，理论上能生成金；加速器或反应堆确实产生过极微量的金，但产额、能耗和成本都让它不可能替代采矿。商业黄金来自矿石、伴生金属和回收。这也解释了为什么“点石成金”更多是寓言，而不是可规模化的工艺。Start by separating two routes: chemistry and nuclear reactions. Ordinary chemical reactions only rearrange atoms and exchange electrons; they cannot turn the nucleus of one element into another. Turning lead, mercury or another element into gold is therefore impossible by chemistry alone. Nuclear reactions can change atomic number and, in principle, produce gold; accelerators and reactors have indeed made extremely small amounts, but the yield, energy cost and expense make this no substitute for mining. Commercial gold comes from ores, by-product metals and recycling.[1][3]

金的耐腐蚀来自热力学。Au³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Au 的标准还原电位约 +1.50 V，比氢电极和多数常见金属正得多；这意味着金不容易失去电子被氧化。它在空气、水和许多酸中保持金属光泽，正是这个“不愿意给电子”的结果。但耐腐蚀不等于不被任何东西侵蚀：王水、含氧的氰化物溶液和某些卤素仍能溶解金。这组数值是标准状态下的比较，实际溶液的 pH、配体和浓度会改变反应趋势。Gold’s corrosion resistance comes from thermodynamics. The standard reduction potential for Au³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Au is about +1.50 V, far more positive than the hydrogen electrode and most common metals; this means gold gives up electrons and oxidises only with difficulty. It keeps its metallic lustre in air, water and many acids because of this reluctance to lose electrons. But corrosion resistance does not mean immunity: aqua regia, oxygenated cyanide solutions and some halogens can still dissolve gold.[3]

金的柔软和合金也常被混淆。纯金很软，牙科、首饰和电子触点常用金合金或镀层来提高强度、耐磨性；耐腐蚀的是金本身，承受结构载荷的往往不是纯金。把“金不变色”理解为“金不会磨损”也不对，薄镀层会被刮掉。电子工业的镀金触点也利用薄层金的稳定导电性，而不是用整块纯金。Gold’s softness and alloying are often confused with corrosion. Pure gold is soft; dentistry, jewellery and electrical contacts often use gold alloys or coatings for strength and wear resistance. The corrosion-resistant part is the gold itself; the part carrying a structural load is often not pure gold. Nor does “gold does not tarnish” mean “gold does not wear”: a thin plating can be scratched away.[3][4]

卫星上的金色和银色“箔”通常不是实心金箔，而是多层隔热毯（MLI）。常见结构是镀铝的聚酰亚胺（Kapton）或聚酯薄膜，多层叠放、用网眼织物隔开；金色常来自聚酰亚胺的琥珀色，银色则来自铝反射层。它的作用是反射太阳光和红外辐射，减少航天器与深空之间的辐射换热，同时兼顾轻量和柔性。层间抽真空或填充低导热气体同样重要，单靠薄膜颜色并不能隔热。The gold- and silver-coloured “foil” on satellites is usually not solid gold foil but multilayer insulation (MLI). A common construction is aluminised polyimide (Kapton) or polyester film, stacked in many layers and separated by scrim or netting; the golden colour often comes from the amber polyimide, while the silver comes from the aluminium reflector. Its job is to reflect sunlight and infrared radiation, reducing radiative heat exchange between the spacecraft and deep space while staying light and flexible.[7]

边界：MLI 不是结构材料，不能承受气动或机械载荷；它需要接地和防护，以免静电积累；真实卫星也会用金膜、银膜或第二表面镜处理特定红外波段，但不等于整颗卫星包着黄金。人工核反应能“造金”是物理事实，却不是经济可行的黄金生产方法。化学、核反应和工程材料三条线要分开看。材料选择还要在耐腐蚀、成本、重量和加工性之间平衡。The boundary: MLI is not a structural material and cannot carry aerodynamic or mechanical loads; it needs grounding and protection against static charging. Real spacecraft may also use gold films, silver films or second-surface mirrors for specific infrared bands, but that does not mean the whole satellite is wrapped in gold. Nuclear transmutation can indeed “make gold” as a physics fact, but it is not an economically viable gold-production method. Keep chemistry, nuclear reactions and engineered materials on separate lines.[3][7]

03锂为什么集中在少数地方？Why is lithium concentrated in some places?

锂在地壳中并不稀少，但可开采富集需要特殊过程：伟晶岩分异把锂赶进残余熔体，干旱封闭盆地的卤水蒸发把锂浓缩到经济品位。Lithium is not rare in the crust, but economic enrichment requires special processes: pegmatite magma differentiation concentrates lithium in residual melts, while evaporation in arid

锂在地壳中的平均含量约 17–20 ppm，按元素丰度并不算“稀有”，但经济可采的锂矿床只分布在少数地区。原因不是锂偏爱某一块大陆，而是它在地质过程中容易被“搬运”和“浓缩”：岩浆结晶时它不愿进入常见矿物，留下来；水流风化时它又容易进入卤水。换句话说，锂的“稀有”是经济地理问题，不是元素本身突然变少。Lithium averages about 17–20 ppm in the crust, so it is not especially rare by elemental abundance, yet economically recoverable lithium deposits occur in only a few regions. The reason is not that lithium prefers one continent but that it is easily transported and concentrated by geological processes: during magma crystallisation it tends to stay out of common minerals, and during weathering it readily enters brines.[3][8]

第一条路径是伟晶岩。花岗质岩浆缓慢冷却时，锂、硼、铍等不相容元素留在残余熔体中；分异越充分，残余熔体越富集这些元素，最后在岩脉中结晶出锂辉石、透锂长石或锂云母。澳大利亚 Greenbushes 的伟晶岩型矿床就是典型例子。这类矿床往往品位高、矿物明确，但脉体规模和分带变化很大。The first route is pegmatite. As granitic magma cools slowly, incompatible elements such as lithium, boron and beryllium remain in the residual melt; the more complete the differentiation, the more these elements are enriched, eventually crystallising as spodumene, petalite or lepidolite in dykes. The pegmatite deposit at Greenbushes, Australia, is a classic example.[3][8]

第二条路径是盐湖卤水。火山岩和火山灰中的锂被雨水、地下水淋滤出来，流入没有排水口的封闭盆地；在干旱气候下，水不断蒸发，锂和其他盐类留在卤水中。智利 Salar de Atacama 的地下卤水被泵到地表，在蒸发池里浓缩，再运到工厂提取。蒸发池的停留时间、卤水化学和补给速率都会影响回收率。The second route is salt-lake brine. Lithium in volcanic rocks and ash is leached by rain and groundwater and flows into closed basins with no outlet; in an arid climate, water keeps evaporating and lithium and other salts remain in the brine. At Chile’s Salar de Atacama, subsurface brine is pumped to the surface, concentrated in evaporation ponds and then shipped to a processing plant.[5][8]

这两种路径都要求“源—搬运—圈闭”配合。伟晶岩需要长期稳定的岩浆分异和合适的围岩；盐湖需要干旱、封闭、持续补给，缺一不可。USGS 2025 年数据显示，全球锂矿产量约 24 万吨（2024 年），智利储量约 930 万吨、澳大利亚约 700 万吨；资源集中在少数国家，但勘探仍在扩展。这些数字也会随新项目投产、价格和勘探结果而变。Both routes require a source, transport and trap. Pegmatites need long-lived, stable magma differentiation and suitable host rocks; salt lakes need aridity, closure and continued recharge — all three. USGS data for 2025 put global lithium mine production at about 240,000 tonnes in 2024, with reserves of about 9.3 million tonnes in Chile and 7.0 million tonnes in Australia; resources are concentrated in a few countries, but exploration continues to expand.[5][8]

边界：锂并非只存在于伟晶岩和盐湖，黏土、地热卤水、油田卤水也可成为资源；不同矿床的提取工艺、能耗和水资源影响差别很大。地壳平均含量低并不等于“稀缺”，储量数字也会随价格、技术和勘探变化。把锂的分布看成地质过程的产物，而不是地图上的偶然点，才能理解供应与开采的边界。对锂供应来说，地质禀赋、提取技术和环境约束必须一起看。The boundary: lithium is not confined to pegmatites and salt lakes; clays, geothermal brines and oilfield brines can also become resources, and the extraction process, energy use and water impact differ greatly between deposit types. A low average crustal abundance does not mean “scarce”, and reserve figures change with prices, technology and exploration. Seeing lithium distribution as the product of geological processes, rather than as accidental dots on a map, is what makes supply and mining limits intelligible.[5][8]

04铁为什么会生锈？锈蚀为什么是一种电化学腐蚀？Why does iron rust, and why is rusting an electrochemical process?

水膜作电解质形成微型原电池：阳极铁溶解，阴极氧气得电子；疏松铁锈不能自保护，潮湿含盐环境加速锈蚀。A water film acts as electrolyte in tiny cells: iron dissolves at the anode, oxygen gains electrons at the cathode; porous rust cannot self-heal.

日常见到的铁器，放在潮湿环境里过一段时间就会出现红棕色锈斑。铁锈质地疏松、容易剥落，和铝表面那种致密贴合的氧化膜不一样，它不能把金属基体完整保护起来。生锈看起来是铁和空气、水接触后的自然结果，实际过程是一组在铁表面同时进行的电化学氧化还原反应，需要把阳极、阴极和导电回路分开看。Iron objects left in damp surroundings develop reddish-brown rust patches after a while. Rust is porous and flakes off easily, unlike the dense, tightly bonded oxide film on aluminum; it cannot fully protect the metal underneath. Rusting looks like a natural consequence of contact with air and water, but it is in fact a set of electrochemical redox reactions running simultaneously on the iron surface, which are best understood by separating the anode, the cathode and the conducting circuit.[1][9]

铁表面吸附一层薄薄的水膜，空气中的二氧化碳、海盐或灰尘溶解进去，水膜就成了能导电的电解质。铁表面不同区域电位有差别，有的区域成为阳极，铁原子失去电子变成亚铁离子进入水膜：Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻；另一些区域成为阴极，溶解在水里的氧气得到电子，和水一起生成氢氧根：O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻。电子通过铁基体本身从阳极流向阴极，水膜在离子层面闭合回路，整块铁器就像无数个微型原电池在同时工作。A thin water film adsorbs onto the iron surface. Carbon dioxide, sea salt or dust dissolves in it, turning the film into a conducting electrolyte. Different spots on the iron surface differ in potential: some act as anodes, where iron atoms lose electrons and enter the film as ferrous ions, Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻; others act as cathodes, where dissolved oxygen gains electrons and reacts with water to form hydroxide, O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻. Electrons travel through the iron itself from anode to cathode, while the film closes the circuit at the ion level, so the whole piece behaves like countless tiny galvanic cells working at once.[1][3][9]

亚铁离子和氢氧根结合成氢氧化亚铁，继续被氧气氧化成氢氧化铁，再脱水形成红棕色的水合氧化铁，也就是肉眼看到的铁锈。这个过程同时需要水和氧气：干燥环境里水膜难以形成，锈蚀很慢；海边空气含盐、道路撒除冰盐时，氯离子提高水膜导电能力并破坏表面层，锈蚀明显加快。划伤、焊缝和缝隙处容易积水，往往先出现锈点。Ferrous ions combine with hydroxide to form iron(II) hydroxide, which is further oxidized by oxygen to iron(III) hydroxide and dehydrates into reddish-brown hydrated iron oxide, the rust visible to the eye. The process needs both water and oxygen: in dry air the film cannot form and rusting is slow. Near the sea, or where de-icing salt is spread, chloride ions raise the film's conductivity and break down the surface layer, so corrosion speeds up. Scratches, welds and crevices trap moisture and usually show rust spots first.[3][9]

铁锈疏松多孔，反而会吸附水分和氧气，让膜下的基体继续反应，锈层一层层往里钻。和铬、铝形成的致密钝化膜不同，铁锈没有自修复能力，剥落之后露出的新表面又接着生锈，所以放任不管，锈蚀会从表面逐步扩展到壁厚。日常看到的掉漆生锈，往往也是漆膜破损处先形成水膜，再沿金属与漆膜之间的缝隙向内蔓延。Rust is porous, so it keeps adsorbing water and oxygen and lets the reaction continue beneath the layer. Unlike the dense passive films formed by chromium or aluminum, rust cannot repair itself; after it flakes off, the newly exposed surface rusts again, and left alone the corrosion advances from the surface into the wall thickness. Paint damage often follows the same path: water forms a film at the broken spot and creeps inward between metal and coating.[1][3]

边界要写清：铁的锈蚀是电化学腐蚀，不是简单的铁在空气中被烧红那样的直接氧化；本章不展开镀锌、涂漆、阴极保护等防锈工艺。锈蚀速率受温度、湿度、含盐量和表面状态共同影响，判断构件是否还安全不能只看锈斑颜色。把生锈理解成铁变轻、被空气吃掉也不准确，锈层是铁与氧、水结合后的增重产物。Boundary: iron rusting is electrochemical corrosion, not direct oxidation like iron being heated red-hot in air; this chapter does not cover galvanizing, painting or cathodic protection. Corrosion rate depends on temperature, humidity, salt content and surface condition, so judging structural safety from rust color alone is unreliable. The idea that iron simply gets eaten away and lighter is also off: rust is a heavier product formed by combining iron with oxygen and water.[9]

05不锈钢为什么不容易生锈？氯离子为什么还会让它点蚀？Why does stainless steel resist rust, and why can chloride ions still cause pitting?

铬 ≥10.5% 形成 1–3 nm Cr₂O₃ 钝化膜并可自修复；氯离子穿透膜缺陷造成局部点蚀。At ≥10.5% chromium a 1–3 nm Cr₂O₃ film forms and self-heals; chloride penetrates film defects and turns a local spot into a small anode that pits.

不锈钢餐具、水槽长期接触水和汤汁，表面仍能保持光亮，给人不会生锈的印象。实际上不锈钢并不是不含铁，它是以铁为基体加入铬、镍等合金元素的合金钢；所谓不锈，靠的是合金成分配合一层极薄的表面钝化膜，而不是金属本身不被氧化。日常使用中，刀具、餐具接触盐分和有机酸，表面仍可能出现细小锈点，通常是膜层局部受损后没有及时修复造成的。Stainless-steel cutlery and sinks stay bright after long contact with water and food, giving the impression that they never rust. Stainless steel does not mean iron-free: it is an iron-based alloyed steel with chromium, nickel and other additions. Its stain resistance comes from the alloy composition together with a very thin passive film on the surface, not from the metal refusing to oxidize at all.[3]

起关键作用的是铬。不锈钢的铬含量通常不低于 10.5%，铬比铁更容易和氧结合，在有氧环境下表面生成一层约 1–3 纳米厚的致密三氧化二铬（Cr₂O₃）钝化膜，把金属基体与水膜、氧气隔开。这层膜很薄却连续，膜下的铁难以直接参与反应；即使表面被轻微划伤，暴露出来的铬在有氧条件下重新氧化，钝化膜可以自行修复，这种能力称为自钝化。Chromium is the key element. Stainless steel usually contains at least 10.5% Cr; chromium binds oxygen more readily than iron, and in oxygen-bearing air it forms a dense Cr₂O₃ passive film about 1–3 nanometers thick that separates the metal from the water film and oxygen. The film is extremely thin but continuous, so the iron beneath cannot react directly. If the surface is lightly scratched, the exposed chromium re-oxidizes in air and the film repairs itself; this ability is called self-passivation.[3][9]

钝化膜的稳定需要一定条件。在干燥、通风、含氧充分的环境里，膜层稳定；遇到强酸、长期缺氧，或者表面附着污垢、盐分把膜下局部环境改变时，膜可能失效。表面划痕、铁屑残留和长期水渍也会改变局部微环境，让膜下腐蚀从这些薄弱点开始。镍、钼等元素会改善耐蚀性，含钼牌号在含氯环境中表现更好，但它们不能替代钝化膜本身的作用。The film stays stable under certain conditions. In dry, ventilated, oxygen-rich air it remains intact; strong acid, prolonged oxygen starvation, or dirt and salts trapped on the surface can change the local chemistry beneath the film and break it down. Nickel and molybdenum improve corrosion resistance, and molybdenum-bearing grades do better in chloride environments, but they cannot replace the passive film itself.[1][9]

氯离子是钝化膜的主要威胁之一。海水、除冰盐、汗液里的氯离子体积小、穿透力强，容易在膜的缺陷或夹杂处吸附并破坏钝化膜。局部失去膜保护的小点成为小阳极，周围大面积有膜区域成为大阴极，蚀坑内离子浓缩、酸性上升，腐蚀越钻越深；外观上只是细小凹坑，却可能向壁厚方向发展，这就是常说的点蚀。Chloride ions are one of the main threats to the film. Chloride in seawater, de-icing salt or sweat is small and penetrating; it adsorbs at film defects or inclusions and destroys the passive layer there. The small unprotected spot becomes a small anode, while the large filmed area around it acts as the big cathode. Inside the pit ions concentrate and acidity rises, and the corrosion drills deeper; what looks like a tiny dimple may advance into the wall thickness, a form known as pitting corrosion.[3][9]

边界要写清：不锈钢不是永不生锈，不同牌号的耐蚀范围差别很大，长期盐雾、潮湿或接触化学品的环境仍需按用途选材并定期清洁维护。本章不展开焊接热影响区、应力腐蚀开裂等专项问题；判断一件不锈钢制品是否耐蚀，要结合成分、表面状态和接触介质，不能只看外观光亮。Boundary: stainless steel is not rust-proof forever, and grades differ widely in their corrosion range; long-term salt spray, dampness or chemical contact still requires matching the alloy to the service and regular cleaning. This chapter does not cover weld heat-affected zones or stress corrosion cracking in detail. Whether a stainless item resists corrosion depends on composition, surface condition and the medium it touches, not on a bright-looking finish alone.[1][3]
